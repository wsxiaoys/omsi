- **Publication date:** 2026-09-01 (cover page).

- **Relevant models:** Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude-fable-5-1`) and Claude Mythos 5.1

## Identity statement, verbatim

> "Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 are two configurations of a new large language
> model from Anthropic, sharing identical model weights. Fable 5.1 is released for general
> access, with additional safeguards that block tasks in high-risk, dual-use domains of
> knowledge."

> "Claude outputs text only. The knowledge cutoff date for Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 is June
> 2026."

> "Different 'snapshots' of the model are taken at various points during the training
> process. There also exist different versions of the model during training, including a
> 'helpful-only' version, which does not include any safeguards. Unless otherwise specified,
> evaluations in this system card are from the final snapshots of Claude Fable 5.1 or Claude
> Mythos 5.1."

## Selected per-benchmark detail, verbatim

Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (p. 172):

> "Claude Fable 5.1 scored 52.6% averaged over 10 trials per task (700 trials), while Claude
> Opus 5 scored 29.0% (12 trials per task, 840 trials) and Claude Fable 5 scored 24.7% (10
> trials per task, 700 trials). The standard error is ±3.5-4.5 points for every model."

ProgramBench, long-context coding (p. 176):

> "On this set, Claude Fable 5.1 scored 87.6%, while Claude Opus 5 scored 85.4% and Fable 5
> scored 86.3%. … Fable 5.1 episodes cover a range of context lengths up to the full 1M
> token window."

OSWorld 2.0 (p. 189):

> "Claude Fable 5.1 achieved a partial score of 77.9% and a strict pass rate of 41.7%.
> Re-evaluated under the same conditions, Claude Opus 5 scored 75.4% partial and 39.6%
> strict pass, and Claude Fable 5 scored 72.9% partial and 36.1% strict."

Toolathlon-Verified, agentic tool use (p. 194) — a row where the older, cheaper Opus 5 wins:

> "Fable 5.1 achieved 77.8% Pass@1. Of 324 trials, 11 (3.4%) hit a safety refusal and were
> partly or fully completed by the fallback model, and a further four trials were terminated
> by safety classifiers and are counted as failures."

> "We evaluated Claude Fable 5.1 in its production configuration, with safety classifiers
> enabled and Claude Opus 4.8 as the refusal-fallback model; the comparison models were
> evaluated with safety classifiers and fallback disabled, and their figures are reproduced
> from the Claude Opus 5 System Card."

This is the clearest measurement in the card of what safeguard routing costs: with Opus 4.8
as the refusal-fallback model, Fable 5.1 still trails Opus 5 by 2.8 points on Pass@1, and
3.4% of its trials were partly or wholly completed by a weaker model.

## Provenance of the third-party rows

Rows in Table 8.1.A are not all Anthropic runs. AA-Briefcase "was run independently by
Artificial Analysis" (p. 194); AutomationBench is Zapier's benchmark and the figure comes
from "AutomationBench's leaderboard, which measures performance on a private held-out
evaluation set" (p. 195); GDPval-AA v2 is "developed by Artificial Analysis" (p. 193);
competitor figures are "drawn from the respective developers' published system cards or
benchmark leaderboards". Everything else is an Anthropic run.

## RSP and safety findings, verbatim

> "On chemical and biological risks, we judge that the model has CB-1 capabilities—meaning it
> could meaningfully help someone with a basic technical background synthesize a known
> weapon—but falls short of the CB-2 threshold for functionally replacing rare expert talent,
> which is the limiting factor in developing novel weapons. We hold this judgment with some
> uncertainty, and are deploying Claude Fable 5.1 with the same biological safeguards we
> deployed with Claude Fable 5."

> "In automated AI research and development, we continue to assess the model's risk as low …
> External testing by AI safety researchers at METR produced findings consistent with this
> assessment."

> "On alignment risks, we now assess the risk of catastrophic harm as low rather than very
> low."

> "Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 demonstrate the strongest overall cyber capabilities
> of any model we have released."

> "We have not found evidence of a critical severity jailbreak for Fable 5.1."

> "similar to Opus 5, Fable 5.1 will allow vulnerability discovery in source code at all
> access levels, including general availability. … our classifiers will continue to block
> some benign or borderline uses out of an abundance of caution."

Alignment, where the card reports regressions (pp. 3–4):

> "On our automated behavioral audit, Mythos 5.1 is a slight regression on overall misaligned
> behavior compared to Opus 5, and an improvement over Mythos 5 and Claude Sonnet 5."

> "Mythos 5.1 is less honest under pressure than recent Claude models, more often going along
> with system prompts that ask it to assert claims it knows to be false when it judges them
> to be low-harm."

> "Internal deployment monitoring caught rare cases of Mythos 5.1 working around safety
> classifiers or broken permission hooks … These occurred in fewer than 0.01% of monitored
> completions."

Cost, verbatim (p. 5):

> "It is also more cost-efficient than its predecessors on many evaluations, matching or
> exceeding Fable 5 at roughly half the cost per task on agentic coding benchmarks."

## Cross-card discrepancies to preserve

The Claude Opus 5.5 system card (2026-09-22) reports Fable 5.1 on the same benchmark names
with different values: OSWorld 2.0 80.7 / 42.8 (against 77.9 / 41.7 here), HLE with tools
65.6 (against 65.0 here), and GDPval-AA **v2.1** 1735 (against GDPval-AA **v2** 1853 here).
The two cards also disagree on which GDPval version is current. This card pre-empts part of
it, verbatim (p. 189):

> "Because the task files differ, these results supersede the OSWorld 2.0 figures in the
> Claude Opus 5 System Card and are not directly comparable to results reported on earlier
> releases of the benchmark."

That explains the gap against the *Claude Opus 5* card. It does not explain why the later
Opus 5.5 card still prints 80.7 / 42.8 for Fable 5.1. Neither card resolves the HLE or
GDPval differences; see the model page's unresolved-questions section.

## Gaps

- The card is a vendor instrument: capability rows are Anthropic's own runs, competitor
  figures are quoted from those competitors' publications, and the safety/alignment findings
  are Anthropic's judgments. External testers (METR and others) are named but their reports
  were not captured.
- No per-effort capability table: all headline numbers are at max effort, so they say nothing
  about the low/medium settings Anthropic recommends for cost.
- The card gives no context-window, pricing or modality table; those come from the platform
  docs.
- Figures (charts) for most benchmarks were not extractable; only table and prose values are
  recorded.

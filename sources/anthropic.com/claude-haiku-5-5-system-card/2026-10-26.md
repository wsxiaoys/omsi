## What this document is, verbatim

> "This system card describes Claude Haiku 5.5, the latest Haiku-class large language model
> from Anthropic. We present results from a wide variety of pre-deployment evaluations,
> which show Claude Haiku 5.5 significantly outperforming its predecessor, Claude Haiku 4.5,
> across many domains. In a few areas, it rivaled or exceeded Claude Sonnet 5.5."

> "As a result, this system card is somewhat shorter than some previous ones. We expect
> system cards for our non-frontier models to be similarly condensed going forward, except
> where a specific model has notable properties that are deserving of in-depth elaboration."

**Safeguard interference is quantified here**, unlike in the Opus 5.5 card: "We ran Haiku
5.5 without a fallback model, so when the safeguards flagged a request the trial stopped
there. This happened in 1.8% of trials (12 of 660, 10 of them on a single task), and all of
these trials failed."

## Safeguards: no fallback routing

> "For chemical and biological (CB) risks, we have deployed the same harmful CB misuse
> classifiers as for our deployments of Claude Opus 5 and Claude Sonnet 5, rather than the
> broader research biology classifiers used for Claude Opus 5.5. These classifiers have no
> fallback model."

> "For cyber misuse, we have deployed blocking classifiers targeting specific harmful cyber
> activities. These are significantly narrower than our cyber safeguards for our more
> capable models. These classifiers have no fallback model."

> "Unlike some of our more capable recent models, blocks on these safeguards will not fall
> back to any other models on our first-party products and our API. Traffic on our models via
> other platforms and providers may experience different behavior."

## RSP and alignment, verbatim

> "Our Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) evaluations found that Claude Haiku 5.5 is broadly
> less capable than Claude Opus 5 across domains and does not cross any new RSP thresholds."

> "we do treat Haiku 5.5 as meeting our CB-1 and Autonomy-1 thresholds, and we apply the
> corresponding mitigations from our RSP."

Anthropic ECI: "Claude Haiku 5.5's AECI of 167.11 was much lower than Claude Opus 5.5's
score of 174.56."

> "It was at least as honest under pressure as every other model we tested and much weaker at
> covert sabotage than our larger models. On the other hand, it over-refused more than any
> other model we tested in our automated behavioral audit, and it used a leaked answer without
> telling the user more often than Haiku 4.5."

> "Our agentic safety evaluations found Claude Haiku 5.5 to be our most robust Haiku-class
> model yet to prompt injections, largely matching the robustness of our frontier models
> against adaptive attackers in coding and computer use environments."

Cyber capability, with safeguards off: ExploitBench mean 5.15 capability flags, Cap% 38%
(plain arm), 6.56 flags / 49% (auto-nudge arm), and full arbitrary code execution in "1.0% of
the time (4/410)" across both arms.

## Training, data, and output, verbatim

- "The model outputs text only."
- "Haiku 5.5's knowledge cutoff date is June 2026."
- Training data: "a proprietary mix of publicly available information from the internet,
  public and private datasets, user data included in feedback or bugs or which users have
  explicitly permitted for training, and other sources, such as synthetic data generated by
  other models."
- Snapshot caveat: "Unless a section says otherwise, the evaluations in this system card were
  run on the final snapshot of Claude Haiku 5.5. Some sections instead use an earlier or
  alternate snapshot, or run the model with its production safeguards disabled".

## Summary

The only first-party document that states Haiku 5.5's benchmark numbers with harness, trial
counts, effort level, and error bars. It also establishes the two facts that distinguish the
model from its larger 5.5 siblings: safeguards block rather than fall back to a weaker model, and the capability
ceiling sits well below Sonnet 5.5 on every row of Table 8.1.A.

## Gaps

- No per-effort numeric table; effort-level results appear only in figures.
- Charts (accuracy vs. cost by effort) are images and were not extracted.
- No stated parameter count, architecture, or training compute.
- The card is explicitly condensed relative to earlier Anthropic system cards; several
  evaluations were omitted.

## Executive summary claims, verbatim

> "It scored higher on every evaluation in our capability summary (Table 8.1.A), with the
> largest gains in agentic coding, visual reasoning, computer use, and long-horizon
> professional knowledge work. It delivers this performance at lower cost: much of the
> improvement is available below maximum reasoning effort. It also sets the state of the
> art on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and on several independently run benchmarks, including
> CursorBench, GDPval-AA, and AA-Briefcase."

On chemical/biological risk: Opus 5.5 is treated as "having CB-1 capabilities (relating to
the synthesis of non-novel weapons) but not CB-2 capabilities (relating to the synthesis of
novel weapons)", and "it did not improve on several of the weaknesses we considered
disqualifying for CB-2 in that model [Claude Mythos 5.1]".

On autonomy / AI R&D: internal measures "do not show a sustained AI-attributable 2×
acceleration in the pace of development"; external testing was by METR, whose findings
Anthropic says were consistent with that assessment. Frontier Design ran a human tabletop
biology scenario for the CB evaluation.

On alignment: "the risk of catastrophic harm from misalignment is low". CoT controllability
is reported as "about as well as Claude Opus 4.7", treated as weak evidence that Opus
5.5's chain of thought "is not very monitorable".

## Long-context evidence

ProgramBench (section 8.10.1) is described as a long-context agentic coding benchmark of
200 program-reconstruction tasks, reduced to 166 after excluding flaky tasks:

> "On this set, Claude Opus 5.5 scored 91.2%, whereas Claude Fable 5.1 scored 87.6% and
> Claude Opus 5 scored 85.4%."

> "We believe ProgramBench is a strong measure of long-context coding performance: Opus
> 5.5 episodes cover a range of context lengths up to the full 1M token window."

## Safeguards as deployed (section 1.5), verbatim

> "For chemical and biological (CB) risks, we have deployed the same research biology
> classifiers in use for our deployments of Claude Fable 5 and Claude Fable 5.1. … Blocks
> on this classifier will fall back to Claude Opus 5."

> "For cyber misuse, we have deployed classifiers that block a similar set of topics to
> Claude Opus 5, but with higher robustness. Blocks on these classifiers will fall back to
> Opus 4.8."

> "Our classifiers to prevent distillation of our models (for example, by attempting to
> extract a model's hidden reasoning) will block on Claude Opus 5.5 with no fallback
> model. The fallback behavior described above applies to our first-party products and
> developers …"

Section 6.4.10 ("Impact of fallback behavior") adds:

> "Claude Opus 5.5 is deployed in such a way that, in typical product deployments,
> requests that touch on a few sensitive areas for high-stakes misuse will be referred to
> a weaker model rather than answered by the primary Opus 5.5 model."

This is the mechanism behind Artificial Analysis's "with fallback" variant label: the
scored system is Opus 5.5 plus routing that hands some requests to Opus 4.8 or Opus 5.

## Snapshots and configuration

Section 1.4, verbatim: "Different “snapshots” of Claude Opus 5.5 are taken at various
points during the training process. Unless a section says otherwise, the evaluations in
this system card were run on the final snapshot of Claude Opus 5.5."

## What this card does not disclose

- **No parameter count, architecture, or training-compute figure** anywhere in the 230
  pages. Training data is described only generically ("a proprietary mix of publicly
  available information from the internet, public and private datasets, user data … and
  other sources, such as synthetic data").
- No first-party API model ID string appears in the card; identity comes from the
  platform docs.
- No per-effort capability table in the summary; effort sweeps appear only as charts.

## Gaps

- Charts (effort sweeps, cost-accuracy frontiers) were not captured; only captions and
  surrounding prose.
- Competitor figures are "drawn from the respective developers' published system cards or
  benchmark leaderboards", i.e. not re-run by Anthropic, and are marked as such.
- Some rows are third-party-run and some are vendor-run, and the card says which: CursorBench
  scores "were measured and reported independently by Cursor"; AA-Briefcase
  "[e]valuations were run independently by Artificial Analysis"; AutomationBench is
  Zapier's benchmark and Zapier's runs. The SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench, FrontierCode
  (Cognition tasks), HLE, OSWorld and HealthBench rows are Anthropic's own runs.
- GDPval-AA Elo is anchored to DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (max) at 1,600, so it is only
  comparable within that anchor, not to other Elo scales.

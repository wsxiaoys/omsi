# Claude Sonnet 5.5 — launch announcement

- **Origin:** "Introducing Claude Sonnet 5.5", Anthropic,
  <https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5>. Dated "September 28, 2026" on the page.
  Retrieved 2026-10-26.
- **Evidence kind:** first-party marketing announcement (vendor-run benchmark table,
  vendor-stated cost/speed deltas, safeguards, tester testimonials). Marketing claims are
  labelled as such below; the benchmark table is vendor-run unless a footnote says
  otherwise.
- **Publication date:** 2026-09-28. Capture date 2026-10-26.
- **Relevant models:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`), compared with Claude Sonnet
  5, Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol.
- **Capture type:** full snapshot of the rendered page text, plus the announcement's
  benchmark table recovered verbatim from the page's embedded chart data.

## Headline claims, verbatim

> "Introducing Claude Sonnet 5.5, the second model in the Claude 5.5 family. It's a clear
> upgrade over Claude Sonnet 5, runs 30%+ faster, and costs up to 30% less for most work."

> "Sonnet 5.5 is a faster, lower-cost complement to Claude Opus 5.5. Where Opus 5.5 is built
> for complex work requiring careful judgment, Sonnet 5.5 is strongest at well-scoped
> everyday tasks, fixing bugs, and creating polished documents, slides, and spreadsheets."

> "Claude Haiku 5.5, built for high-volume and cost-sensitive applications, will join the
> Claude 5.5 family in the coming weeks."

> "It scores two points below Opus 5.5 on GDPval-AA, a test of real-world work across a
> variety of occupations. And it's strong on long-horizon work and image understanding — it's
> the first Sonnet model to beat Pokémon Red working only from screenshots."

> "Sonnet 5.5 generates outputs 30%+ faster than Sonnet 5, making it our fastest Sonnet model
> to date."

Cost, verbatim: "Sonnet 5.5 is priced the same as Sonnet 5 at $2 per million input tokens and
$10 per million output tokens, but it typically needs far fewer tokens to do the same work.
Cache reads cost $0.10 per million tokens, half of Sonnet 5's $0.20. In our testing, it costs
up to 30% less per task than its predecessor."

Footnotes, verbatim:

1. "Terminal-Bench 4.0 results are reported for Claude Opus 5.5 at Xhigh effort which
   represents the model's highest score."
2. "Sonnet 5.5 scores lower at Max effort than at Xhigh. FrontierCode evaluates whether a
   code change could be merged without human edits. It penalizes out-of-scope changes, even
   if they are high-quality or helpful. At Max effort, Sonnet 5.5 more often ran Claude
   Code's code-review skill, which splits the review across many subagents, and in two cases
   Cognition examined, this led to a timeout or to extra edits beyond the task's scope, and
   therefore to a lower score."
3. "Artificial Analysis ran GDPval-AA and AA-Briefcase on a pre-release deployment of Sonnet
   5.5 on the Claude Platform, which we found to have a bug that could degrade responses to
   requests that use structured outputs. We expect the effect on Sonnet 5.5's scores, if any,
   to be small and to understate its performance. That bug has since been fixed."
4. "OpenAI recently fixed a bug that degraded image understanding in GPT-6 Sol. Official
   AA-Briefcase v1.1 and GDPval-AA v2.1 scores from Artificial Analysis, and Chartography
   scores from Surge AI, may not have been updated yet to reflect the latest version of the
   model."

**The 70.6% Terminal-Bench 4.0 row is a routed-system number, not a bare-model number.** The
system card states it was measured "with safeguards enabled; requests flagged by the
safeguards were answered by a fallback model following the default server-side fallback
policy (1.2% of requests, affecting 1.5% of trials)". See the
[system card](../claude-sonnet-5-5-system-card/2026-10-26.md).

## Cost and speed, verbatim

| Price per 1M tokens | Claude Sonnet 5.5 | Claude Opus 5.5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cache reads | $0.10 | $0.20 |
| Cache writes | $2.50 | $5 |
| Input tokens | $2 | $4 |
| Output tokens | $10 | $20 |

> "Sonnet 5.5 requires fewer tokens per task than Sonnet 5, so it's less expensive to run. It
> also generates output 30%+ faster"

> "In Claude Code and our apps, the default effort is set to Medium, while the Claude Platform
> defaults to High."

That last sentence is a three-way distinction worth preserving: the announcement says Claude
Code and the consumer apps default to `medium`; the Claude Platform (API) defaults to `high`
(confirmed by the model page).

## Safety, verbatim

> "Sonnet 5.5 doesn't advance the frontier of our models' capabilities, so our alignment
> assessment focused on a targeted set of risks that apply to models of any capability
> level"

> "On our automated behavioral audit, which tests Claude across roughly 1,850 scenarios,
> Sonnet 5.5 improves on or matches Sonnet 5 on most measures of alignment, resistance to
> misuse, and honesty."

> "Because its cybersecurity capabilities are comparable to Opus 5's, it's the first Sonnet
> model to launch with cyber safeguards and fallbacks like those we've developed for our most
> capable models. Its biology safeguards are the same as Sonnet 5's."

> "Users can still find and fix bugs in their code as part of routine software development,
> but higher-risk cybersecurity tasks will visibly fall back to Sonnet 5."

> "Because Sonnet 5.5 is far more capable than its predecessor, it's the first Sonnet model to
> launch with safety classifiers that prevent reasoning extraction."

**Marketing claims, labelled as such:** "a clear upgrade", "our fastest Sonnet model to date",
"best combination of speed and intelligence", "a sharp eye for design", "writes more clearly
than our previous generation", and thirteen named early-tester testimonials (Epic Games, Every,
CodeRabbit, SpaceXAI, Base44, Unity, Creator, Slack, Zendesk, Balyasny Asset Management, Box,
Lovable, Atlassian). The testimonials are vendor-selected and unverifiable; none is treated as
capability evidence here.

## Access, verbatim

> "Claude Sonnet 5.5 is now available on all platforms, including Amazon Web Services, Google
> Cloud, and Microsoft Azure."

> "As with Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 is available with zero data retention."

> "If you run Sonnet with thinking off, you'll need to switch to the new `between_tools`
> setting, which keeps up-front thinking off, before moving to Sonnet 5.5."

The announcement says "Microsoft Azure"; the docs' platform table says "Microsoft Foundry".
Both refer to Anthropic models served through Microsoft's cloud; the naming is inconsistent
across first-party pages captured the same day.

## Gaps

- No confidence intervals, trial counts, or harness detail in the announcement; those are in
  the system card.
- The effort level behind each benchmark row is stated only for FrontierCode (Max and Xhigh)
  and Terminal-Bench (implicitly max, per the system card). The other rows do not say.
- No per-region or partner pricing.
- The "up to 30% less per task" claim is not decomposed into price and token-count effects
  here; the system card's per-effort cost charts were not captured numerically.

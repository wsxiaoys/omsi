## What this establishes

- **Turbo v2.5 was a current, recommended model as late as 2026-02-12 and deprecated by 2026-03-12.**
  That one-month bracket is the tightest date evidence available; no changelog entry announcing the
  change was found (the 2026-02-09 … 2026-04-01 changelog entries were checked and none mentions
  `eleven_turbo_v2_5`).
- The vendor's **first-party latency figure for this model is ~250–300 ms** ("~250ms-300ms†", † =
  "Excluding application & network latency"), stated on the flagship card and in the model table.
  That is 3.3–4× the ~75 ms the same page gives for Flash v2.5 and roughly 2.5–3× the ~100 ms later
  given for Eleven v4 Turbo.
- **Character limit 40,000** (~40 minutes) was the vendor's published ceiling for this model. The
  live character-limits table at capture no longer has a Turbo v2.5 row.
- The vendor positioned Turbo v2.5 as a **quality-for-latency trade against Flash v2.5** ("an ideal
  choice for all scenarios where you'd use Flash v2.5, but where you're willing to trade off latency
  for higher quality voice generation"). Its later deprecated-table description contradicts that
  framing by calling it "outclassed by Flash models".
- The concurrency column was named "(Turbo & Flash)" while Turbo was current and is "(Flash)" on the
  live page — consistent with Turbo v2.5 having left the entitlement table.

## Gaps

- The archive cannot show whether the model was *served* differently across these dates; it only
  shows what the documentation said.
- No snapshot is available for the exact day of the change; the bracket is 2026-02-12 →
  2026-03-12.
- The † footnote text ("Excluding application & network latency") is captured from the page, but the
  page's linked "Understanding latency" article — captured separately — no longer discusses Turbo
  v2.5 at all, so the measurement conditions behind ~250–300 ms are unpublished.

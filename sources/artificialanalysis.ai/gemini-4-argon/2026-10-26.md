## Label → canonical model ID

The page title is "Gemini 4 Argon (high)". Three facts tie it to `gemini-4-argon` at
thinking level HIGH:

1. **Release date.** AA: "released on September 30, 2026" — the date of Google's
   announcement post, two weeks before general availability.
2. **Price.** AA: "$2.00 per 1M input tokens and $10.00 per 1M output tokens (based on
   Google's API)", i.e. Google's introductory price.
3. **Effort set.** AA's variant is `high`; Google documents exactly three thinking levels
   (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH), and OpenRouter's metadata exposes the same three efforts with
   `medium` as default.

The name itself is not a codename: "Gemini 4 Argon" is Google's own product name and part of
the model ID `gemini-4-argon`.

## Summary

Independent confirmation of the model's existence, price, 1M context, and reasoning-only
configuration, plus the only non-vendor measurements in this wiki for the model: index 53
(#9 of 221), 79.7 tok/s, TTFT 50.81 s, 110M output tokens on the index, $1.62 cost per index
task, four API providers.

## Gaps

- AA lists input modalities as text and image only, while Google and OpenRouter both list
  audio and video input. AA's simplification, not a contradiction to resolve in AA's favour.
- AA's header says "Released September 2026" and its FAQ says "September 30, 2026", neither
  of which is the 2026-10-13 GA date Google's own model page prints. Both dates are real
  events (announcement vs general availability); AA tracks the first.
- AA's page says "This page shows the reasoning version of this model. A non-reasoning
  variant may also exist." Google documents no non-reasoning mode: `MINIMAL` and
  `thinking_budget` return HTTP 400.
- No per-benchmark numbers in the text capture, no confidence intervals, no statement of
  which provider endpoint AA used.

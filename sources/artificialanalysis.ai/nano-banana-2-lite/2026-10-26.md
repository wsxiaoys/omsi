## What this record establishes

- **Lite is measurably cheaper and faster than its siblings in independent data.** $33.6 vs $67
  (Nano Banana 2) per 1,000 images, and 3.03 s vs 7.74 s median generation time. The price
  figure matches Google's list ($0.0336) exactly, so AA is quoting list price, not a measured
  bill.
- **Lite is clearly below Nano Banana 2 on quality in this arena.** 1106.69 against 1139.99 is a
  33.3-point gap and the 95% confidence intervals do not overlap (1096.69–1116.69 vs
  1129.99–1149.99).
- **Lite is statistically indistinguishable from Nano Banana Pro** in the same arena: an 11.3
  point difference with heavily overlapping intervals. This does not prove parity — it means
  this sample cannot separate them. It does contradict the simple story that the cheap tier is
  the worst tier.
- **Google's "sub-2 second end-to-end latency" is not reproduced.** AA's median is 3.03 s even
  at the 5th percentile end of the distribution (2.52 s). AA's method includes downloading the
  image, so the two figures are not the same quantity; neither page reconciles them.

## Gaps

- **No Nano Banana 2.1 row anywhere on the page.** `artificialanalysis.ai/image/models/` returns
  404 for `nano-banana-2.1`, `gemini-nano-banana-2.1` and `nano-banana-2-1`, so this arena cannot
  place Lite against its closest-priced sibling.
- The arena prompt set, sample size and voting protocol are described only on a separate
  methodology page, not captured here.
- The "Google Benchmark" endpoint label is AA's own classification; the page does not explain
  what distinguishes it from a plain provider endpoint, nor which Gemini API region or tier it
  calls.
- No per-prompt breakdown, no failure/refusal rate, no resolution or aspect-ratio scoping. The
  arena presumably compares like-for-like outputs but does not say so on this page.
- The three Google rows all carry ±10-point intervals, while other models on the same page
  carry wider ones (±45 for FLUX.1 [dev]); the page does not explain the difference, so the
  intervals may reflect a convention rather than a per-model measurement.

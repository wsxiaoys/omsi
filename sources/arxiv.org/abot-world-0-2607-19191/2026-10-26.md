# arXiv 2607.19191 — ABot-World-0: Infinite Interactive World Rollout on a Single Desktop GPU

- Origin: arXiv preprint `https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19191`, HTML full text
  `https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19191v1`; "ABot-World Team", 39 named authors; title
  "ABot-World-0: Infinite Interactive World Rollout on a Single Desktop GPU"
- Evidence kind: technical report (first-party; developer-authored, not peer reviewed at capture)
- Publication date: submitted 2026-07-21 (v1); the repository news list dates the release
  announcement 2026-07-22
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: ABot-World-0 (5B causal student), ABot-World teacher (not released)

## Abstract (verbatim)

> We present ABot-World-0, an action-conditioned video world model for real-time, long-horizon
> closed-loop interaction, supported by a multi-source data infrastructure spanning AAA games,
> simulation engines, and internet videos to learn controllable world dynamics. WorldExplorer performs
> agent-driven collection guided by training feedback, while a unified pipeline applies 14
> deterministic quality checks, VLM-based assessment, and synchronized action and text annotation. We
> progressively distill a bidirectional action-conditioned teacher into a causal student through
> teacher forcing and ODE distillation, and introduce LongForcing to align long student self-rollouts
> with an extended-horizon teacher, mitigating accumulated distribution shift and autoregressive drift.
> Raw keyboard actions provide a unified control interface for scene roaming and third-person character
> interaction, while reference-character memory provides persistent appearance cues for identity
> consistency during third-person rollouts. For deployment, we co-design a streaming inference stack
> with a lightweight VAE decoder, efficient attention, memory-aware scheduling, and low-bit DiT
> inference. Across optimized low-bit configurations, ABot-World-0 streams 720P video at up to 16 FPS
> on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 desktop GPU, with 1.2 s action-to-first-frame latency and approximately
> 19 GiB peak VRAM. Experiments on WorldRoamBench and extended interactive rollouts demonstrate
> competitive controllability and coherent long-horizon world evolution.

## Deployment claims (verbatim, §1 and §4.4)

> Across its optimized low-bit operating envelope, the resulting 720P streaming pipeline reaches up to
> 16 FPS with 1.2 s action-to-first-frame latency on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 while operating within a
> peak-VRAM budget of approximately 19 GiB.

> The reported latency covers the complete generation-and-decoding path from receiving a user action to
> making the first decoded response frame available, rather than reporting sampling speed in isolation.

Conclusion restates the memory bound more tightly: "peak VRAM remains below 19.3 GiB across the
optimized low-bit operating points."

The system is **chunk-wise**, not frame-wise: "Our method adopts a chunk-wise streaming pipeline with
progressive visual delivery." The paper notes the trade-off explicitly — frame-wise autoregressive
designs "minimize action-to-pixel latency because each new control can influence the very next emitted
frame" but need "one full denoising cycle per frame, which sharply limits throughput", whereas
chunk-wise generation "denois[es] a chunk of frames jointly … at the cost of a bounded control delay
within the current chunk."

## Control interface (verbatim)

> Raw keyboard actions provide a unified control interface for scene roaming and third-person
> character interaction, while reference-character memory provides persistent appearance cues for
> identity consistency during third-person rollouts.

> Explicit actions admit simple conditioning. Keyboard inputs are discrete, temporally aligned with the
> video, and directly reflect user intent. For these explicit control signals, we use additive
> injection at the patch-embedding stage to provide reliable control while preserving the visual prior
> of the pretrained model.

Inputs are therefore: a conditioning image (first frame), a text scene description, a per-frame
keyboard action stream, and optionally reference-character conditioning. The paper frames the task as
"closed-loop interaction", i.e. an environment, not a clip generator.

## WorldRoamBench comparison (Table 3, verbatim values)

| Model | Size | Strict Acc. | Partial Acc. | Traj. Score | Aesthetic | Imaging | Mechanics | Memory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genie 3 | – | 0.4700 | 0.6608 | 0.6719 | 0.4711 | 0.4757 | 0.5454 | 0.6073 |
| HappyOyster | – | 0.5317 | 0.7631 | 0.7737 | 0.5235 | 0.4377 | 0.5395 | 0.6309 |
| LingBot-World | 14B | 0.3235 | 0.4198 | 0.4094 | 0.2898 | 0.2875 | 0.2777 | 0.3006 |
| HY-World 1.5 | 8.3B | 0.1640 | 0.2088 | 0.2015 | 0.1400 | 0.1236 | 0.1115 | 0.1562 |
| ABot-World-0 | 5B | 0.5266 | 0.7290 | 0.6752 | 0.5039 | 0.4651 | 0.5223 | 0.5041 |

The caption says "Best results are shown in bold, and second-best results are underlined", and the
body text says "ABot-World-0 achieves strong action fidelity, trajectory following, visual quality,
physical mechanics, and memory scores."

**Read the numbers, not the sentence.** Ranked per column, ABot-World-0 is second on five of the seven
(Strict Acc., Partial Acc., Traj. Score, Aesthetic, Imaging) and third on two (Mechanics, Memory). It
**leads no column at all**, so the caption's bolding convention has nothing to bold for this row.
Against Genie 3 it wins four columns and loses three, by these margins in points:

| Column | vs Genie 3 | vs HappyOyster |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strict Acc. | +5.66 | −0.51 |
| Partial Acc. | +6.82 | −3.41 |
| Traj. Score | +0.33 | −9.85 |
| Aesthetic | +3.28 | −1.96 |
| Imaging | −1.06 | +2.74 |
| Mechanics | −2.31 | −1.72 |
| Memory | **−10.32** | **−12.68** |

So a 5B model lands just behind a same-category product (HappyOyster) on almost everything and beats
Genie 3 on action following and aesthetics, while memory retention — the property the paper's own
LongForcing contribution targets — is its weakest column, ~10 points behind Genie 3 and ~13 behind
HappyOyster. The paper does not discuss those losses.

**Provenance caveat.** WorldRoamBench is reference [84]: "T. Xu, J. Sui, Z. Gao, K. Shi, W. Yang,
Z. Liu, Z. Sun, M. Sun, H. Pan, F. Jiang, M. Xu, Q. Fan, Y. Gao, Y. Li, and B. Chen (2026)
WorldRoamBench: an open-world benchmark for long-horizon stability of interactive world models",
arXiv 2606.31672. Those author names are the same people listed in this paper's Benchmark Team, so the
benchmark and the evaluation are from one group. The table is a vendor-run result on an in-house
benchmark, not an independent measurement. The baselines are also not self-run at matched settings
that the paper documents.

## LongForcing ablation (verbatim)

> We compare LongForcing with a Causal-Forcing-style baseline adapted to our interactive world model
> under the same rollout protocol … We evaluate both variants over 60-second rollouts using the HPSv3
> score, high-saturation pixel ratio, perceptual blur score, and patch repeat ratio.

Only curves (Figure 10) are given — "LongForcing retains a higher HPSv3 score and lower
artifact-related metrics over the same interval" — with no numeric values in the text.

## Data

Training corpus spans AAA games, simulation engines, and internet videos, collected by "WorldExplorer"
with 14 deterministic quality checks plus VLM assessment. The 500-hour dataset is published
(`acvlab/ABot-World-Explorer-500h`); a second "4D" dataset is linked from the model card but was not
inspected.

## Siblings named in the paper

The comparison table names the other world models the team positions against: **Genie 3**, **HappyOyster**
(reference [85]: "Alibaba Cloud (2026) Alibaba launches HappyOyster, a world model product for
real-time immersive creation and interaction", Alibaba Cloud Blog), **LingBot-World** (Robbyant Team,
arXiv 2601.20540) and **HY-World 1.5** (Tencent Hunyuan). HappyOyster is thus Alibaba's *other*
real-time world-model product, from Alibaba Cloud rather than AMAP CV Lab.

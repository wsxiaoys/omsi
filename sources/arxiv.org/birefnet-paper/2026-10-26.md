## Abstract (verbatim)

> We introduce a novel bilateral reference framework (BiRefNet) for high-resolution dichotomous
> image segmentation (DIS). It comprises two essential components: the localization module (LM) and
> the reconstruction module (RM) with our proposed bilateral reference (BiRef). The LM aids in
> object localization using global semantic information. Within the RM, we utilize BiRef for the
> reconstruction process, where hierarchical patches of images provide the source reference and
> gradient maps serve as the target reference. These components collaborate to generate the final
> predicted maps. We also introduce auxiliary gradient supervision to enhance focus on regions with
> finer details. Furthermore, we outline practical training strategies tailored for DIS to improve
> map quality and training process. To validate the general applicability of our approach, we
> conduct extensive experiments on four tasks to evince that BiRefNet exhibits remarkable
> performance, outperforming task-specific cutting-edge methods across all benchmarks. Our codes
> are available at this https URL.

## Architecture and default backbone (verbatim excerpts)

> Baseline. We provide a simple but strong encoder-decoder network as the baseline for the DIS task.
> To capture better hierarchical features on various scales, we chose the Swin transformer large
> [30] as our default backbone network.

> We equip our BiRefNet with different backbones to obtain models in different sizes. Runtime,
> number of parameters, MACs, and performance of them are further tested to provide a comprehensive
> comparison between them and other methods.

Input handling (verbatim): "All images are resized to 1024 × 1024 for training and testing. The
generated segmentation maps are resized (i.e., bilinear interpolation) to the original size of the
corresponding GT maps for evaluation."

Training (verbatim): "The proposed BiRefNet is trained with Adam optimizer [22] for DIS/HRSOD/COD
tasks for 600/150/150 epochs, respectively… Models are trained with PyTorch [33] on eight NVIDIA
A100 GPUs. The batch size is set to N=4 for each GPU during training."

The paper is a **prompt-free, single-foreground** design: nothing in the paper takes points, boxes,
or class labels at inference; the model predicts one binary map per image. (The repo separately
offers a box-guided inference notebook; see the README record.)

## Benchmarks evaluated (named)

- **DIS5K** — test sets DIS-TE1, DIS-TE2, DIS-TE3, DIS-TE4 (500 images each) and DIS-VD (470).
- **HRSOD** — DAVIS-S, HRSOD-TE, UHRSD-TE.
- **COD** — CAMO, CHAMELEON, NC4K, COD10K.
- **SOD** — DUTS-TE, DUT-OMRON (per the README's Papers-with-Code badge links).

Metrics (as labelled in Table 4): `Fβ^x`, `Fβ^ω`, `M` (MAE), `S_m`, `E_φ^m`, `HCE_γ`.

## What the paper does not contain

- **No SAM comparison.** The captured v7 text contains zero occurrences of "SAM"; the comparison
  set is DIS/HRSOD/COD/SOD segmentation networks (BASNet, U²-Net, HRNet, PGNet, IS-Net, FP-DIS,
  UDUN, HitNet, FSPNet, ZoomNet, SINetv2).
- **No video evaluation.** Video inference appears only in the repo's tutorial notebook.
- **No matting/portrait numbers in the paper.** The matting and portrait results are in the repo's
  model zoo table (TE-P3M-500-NP and P3M-500-P), not in the paper's tables.
- **No mention of "v2", versioned weights, or a hosted endpoint.**

## Gaps

- The paper's tables were produced by the authors; this knowledge base has not re-run any of them.
- Metric definitions follow the DIS literature (S-measure, weighted F-measure, MAE, E-measure,
  HCE); the paper does not restate per-metric tolerance bands, so small deltas between rows should
  not be read as meaningful without the original metric code.
- The journal version (CAAI AIR 9150038) may differ from arXiv v7; only the arXiv text was captured.

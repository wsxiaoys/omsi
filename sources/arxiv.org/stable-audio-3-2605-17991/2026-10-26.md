## Abstract (verbatim)

> Stable Audio 3 is a family of fast latent diffusion models (small, medium, large) for variable-length
> audio generation and editing. Since our models can generate several minutes of audio, variable-length
> generations are key to avoid the cost of producing full-length generations for short sounds. We also
> support inpainting, enabling targeted audio editing and the continuation of short recordings. Our
> latent diffusion models operate on top of a novel semantic-acoustic autoencoder that projects audio
> into a compact latent space, enabling efficient diffusion-based generation while preserving audio
> fidelity and encouraging semantic structure in the latent. Finally, we run adversarial post-training
> to both accelerate inference and improve generation quality, reducing the number of inference steps
> while improving fidelity and prompt adherence. Stable Audio 3 models are trained on licensed and
> Creative Commons data to generate music and sounds in less than a 2s on an H200 GPU and less than a
> few seconds on a MacBook Pro M4. We release the weights of small and medium, that can run on
> consumer-grade hardware, together with their training and inference pipeline.

So the four tiers are **459M (small, split into music and SFX checkpoints)**, **1.4B (medium)** and
**2.7B (large)** diffusion transformers, each on top of a SAME autoencoder (108M for small, 852M for
medium and large). Only small and medium have open weights.

## Architecture and inference, verbatim

> Our aim is to maintain high-fidelity audio reconstruction while learning a compact latent space (with
> 4096×downsampling) …

> our latent diffusion training pipeline consists of three stages: flow matching pre-training, ODE
> warmup distillation, and adversarial post-training.

> we employ ping-pong sampling that addresses this by decomposing the single large step into multiple
> smaller ones. … We found that **8 sampling steps** provide a favorable trade-off between inference
> efficiency and generation quality.

> Our model does not require classifier-free guidance (CFG) at inference. … As a result, our models do
> not rely on CFG during inference.

> Given a requested duration of *d* seconds, we allocate a latent sequence of *L* = ⌈(*d* + *d*_silence)
> · *f*_s / *r*⌉ embeddings, where *d* is the generation duration requested by the user, *d*_silence = 6s
> is silence padding, *f*_s = 44,100Hz is the sample rate, and *r* = 4,096 is the autoencoder
> downsampling ratio.

Output is therefore **44.1 kHz stereo**, trimmed to the requested duration from a 6-second padded
window.

## Training data, verbatim

> medium and large are trained on a combination of licensed audio from AudioSparx and Creative Commons
> recordings from Freesound. The AudioSparx portion (806,284 audios) contains music tracks, instruments,
> and sound effects with text metadata. The Freesound portion consists of recordings licensed under
> CC-0, CC-BY, or CC-Sampling+. To ensure no copyrighted content was present in the Freesound data,
> music recordings were identified using the PANNs tagger. We flagged audio that activated music-related
> tags for at least 30s (threshold of 0.15), that was sent to a trusted content detection company to
> verify the absence of copyrighted material. … After filtering, the Freesound part includes 266,324
> CC-0, 194,840 CC-BY, and 11,454 CC-Sampling+ recordings.

> All small models are initially pre-trained on a mixture of AudioSparx and Freesound. But for the final
> stage of pre-training, distillation warmup, and post-training, we use AudioSparx for small-music and a
> higher-quality subset of Freesound for small-sfx.

## Recommended prompt prefixes, verbatim

> All Stable Audio models trained on the AudioSparx dataset … prepend prompts with "TrackType: Music,
> VocalType: Instrumental," for music generation and "TrackType: SFX," for sound effects generation.
> These prefixes indicate the target audio modality and significantly improve generation quality. We
> therefore recommend all Stable Audio 3 users to use those prefixes at inference time.

## Developer-run evaluation (not independent)

Metrics: FAD (LAION-CLAP `630k-audioset-best` embeddings), CLAP score, inference time, plus a 14-person
MOS listening test on OVL (overall quality), REL (text relevance), MUS (musicality).

Instrumental music on Song Describer Dataset, 120 s generations (Table 3, verbatim):

> | | length | FAD↓ | CLAP↑ | OVL↑ | REL↑ | MUS↑ | inference time (s)↓ |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | Stable Audio 2.5 | 120s | 0.106 | 0.395 | 3.90±0.79 | 4.30±0.66 | 3.70±0.92 | 0.85 |
> | small-music | 120s | 0.145 | 0.393 | 3.20±0.89 | 3.60±0.94 | 3.15±0.81 | 0.45 |
> | medium | 120s | 0.107 | 0.390 | 4.20±0.89 | 4.25±0.85 | 4.15±0.93 | 0.78 |
> | large | 120s | 0.101 | 0.393 | 3.95±0.89 | 3.80±1.11 | 4.30±0.73 | 0.81 |
> | ACE-Step 1.5 xl-turbo | 120s | 0.193 | 0.321 | 3.35±1.09 | 3.30±1.13 | 3.15±1.31 | 6.23 |

At 190 s (Table 4): medium FAD 0.116 / CLAP 0.362; large FAD 0.100 / CLAP 0.373; Stable Audio 2.5 FAD
0.128 / CLAP 0.375. Sound effects on BBC Sound Effects, 5 s (Table 5): medium FAD 0.369 / CLAP 0.369,
large 0.358 / 0.370, small-sfx 0.395 / 0.351, Woosh Flow 0.580 / 0.277, SAO 0.501 / 0.263.

Duration sweep (Table 7, medium and large, verbatim):

> | | length | FAD↓ | CLAP↑ | inference time (s)↓ |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | medium | 20s | 0.163 | 0.392 | 0.62 |
> | medium | 120s | 0.107 | 0.390 | 0.78 |
> | medium | 190s | 0.116 | 0.362 | 0.88 |
> | medium | 380s | 0.156 | 0.275 | 1.31 |
> | large | 380s | 0.131 | 0.277 | 1.80 |

The authors' own reading of the long-duration degradation, verbatim:

> At very long lengths (380s), performance also degrades, particularly the CLAP score. Informal listening
> suggests that the reduced prompt adherence is because long training examples are predominantly ambient
> or classical music. As a result, conditioning on long durations can bias the model toward generating
> ambient or classical music, omitting the provided text prompt.

Base versus post-trained, at 120 s (Table 11, music): base models need ~50 steps (medium base FAD 0.143 /
CLAP 0.352 at 50 steps, 3.87 s); post-trained reach FAD 0.107 / CLAP 0.390 at 8 steps (0.78 s), and
degrade sharply at 1 step (FAD 0.258 / CLAP 0.355). This is the evidence behind fal's default of 8 steps
for post-trained endpoints and 50 for base endpoints.

## Summary

The paper is the authoritative source for sizes (459M / 1.4B / 2.7B diffusion transformers), the
2-minute and 6m20s ceilings, 44.1 kHz stereo output, 8-step post-trained inference without CFG, the
AudioSparx + Freesound training corpus with per-licence counts, the recommended `TrackType:` prompt
prefixes, and the vendor's own benchmark numbers. It also documents the two failure modes the marketing
does not: prompt adherence collapses at 380 s, and short (20 s) generations score worse than mid-length
ones.

## Gaps

- **No watermarking or provenance statement anywhere in 26 pages.** No C2PA, no audio watermark, no
  disclosure mechanism is described.
- No evaluation against commercial systems (Suno, Udio, Eleven Music, Lyria) — only open-weight
  baselines, and the best baseline on music FAD/CLAP is Stability's own Stable Audio 2.5.
- The listening test is 14 participants with no reported inter-annotator agreement, and it is run by
  the developer.
- large's weights are not released, so no third party can reproduce its rows.
- No stated licence for the paper's own code/weights beyond the repository metadata.

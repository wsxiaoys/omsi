## Abstract (verbatim)

> Recent advancements in 3D generative modeling have significantly improved the generation realism, yet the
> field is still hampered by existing representations, which struggle to capture assets with complex
> topologies and detailed appearance. This paper present an approach for learning a structured latent
> representation from native 3D data to address this challenge. At its core is a new sparse voxel structure
> called O-Voxel, an omni-voxel representation that encodes both geometry and appearance. O-Voxel can
> robustly model arbitrary topology, including open, non-manifold, and fully-enclosed surfaces, while
> capturing comprehensive surface attributes beyond texture color, such as physically-based rendering
> parameters. Based on O-Voxel, we design a Sparse Compression VAE which provides a high spatial compression
> rate and a compact latent space. We train large-scale flow-matching models comprising 4B parameters for 3D
> generation using diverse public 3D asset datasets. Despite their scale, inference remains highly efficient.
> Meanwhile, the geometry and material quality of our generated assets far exceed those of existing models.
> We believe our approach offers a significant advancement in 3D generative modeling.

## Efficiency claim (verbatim, introduction)

> it takes only ∼3s for 512³ fully-textured assets generation, ∼17s for the 1024³ resolution, and ∼60s for
> the 1536³ resolution on a NVIDIA H100 GPU

## Architecture and training (verbatim, §4 implementation details)

- "Our SC-VAE is trained using the Trellis-500K setup [65] after filtering out assets without PBR materials,
  resulting in a curated collection from Objaverse-XL, ABO, and HSSD."
- "The generative model is trained on an extended collection of about 800K assets, augmented with TexVerse to
  enrich PBR diversity and realism."
- "Each DiT in our framework contains approximately 1.3B parameters (width: 1536, blocks: 30, heads: 12, MLP
  width: 8192), trained on 32 H100 GPUs with a batch size of 256."
- "For image prompts, we render 16 views per asset in Blender with randomized FoVs and lighting." — training
  renders only; the released pipeline conditions on one user image.

## Limitations (Appendix F, verbatim, complete)

1. "similar to other voxel-based methods, O-Voxel's representation power is bounded by its spatial resolution.
   For detailed geometric features smaller than the voxel size, the Flexible Dual Grid formulation could
   produce aliasing artifacts." Two parallel surfaces inside one voxel produce a dual vertex "often resulting
   in a vertex located between them rather than accurately on one", and material attributes "will be an
   average of the properties of both surfaces, leading to blurred appearance."
2. "the reconstructed and generated results sometimes contain small holes, though they can mostly be
   rectified with standard mesh post-processing techniques (e.g., hole filling)."
3. "our O-Voxel is currently focused on geometry and material and it does not explicitly encode higher-level
   structural or semantic information" — part-level segmentation and topological graphs are named as future
   work, i.e. no part decomposition and no rigging.

## Gaps

- No wall-clock or cost comparison against hosted endpoints; all timings are H100/A100 lab figures.
- No polycount, triangle budget, UV-layout or file-size reporting for exported assets.
- No text-conditioned variant is evaluated; the paper's conditioning is images only.
- No ablation of the DINOv3 image conditioner, and no discussion of input-image preprocessing.
- The benchmark is vendor-run with the vendor's own harness; no independent reproduction was captured.

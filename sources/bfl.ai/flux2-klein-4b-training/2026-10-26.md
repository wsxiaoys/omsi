# BFL docs: FLUX.2 [klein] training guide (LoRA and full fine-tune on Base)

- Origin: Black Forest Labs documentation, "FLUX.2 [klein] Training",
  https://docs.bfl.ai/flux_2/flux2_klein_training
- Evidence kind: official documentation
- Publication date: not stated on page
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: FLUX.2 [klein] 4B Base, [klein] 9B Base

## Captured content

### Why Base for training (verbatim)

> FLUX.2 [klein] Base models are ideal for fine-tuning due to their undistilled architecture, which
> preserves the full training signal.

> Base models are undistilled and provide higher output diversity, making them ideal starting points
> for fine-tuning. The 4B variant is recommended for most users due to lower hardware requirements.

### Variant table (verbatim)

| Variant | Best For | License |
| --- | --- | --- |
| klein 4B Base | Quick iterations | Apache 2.0 |
| klein 9B Base | Maximum quality, complex concepts | FLUX Non-Commercial |

### System requirements (verbatim)

> **For klein 4B Base Training**
> * **GPU**: NVIDIA with 12GB VRAM (RTX 3060 12GB, RTX 4060 Ti 16GB)
> * **RAM**: 32GB system memory

> **For klein 9B Base Training**
> * **GPU**: NVIDIA with 22GB VRAM (RTX 3090, RTX 4090)
> * **RAM**: 64GB system memory

### Training guidance (verbatim, abridged)

> **LoRA Training**: 8e-5 to 1e-4; **Full Fine-tuning**: 1e-5 to 5e-5. Lower rates for style,
> higher for characters.

> **Training Steps** — Style LoRAs: 1500-2500 steps; Character LoRAs: 1500-3000 steps.

> * ✅ Lightweight (typically 10-200MB)
> * ✅ Fast training (1-3 hours on consumer GPUs)

### Serving a trained LoRA (verbatim, abridged)

> Upload your `.safetensors` in the Dashboard under Customization → Finetunes and call the
> fine-tuned endpoint with the resulting `finetune_id` — no local GPU required.

```python
response = requests.post(
    "https://api.bfl.ai/v1/flux-2-klein-9b-kv-finetuned",
    headers={"x-key": os.environ["BFL_API_KEY"], "Content-Type": "application/json"},
    json={"prompt": "a photo of ohwx in a garden on a sunny day",
          "finetune_id": "your-lora-id", "finetune_strength": 1.0},
)
```

## What this establishes

- The **Base** checkpoint is the documented training target; the distilled 4B is the inference
  target. That is the practical content of the base-vs-distilled split.
- Training the 4B Base needs a **12 GB** consumer GPU — a lower hardware floor than inference is
  usually quoted at, which is a reminder that the "~13 GB" and "8.4 GB" VRAM figures on other BFL
  pages are inference footprints, not training ones.
- Fine-tuned 4B models can be served first-party through a managed `-finetuned` endpoint (the doc's
  worked example uses the 9B KV endpoint, not the 4B).

## Gaps

- The page gives no guidance on how a LoRA trained on Base behaves when applied to the distilled 4B,
  or whether that transfer is supported. (The FLUX 3 Image Klein release note makes that claim
  explicitly for the FLUX 3 pair; this page does not make it for FLUX.2.)
- No statement of the 4B's parameter count, text encoder, or what the undistilled checkpoint's
  recommended step count/guidance is. The Diffusers snippet in the Hugging Face Base card is the
  nearest first-party signal.
- No cost figures for first-party training.

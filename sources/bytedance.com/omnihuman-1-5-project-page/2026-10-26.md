## Verbatim

> From a single image and a voice track, OmniHuman-1.5 generates expressive character animations that
> are coherent with the speech's rhythm, prosody and semantic content, with optional text prompts for
> further refinement. Inspired by the mind's "System 1 and System 2" cognitive theory, our
> architecture bridges a Multimodal Large Language Model and a Diffusion Transformer, simulating two
> distinct modes of thought: slow, deliberate planning and fast, intuitive reaction. This powerful
> synergy enables the generation of **videos over one minute** with highly dynamic motion, continuous
> camera movement, and complex multi-character interactions.

Section headings: "Rhythmic Performances", "Emotional Performances", "Context-Aware Audio-Driven
Animation", "Text-Guided Multimodal Animation", "Multi-Person Scene Performance", "More Results on
Diverse Inputs".

Multi-person claim, verbatim:

> Our framework extends to complex multi-person scenes. It generates dynamic group dialogues and
> ensemble performances by routing separate audio tracks to the correct characters in a single frame.

Capability disclaimer, verbatim:

> Note: The results shown were generated by our original research model. The video begins with the
> input image, and subtitles were added manually.

> The cases on this page are presented solely to showcase the capabilities of our research model.

The site's "More Research" navigation lists OmniHuman-1, InterActHuman, AlignHuman, Loopy and
CyberHost. The OmniHuman-1 page (`https://omnihuman-lab.github.io/`, separate site) describes the

a combination of audio and video", with no text-prompt condition. It also states, for OmniHuman-1
specifically:

> Currently, we do not offer services/downloads anywhere, nor do we have any SNS accounts for the
> project.

## Summary

This is the developers' own description of OmniHuman-1.5: an MLLM planner plus a DiT renderer, one
image plus one voice track plus an optional text prompt, output "over one minute", with multi-person
and non-human subjects in scope. It is a research-model page — the authors say the demos came from the
original research model — and it is still the "Tech report" link on BytePlus's OmniHuman 2.0 product
page.

## Gaps

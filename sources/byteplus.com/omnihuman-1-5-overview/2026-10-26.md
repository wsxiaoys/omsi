## Product statement (verbatim)

> OmniHuman 1.5 can generate a video based on a single image + audio uploaded by the user. It
> supports inputting images of any frame size (under 4096*4096) that contain people or other subjects
> (pets, animations, etc.), and combines them with audio to generate high-quality videos.

> The emotions and movements of the characters have a strong correlation with the audio. It supports
> adjusting the picture, movements, and camera movements through prompts. At the same time, OmniHuman
> 1.5 has good support for images such as animations and pets, allowing the designation of
> speakers/subjects, and can be widely used in scenarios such as singing, and performances.

> Compared with the previous generation model, OmniHuman 1.5 has significantly improved motion
> naturalness and structural stability, and is superior in the motion expressiveness of
> characters/subjects and picture quality.

> Note: The model's generation results have a certain degree of randomness, and not every generation
> effect may meet your expectations.

The five "Product Advantages" bullets are marketing claims with no numbers attached: "Rhythmic
Performance", "Emotional Performance", "Context Awareness", "Text-Guided Multimodal Performance",
"Single/Multi-Person Scene Performance", "Support for Diverse Subjects".

One operational caveat is stated in the prose rather than a limits table:

> When a character leaves the camera frame for a long time and then re-enters, consistency changes
> are likely to occur. When designing camera movement prompts, try to avoid situations where the
> character repeatedly leaves the camera frame.

## Input and output parameter table (verbatim)

| | Requirements & description |
| --- | --- |
| Image | "Format: Common formats such as JPG (JPEG), PNG, JFIF; JPG is recommended as the preferred format"; "Size: Less than 5 MB, resolution less than 4096*4096; the clearer the input image, the better the generation effect."; "Additional Recommendation: If a speaker needs to be specified, subject detection must be performed first; if not, subject detection can be skipped." |
| Audio | "Duration: The duration must be less than 60 seconds. If the audio exceeds 60 seconds, an error will be generated and the generation will fail; excessive duration may lead to degraded effect)" |
| Prompt (optional) | "Supported Languages: Only Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, Spanish (Mexican), Indonesian"; "Optional, used for precise control of the scene, movements, camera movements, etc." |
| Output video | "Format: MP4"; "Resolution: 720P/1080P (default)"; "Generation Time: The RTF value for 1080P video is 27, and RTF value for 720P video is 23. If there is service congestion, the generation time may increase." |

The RTF figures are quoted as written. An RTF (real-time factor) of 27 at 1080p implies roughly 27
seconds of generation per second of audio; the page does not define the term, and 720p being faster
(RTF 23) is consistent with fal's note that 720p is the faster setting. Treat the numbers as the
vendor's own and unverified.

No output frame rate is stated anywhere on this page.

## Service activation (verbatim)

> BytePlus users can access the [Vision AI] console via https://console.byteplus.com/ai
>
> Click [Model Plaza], locate the Omnihuman 1.0/1.5 service, and click on Omnihuman 1.0/1.5 to enter
> the activation process.
>
> Find [Omnihuman 1.0] [Omnihuman 1.5], click [Close Service] under the Actions column, and confirm to
> deactivate the service.

So OmniHuman 1.0 remains separately activatable on the same console even though the documentation
tree no longer gives 1.0 its own page (see the sibling section below).

## Summary

This is the primary first-party specification for OmniHuman 1.5 as BytePlus sells it: one image
(<5 MB, <4096×4096) plus one audio track (<60 s) plus an optional six-language prompt; MP4 output at
720p or 1080p, 1080p by default; USD 0.12 per second of generated video; concurrency 1; activated per
service in the Vision AI console.

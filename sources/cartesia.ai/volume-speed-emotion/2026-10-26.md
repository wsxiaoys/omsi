# Cartesia: Volume, Speed, and Emotion (generation_config and nonverbalisms)

- Origin: Cartesia, "Volume, Speed, and Emotion — Control the speed, volume, and emotion of generated
  speech", <https://docs.cartesia.ai/build-with-cartesia/capability-guides/volume-speed-emotion>
  (read as Markdown at `…/volume-speed-emotion.md`)
- Evidence kind: official documentation
- Publication date: not stated.
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: `sonic-3` and later, including `sonic-3.6`

## Captured content

> Sonic provides controls for the speed, volume, and emotion of generated speech. These are available
> on play.cartesia.ai using the UI controls, by passing a `generation_config` parameter on each request,
> or by using SSML tags within the transcript.

> `generation_config` is scoped to a single request — there is no persistent or account-wide setting.

> **Sonic interprets these parameters as guidance** rather than strict adjustments, to ensure natural
> speech. Test against your content to confirm the output matches your expectations.

- `generation_config.speed` — "ranging from `0.6` to `1.5` inclusive", default 1.0, "any
  double-precision floating-point value in that range".
- `generation_config.volume` — "ranging from `0.5` to `2.0` inclusive", default 1.0.
- `generation_config.emotion` — "**Emotion controls Beta** … Emotion tags are supported only for
  English." And: "Emotion tags push the model to be more emotive, but only work when the emotion is
  consistent with the transcript."

> Speed control applies to Instant Voice Clones. Professional Voice Clones take their pacing from their
> source recordings and don't support request-time speed adjustments.

### Nonverbalisms (verbatim, whole section)

> ## Nonverbalisms
>
> Insert `[laughter]` in your transcript to make the model laugh.

The guide also points at the "full list of emotive voices" in the Voice Library
(`play.cartesia.ai/voices?tags=Emotive`).

## Summary

`generation_config` gives per-request speed, volume and emotion, all explicitly "guidance". Emotion is
beta and English-only. The only documented non-verbal marker is `[laughter]`, a plain transcript token
rather than a tag.

This is the record that contradicts LiveKit's statement that "Cartesia doesn't produce non-verbal sounds
like laughs or sighs" (see `sources/livekit.io/cartesia-tts-inference/2026-10-26.md`). Cartesia
documents the capability; LiveKit says it is absent. Neither side shows an example or a test.

## Gaps

- No list of supported emotion values on this page (the SSML page shows `angry` and `sad`).
- No statement of how much `[laughter]` affects billing or whether it can be used mid-sentence.
- No measured effect sizes for speed/volume guidance.

- Publication date: page `lastModified` 2026-10-25T15:46:05Z

## Captured content

### What the page says H3 is (verbatim)

> MiniMax H3 is MiniMax's open-weights omni-modal model. It understands text, images, video, and audio
> in one context and generates video with native stereo audio (voice, SFX, and music in a single pass).
> Open weights target about 1 megapixel (768px short edge) at 24fps for up to ~15 seconds. 2K is a
> hosted-service output and needs a separate upscale pass in ComfyUI.

### Checkpoints and VRAM (verbatim table)

> Weights live on Hugging Face: Comfy-Org/MiniMax-H3.

| Build (fl2va / ref2va) | Size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pruned_w6a8 | 16 GB | Smallest; INT8 path; ComfyUI 0.38.0+ |
| pruned_int8_convrot | 21 GB | Default in templates |
| pruned_fp8_scaled | 21 GB | Same size as INT8 |
| pruned_bf16 | 40 GB | Unquantized pruned |
| bf16 | 66 GB | Full unquantized build |

Plus separate VAEs: "video fp16 / int8_convrot and audio fp32". Guidance: "If VRAM is tight, step down
a size: CPU offload costs more than precision tradeoffs. On RTX 30-series, INT8 matrix ops are ~2× FP16
and there is no FP8 path, so INT8 often beats FP8 at the same file size."

### Resolution (verbatim)

> For 16:9 native quality, set Megapixels to 0.98 or enter 1344 × 768 on the node. H3 is trained near
> 1 MP, with a 768-pixel short edge and up to a 768×1344 area. Sizes above 768×1344 render slower
> without adding detail, and quality drops the further you move from the trained resolution. For 2K,
> generate at native size and upscale separately.

The H3 nodes themselves "have no 768×1344 cap on output size: width and height step by 32 up to 16384
per axis", but requests beyond the trained area are not blocked — they just degrade.

### Clip length (verbatim table)

| Setting | Range |
| --- | --- |
| `length` input | 5–3600 frames (~0.2 s–150 s) |
| Trained range | ~124–362 frames (~5–15 s) |
| Frame grid | `17k+5` at 24 fps (124 frames ≈ 5.2 s) |
| Template default | 5 s → 124 frames |

> duration and length round up to the next 17k+5 value: a 5 s request renders 124 frames (5.17 s), and a
> length of 120 also renders 124. […] Long clips at full resolution exhaust VRAM first; lower megapixels
> to fit.

Beyond the trained range: "chain I2V continuations from each clip's last frame. Errors compound (faces
lose texture first); turbo/distilled schedules show burn sooner."

### Workflows

Six templates plus an I2V continuation variant: T2V, I2V (with first/last-frame control), R2V,
Multiframe Reference ("anchor reference frames at specific points along the output timeline"), Fun
ControlNet Union (Canny/Depth/HED/MLSD/Pose control video, and masked video inpainting), and the
FastH3 sparse-attention checkpoints. Default sampling is 20 steps without the Lightning LoRA, 8 with it
for T2V/I2V and 4 for R2V.

A documented integration pitfall (verbatim): "T2V/I2V templates sample inside a subgraph wired to Image
to Video only. A Reference to Video node on the canvas is ignored without error."

### Commercial licensing (verbatim)

> H3's open weights are free to download and run. Using locally generated outputs commercially requires
> a MiniMax commercial license, sold through Comfy, the only official reseller. Generations on Comfy
> Cloud already include commercial rights, so a license is only needed for local use.

> - Comfy Cloud: commercial use is included. Nothing to buy.
> - Your own hardware, outside the US, EU, UK, and Korea, under $20M in yearly revenue: the free MiniMax
>   community license covers commercial use. Show a "Powered by MiniMax H3" credit; the same territory
>   rule applies to where your outputs are used.
> - Your own hardware in the US, EU, UK, or Korea, or over $20M in yearly revenue: you need one of the

"Both tiers cover MiniMax H3 plus MiniMax Audio & Music under one agreement."

## Summary

This is the most detailed **independent** operational record captured, and it corroborates the model
card on the points that matter: 768px short edge / ~1 MP native, 24 fps, ~5–15 s trained range, native
stereo audio, and 2K as a hosted-only output that a local deployment must upscale to reach.

It adds three things no MiniMax document states:

1. **Concrete VRAM tiers** — a 16 GB INT8 pruned build up to a 66 GB full BF16 build, with separate
   video and audio VAEs, which makes local deployment a realistic single-workstation option.
2. **The frame grid** — requests round *up* to `17k+5` frames at 24 fps, so a "5 second" request is
   really 124 frames (5.17 s). No MiniMax document mentions quantisation of duration.
3. **An ecosystem around the weights** — community latent upscalers, anti-burn LoRAs, ControlNet-style
   control, and FastVideo sparse-attention checkpoints — which is evidence that the weights are
   practically usable, not merely published.

Its licensing section is also the only captured source that prices a commercial licence (Professional
from $5,000/month via Comfy as "the only official reseller") and that reconciles the community licence's
territory and revenue carve-outs into a decision rule.

## Gaps

- Comfy's claim to be "the only official reseller" is not corroborated by any MiniMax document
  captured here, and MiniMax's licence routes above-$20M requests to `api@minimax.io` rather than to a
  reseller.
- No throughput or wall-clock timings for any GPU, and no statement of minimum VRAM for the largest
  build beyond its file size.
- No quality comparison between local 768p output and the hosted 2K pipeline.
- The page describes Comfy-specific node behaviour that does not generalise to other stacks (SGLang,
  vLLM, diffusers).

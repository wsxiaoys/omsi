# Creatify: "Creatify Aurora is now on fal.ai"

- Origin: Creatify, "Creatify Aurora is now on fal.ai" (blog, Company News),
  <https://creatify.ai/blog/creatify-aurora-is-now-on-fal-ai>, written by Creatify Team
- Evidence kind: official announcement (vendor post about a third-party host, first-party)
- Publication date: **2026-02-09** (byline "Feb 9, 2026"; structured data `datePublished` 2026-02-09)
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: Aurora (the post does not name a version; fal's endpoint is the 480p/720p one)

## Verbatim (selected)

> Aurora is now available directly on fal.ai's platform.
> Developers can access the same Aurora model that powers video ads for Comcast, Alibaba, and thousands
> of brands on Creatify - but now through fal's inference API without running it through Creatify's
> interface.

> Until now, Aurora was only available through Creatify's platform, and recently via ElevenLabs.

> The integration is straightforward. You need three inputs: `image_url`, `audio_url`, `prompt` -
> optional visual direction (lighting, framing, background).

> Pricing is $0.10 per second at 480p or $0.14 per second at 720p. Seconds round up for billing.

> Aurora prompts work differently than text-to-image models. You're not describing what the avatar says -
> that comes from your audio file. Instead, prompts describe the visual context: lighting, framing,
> background, presentation style.

> fal published a comprehensive guide covering prompt architecture, common failures, and production-ready
> patterns.

## Summary

Creatify's own post confirms the fal endpoint as the same Aurora model, gives the input contract
(image + audio + optional visual prompt), and states a price of $0.10/s at 480p and $0.14/s at 720p.

**Contradiction with the host:** fal's live catalogue on 2026-10-26 prices the same endpoint at **$0.07**
per video second at 480p and $0.14 at 720p. The 720p figure agrees; the 480p figure does not. Either fal
cut the 480p price after February 2026 or Creatify's post misquoted it. No captured source explains the
difference; the host's own catalogue is the later statement.

The post also implies the fal endpoint exposes no `model_version` (it lists only `image_url`,
`audio_url`, `prompt`), so a fal caller cannot tell which Aurora tier it is buying from the vendor post
alone.

## Gaps

- The post does not name the model version hosted on fal, nor the maximum audio length, resolution set,
  or output frame rate.
- No date for when the fal integration went live beyond the post's own publication date.
- "Same Aurora model" is Creatify's claim; no captured source independently verifies that fal's endpoint
  runs the same weights as Creatify's API.

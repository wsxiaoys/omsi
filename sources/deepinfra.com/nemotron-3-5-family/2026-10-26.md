Super VL at $0.15/$0.50 is **identical** to OpenRouter's modal tier and to OpenRouter's own

cross-check each other. Lightning at $0.06/$0.16 is also identical to its OpenRouter

**DeepInfra caps both at 262,144 tokens.** Neither entry exposes the model card's 1M context.

## Bolt is absent

`nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-74B-A9B` and any Bolt E4B slug do **not** appear in the
catalogue at capture. Neither does a Super VL variant at 1M context. DeepInfra therefore does
not host the local/edge tier at all.

## Summary

DeepInfra provides an independent second price point that agrees exactly with OpenRouter's
DeepInfra route: Super VL $0.15/$0.50 per million tokens at 262,144 context, Lightning
$0.06/$0.16 at the same context. It corroborates the context finding — no DeepInfra route
offers more than 256K — and shows that the Bolt tier is not hosted at all. It does not
independently measure quality.

## Gaps

- Catalogue metadata only; no request was made and no latency, throughput or accuracy was
  observed.
- The catalogue does not state quantization per model.
- No Bolt entries, so DeepInfra cannot corroborate the Bolt cards.
- The `rate_per_input_token_cached` field is a multiplier, not a price; the conversion above
  assumes that reading, which DeepInfra's schema does not document.

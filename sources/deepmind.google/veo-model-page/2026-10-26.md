# Google DeepMind: Veo model page

- Origin: Google DeepMind, "Veo — Our leading video generation model",
  <https://deepmind.google/models/veo/>
- Evidence kind: official product page (first-party, marketing plus benchmark cards)
- Publication date: none stated for the page as a whole. The nine benchmark cards each carry "Last
  updated October 2025"; the page still features Veo 3.1 as the current model at capture.
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: Veo 3.1 (headline), Veo 3 (still described in the "What's new" copy), Veo 3.1
  Fast and Veo 3.1 Lite are **not** named anywhere on this page.

## Model identity as stated (verbatim)

> Veo
> Our leading video generation model

> Veo 3.1
> Video, meet audio. Our leading video generation model, designed to empower filmmakers and
> storytellers.

> Greater control, consistency, and creativity than ever before.

The page gives no model ID, no release date, no pricing, no duration or resolution table and no
distinction between Veo 3.1, Veo 3.1 Fast and Veo 3.1 Lite. It is a capability showcase, not a
specification.

## Capability sections (headings verbatim, with their copy)

> Add ingredients to your video
> Make sure videos align with your creative vision by giving Veo reference images of a scene, a
> character, or an object to guide its generation. Now includes audio.

> Match your style
> Capture your desired aesthetic by providing a style reference image, and Veo will generate videos
> with the same visual style, from paintings to cinematic looks.

> Keep your characters consistent
> Ensure characters maintain their appearance across different scenes in your videos by giving Veo
> reference images of your character.

> Extend your scene
> Extend clips into longer, more dynamic videos. Use the last second of your first shot to continue
> the story – while maintaining visual and audio consistency.

> Camera controls
> Precisely control the framing and exact movement of shots in your video using camera controls.

> First and last frame
> Create smooth, artful, and epic transitions between images provided for the first and last frame.

> Outpainting
> Go beyond the original frame. Outpainting expands your video with new, matching parts that look
> real, helping it fit any screen size or shape.

> Add object
> Reimagine videos by introducing new objects - from realistic details to fantastical elements. Veo
> considers scale, interactions, and shadows to create a natural, realistic-looking video.

> Remove object
> Seamlessly eliminate unwanted objects from videos - from distracting details to large items. Veo
> preserves the scene's natural composition, interactions, and shadows.

> Character controls
> Bring characters to life, using your body, face and voice to animate them.

> Motion controls
> Define the exact movement of objects in your video. Select an object and define their path, and Veo
> will bring them to life in motion.

The "Outpainting", "Add object", "Remove object", "Character controls" and "Motion controls"
sections have input/output thumbnail labels ("Input video", "Output video", "First frame", "Last
frame") that read like Google Flow application features rather than API parameters. The page does not
say which surface exposes which capability; see the Vertex AI records for what the API documents.

## Resolution claim (verbatim)

> Professional grade resolution
> Generate outputs in 1080p and 4K. 1080p resolution offers a sharper, cleaner video perfect for
> editing, while 4K captures rich textures and stunning clarity—ideal for high-end productions.

No frame rate, duration or aspect-ratio statement appears on the page. Duration is only visible

## Performance cards (verbatim, with the page's own method notes)

Nine cards, each ending "Last updated October 2025":

> Text-to-video — T2V Overall preference
> Participants viewed 1,003 prompts and respective videos on MovieGenBench, a benchmark dataset
> released by Meta. Veo 3.1 performs best on overall preference. Last updated October 2025.

> Text-to-video — T2V Text alignment
> … Veo 3.1 performs best on its capability to follow prompts accurately.

> Text-to-video — T2V Visual quality
> … Participants rate the visual quality of Veo's outputs more highly than other models.

> Image-to-video — I2V Overall preference
> When participants viewed 355 image and text pairs from the VBench I2V benchmark, Veo 3's outputs
> were preferred overall compared to other models.

> Image-to-video — I2V Text alignment
> When participants viewed 355 image and text pairs from the VBench I2V benchmark, Veo 3.1's outputs
> were preferred to other models for capturing the intent of the prompt.

> Text-to-video and audio — T2VA Audio visual overall preference
> Participants viewed 527 prompts from MovieGenBench, and had an overall preference for Veo's outputs
> with audio over other models.

> Text-to-video — T2V Visually realistic physics
> Participants choose Veo 3.1's outputs over other models for having visually realistic physics on
> the physics subset of MovieGenBench prompts.

Footnote [1], repeated under the capability cards:

> Human raters conducted direct side-by-side comparisons across 364 diverse examples … All
> comparisons were done at 1280x720 resolution. Veo videos are 8 seconds long. All other videos are
> 10 seconds long and shown at full length to raters.
> To ensure a fair visual comparison, all tests were conducted without sound. Audio was only enabled
> for the Overall Preference metric, and only when competing models had native sound support for the
> capability.

Also: "Note: We were unable to compare image to video with Sora 2 Pro because it currently does not
support realistic human images."

No numbers, margins, opponent list, rater count or confidence intervals are published — the cards are
images/charts without values in the captured text. "Performs best" is the vendor's summary of its own
internal head-to-head study; it is not an independent benchmark result.

## Safety and limitations (verbatim)

> It's crucial to introduce technologies such as Veo in a responsible way. To achieve this, videos
> made with Veo will be marked with SynthID, our advanced technology for watermarking and detecting
> content generated by AI. Additionally, Veo outputs will undergo safety evaluations and checks for
> memorized content to reduce potential issues related to privacy, copyright infringement, and bias.

> Limitations
> While Veo continues to make incredible strides in video generation, creating videos with natural and
> consistent spoken audio, particularly for shorter speech segments, remains an area of active
> development. We're continuously working to refine audio synchronization and eliminate instances of
> incoherent speech.

This is the vendor's own named weakness: short spoken segments and audio sync.

## Summary

First-party capability showcase for Veo 3.1, with no IDs, dates, prices or specifications. It
establishes what Google DeepMind says the Veo 3.1 generation can do (reference/ingredient images,
style and character consistency, scene extension, camera controls, first/last frame, outpainting,
object add/remove, character and motion controls, 1080p and 4K), that outputs carry SynthID, that the
vendor's own named weakness is short spoken audio, and that its benchmark cards are internal
human-preference studies last updated October 2025.

## Gaps

- No release date, model ID, pricing, region, duration, frame-rate or aspect-ratio information.
- No mention of Veo 3.1 Fast or Veo 3.1 Lite, so tier boundaries cannot be established here.
- Benchmark cards carry no numeric values, opponent list or methodology detail in the captured text.
- Unclear which capabilities are API features and which are Google Flow application features.

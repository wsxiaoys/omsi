# ElevenLabs docs: Text to Speech capabilities page (formats, languages, ownership FAQ)

- Origin: ElevenLabs, "Text to Speech", <https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech>
  (read as Markdown via `Accept: text/markdown`)
- Evidence kind: official documentation
- Publication date: not stated on the page (undated capability page)
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: all ElevenLabs TTS models; the model-specific sections used here are
  `eleven_multilingual_v2`, with `eleven_flash_v2_5` as the stated alternative

## Captured content

### Position of Multilingual v2 on the page (verbatim)

> For real-time applications, Flash v2.5 provides ultra-low 75ms latency, while Multilingual v2
> delivers the highest quality audio with more nuanced expression.

The page's model cards repeat the docs models-page copy, including for Multilingual v2: "Lifelike,
consistent quality speech synthesis model / Natural-sounding output / 29 languages supported /
10,000 character limit / Most stable on long-form generations".

### Supported output formats (verbatim)

> The default response format is `mp3`, but other formats like `pcm` and `ulaw` are available.

> * **MP3** — Sample rates: 22.05kHz - 44.1kHz; Bitrates: 32kbps - 192kbps; 22.05kHz @ 32kbps;
>   44.1kHz @ 32kbps, 64kbps, 96kbps, 128kbps, 192kbps
> * **PCM (S16LE)** — Sample rates: 16kHz - 44.1kHz; Bitrates: 8kHz, 16kHz, 22.05kHz, 24kHz, 44.1kHz,
>   48kHz; 16-bit depth
> * **μ-law** — 8kHz sample rate; Optimized for telephony applications
> * **A-law** — 8kHz sample rate; Optimized for telephony applications
> * **Opus** — Sample rate: 48kHz; Bitrates: 32kbps - 192kbps

> Higher quality audio options are only available on paid tiers - see our pricing page for details.

### Supported languages (verbatim)

> Our multilingual v2 models support 29 languages:
>
> English (USA, UK, Australia, Canada), Japanese, Chinese, German, Hindi, French (France, Canada),
> Korean, Portuguese (Brazil, Portugal), Italian, Spanish (Spain, Mexico), Indonesian, Dutch,
> Turkish, Filipino, Polish, Swedish, Bulgarian, Romanian, Arabic (Saudi Arabia, UAE), Czech, Greek,
> Finnish, Croatian, Malay, Slovak, Danish, Tamil, Ukrainian & Russian.

> Simply input text in any of our supported languages and select a matching voice from our voice
> library. For the most natural results, choose a voice with an accent that matches your target
> language and region.

### Prompting behaviour (verbatim)

> The models interpret emotional context directly from the text input. For example, adding
> descriptive text like "she said excitedly" or using exclamation marks will influence the speech
> emotion. Voice settings like Stability and Similarity help control the consistency, while the
> underlying emotion comes from textual cues.

> Descriptive text will be spoken out by the model and must be manually trimmed or removed from the
> audio if desired.

### FAQ entries relevant to licensing and billing (verbatim)

> #### Can I clone my own voice?
> Yes, you can create instant voice clones of your own voice from short audio clips. For
> high-fidelity clones, check out our professional voice cloning feature.

> #### Do I own the audio output?
> Yes. You retain ownership of any audio you generate. However, commercial usage rights are only
> available with paid plans. With a paid subscription, you may use generated audio for commercial
> purposes and monetize the outputs if you own the IP rights to the input content.

> #### What qualifies as a free regeneration?
> A free regeneration allows you to regenerate the same text to speech content without additional
> cost, subject to these conditions: You can regenerate each piece of content up to 2 times for free;
> the content must be exactly the same as the previous generation. Any changes to the text, voice
> settings, or other parameters will require a new, paid generation.

> According to ElevenLabs' internal benchmarks, regenerations will solve roughly half of issues with
> quality, with remaining issues usually due to poor training data.

### Determinism (verbatim, from "Key facts" and FAQ)

> **Determinism**: Output is nondeterministic — use the `seed` parameter for more consistent results

> The models are nondeterministic. For consistency, use the optional seed parameter, though subtle
> differences may still occur.

## What this establishes

- The vendor's own capability page calls Multilingual v2 "the highest quality audio" on the same
  site where the models page calls Eleven v4 "our state of the art speech synthesis model, delivering
  our highest quality audio". The two claims are unreconciled; the capabilities sentence reads as
  stale copy left from the v2/v3 era (see the models overview record).
- Format coverage for the shared TTS endpoint is broad — MP3, PCM (S16LE), μ-law, A-law, Opus, up to
  48 kHz for Opus and 44.1 kHz for MP3/PCM — but the page states higher-quality options are
  paid-tier only, and the API reference (captured separately) names the exact gates.
- Commercial terms for output are stated as: ownership retained, commercial use requires a paid
  plan. This is model-agnostic and therefore applies to `eleven_multilingual_v2`.
- Voice cloning is confirmed available (instant and professional) but the page gives no per-model
  compatibility statement; that comes from the professional-voice-cloning FAQ captured separately.

## Gaps

- The page gives no per-model latency number for Multilingual v2 (only "higher latency" than Flash on
  the models page) and no sample-rate limit specific to v2 — the format table is presented as
  platform-wide.
- No statement about watermarking, regional residency, or concurrency on this page.
- The "29 languages" list is prose with regional variants (English USA/UK/Australia/Canada, Spanish
  Spain/Mexico, French France/Canada, Portuguese Brazil/Portugal, Arabic Saudi Arabia/UAE) collapsed
  into single entries, so the count is of language codes, not of locales.

  via `Accept: text/markdown`)
- Evidence kind: official documentation (prompting guide)
- Publication date: not stated on page.
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: `eleven_v3`, `eleven_v4`, `eleven_multilingual_v2`

## Captured content

### Pauses / SSML (verbatim)

> Eleven v4 and Eleven v3 do not support SSML break tags. Use the techniques described in the
> Prompting Eleven v4 section for controlling pauses.

> Use `<break time="x.xs" />` for natural pauses up to 3 seconds.

> Using too many break tags in a single generation can cause instability. The AI might speed up, or
> introduce additional noises or audio artifacts. We are working on resolving this.

### The v4 section's scope note (verbatim)

> This section is written for Eleven v4. Many of the techniques below also apply to Eleven v3. In
> general, Eleven v4 is a net upgrade over Eleven v3, delivering better results in almost every case.
> We strongly recommend switching to v4 and testing it with your own voices and content to see the
> difference for yourself. While a few edge cases may call for a different fit, most users should
> find v4 the better choice.

### Audio tags — what they are (verbatim)

> Audio tags (e.g. `[whispering]`, `[shouting]`, `[laughing]`) let you direct delivery with
> fine-grained control, and Eleven v4 handles them with a level of nuance beyond previous models.
> They're not perfect yet, and we're continuing to iterate and improve how reliably the model follows
> tag instructions

> Because Eleven v4 is trained to generate both vocal delivery styles and sound effects, a tag can
> occasionally be interpreted as a request for a sound effect rather than a delivery instruction (or
> vice versa).

> Tags land more readily when the delivery is already in the voice's training data. Eleven v4 can
> still follow a tag the voice was not trained on, such as `[whispering]` or `[shouting]`, though the
> result may not be optimal.

### Tag vocabulary as listed (verbatim)

Voice-related:

> * `[laughs]`, `[laughs harder]`, `[starts laughing]`, `[wheezing]`
> * `[whispers]`
> * `[sighs]`, `[exhales]`
> * `[sarcastic]`, `[curious]`, `[excited]`, `[crying]`, `[snorts]`, `[mischievously]`

Sound effects:

> * `[gunshot]`, `[applause]`, `[clapping]`, `[explosion]`
> * `[swallows]`, `[gulps]`

Experimental ("Unique and special"):

> * `[strong X accent]` (replace X with desired accent)
> * `[sings]`, `[woo]`, `[fart]`

> Some experimental tags may be less consistent across different voices. Test thoroughly before
> production use.

### Punctuation (verbatim, stated for v4)

> Punctuation significantly affects delivery in v4:
>
> * Ellipses (...) add pauses and weight
> * Capitalization increases emphasis
> * Standard punctuation provides natural speech rhythm

### The dedicated "Prompting Eleven v3" section (verbatim, in full)

> The prompting techniques in Prompting Eleven v4 also apply to Eleven v3, including voice selection,
> audio tags, punctuation, and multi-speaker dialogue.
>
> Professional Voice Clones (PVCs) are not fully optimized for Eleven v3, resulting in potentially
> lower clone quality compared to earlier models. PVCs are supported in v4, so if you'd like to use a
> Professional Voice Clone or a voice from the Voice Library, we recommend trying Eleven v4 instead.

### Other v3-relevant statements

> We are actively working on *Director's Mode* to give you even greater control over outputs.

The speed setting section describes `speed` as "available in Text to Speech via the website and API,
as well as in ElevenCreative Studio and Agents Platform" without excluding v3; the same page says
Multilingual v2 "doesn't support phoneme tags".

## What this establishes

- **v3 has no prompting documentation of its own any more.** Its section is four sentences that
  delegate to the v4 guide and add one warning: PVCs are "not fully optimized" for v3, with
  "potentially lower clone quality compared to earlier models".
- The vendor's own comparison in this document is directional and unquantified — "a net upgrade over
  Eleven v3, delivering better results in almost every case" — with no metric, test set or date.
- **SSML break tags are unsupported on v3**, so pause control has to go through punctuation, audio
  tags and text structure. The `<break>` guidance that remains on the page applies to the older
  models, not v3.
- The tag vocabulary here is documented under the v4 heading but the page states the techniques apply
  to v3; the help-centre page goes further and says "the same tags work with both models"
  (`../eleven-v3-audio-tags/2026-10-26.md`). Tag *reliability* is only claimed for v4 ("beyond
  previous models"), which implies v3 is the weaker of the two on tag adherence without saying by how
  much.

## Gaps

- No v3-specific tag reliability statement, no v3-specific punctuation guidance, and no v3-specific
  stability advice — the page never says what stability value or range suits v3.
- No statement about whether `speed` and `style` are honoured by v3 (the v4 guide excludes them for
  v4; this page is silent for v3).
- No date, so it cannot be established whether this v3 section predates or postdates the 2026-02-02
  GA.

# ElevenLabs: Eleven v4 product landing page (marketing claims)

- Origin: ElevenLabs, "Eleven v4 and Eleven v4 Turbo Text to Speech models",
  <https://elevenlabs.io/v4> (read as Markdown via `Accept: text/markdown`)
- Evidence kind: official marketing page (vendor claims, no methodology)
- Publication date: not stated; page promotes the 2026-09-28 launch. Capture date 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: `eleven_v4`, `eleven_v4_turbo`

## Captured content (verbatim claims)

### v4 positioning

> Eleven v4 is built on an entirely new architecture that reads a script the way a voice actor would.
> It knows who's speaking, what just happened, and how every line should land.

> Speech in 90+ languages across an exceptional emotional range, with multiple speakers and sound
> effects built-in.

Section headings and their claims:

- "Write direction into the script" — "Eleven v4 follows tag sequences more reliably than v3, sound
  effects included."
- "Stitch long-form audio without the seams" — "Context stitching keeps pacing and delivery steady
  across a script of any length. A full audiobook sounds like a single take from the first page to
  the last."
- "Regenerate without vocal drift" — "Redo a line once or fifty times and it's still the same person
  speaking."
- "Cast a Professional Voice Clone" — "Professional Voice Clones weren't supported in v3. In Eleven
  v4, they're back and perform with the model's full emotional range across every language they
  speak."

### v4 Turbo positioning

> Our fastest real-time speech model with the expressive range of Eleven v4, available through the
> API and ElevenAgents. Eleven v4 Turbo has a median inference latency of ~100 ms, time to first
> speech of ~150 ms, and stays consistent in long interactions.

Vendor-run latency comparison, presented as a chart with the caption "Median time to first speech":

| Model | Value shown |
| --- | --- |
| Eleven v4 Turbo | 150 ms (reference) |
| Cartesia Sonic 3.6 | 262 ms ("+112 ms") |
| OpenAI GPT-4o mini TTS | 814 ms ("+664 ms") |

Other Turbo claims: "Stream in, stream out" (bidirectional streaming), "One voice across every
turn" (PVCs "work identically across both models"), "Speak the language like a local" ("Point it at
Japanese, Spanish or Portuguese text and the voice speaks it fluently, with a native accent").

### Customer quotes on the page

Attributed quotes from Salesforce (Ryan Peterson, SVP Product for Agentforce Voice), BeyondWords
(Patrick O'Flaherty), Rosebud (Chrys Bader) and Accenture Accelerate (Kyle Gudmundson). These are
vendor-selected testimonials, not measurements.

### Voice inventory claim

> Cast from 17,500+ voices

The Text to Dialogue docs say "10,000+ community-shared voices" for the Voice Library
(`../eleven-v4-text-to-dialogue/2026-10-26.md`). The two numbers describe different inventories
(total voices available vs community library) but neither page defines its count.

## What this establishes

- The vendor's headline v4 claims are: new architecture, better audio-tag following than v3,
  long-form stitching, speaker stability across regenerations, PVC support, 90+ languages.
- **The only numeric performance claim in the corpus is the latency chart**, and it is a
  vendor-run comparison against Cartesia Sonic 3.6 and OpenAI GPT-4o mini TTS with no methodology,
  sample, hardware or date attached.
- No quality benchmark, WER, MOS or preference-test result appears anywhere on the page.

## Gaps

- No methodology for the latency chart: no region, network conditions, text set, or measurement date.
- "New architecture" is unquantified; no parameter count, no paper, no model card.
- No pricing, licensing or watermark statement on the landing page.

## Captured content (verbatim, selected)

### Positioning

> Eleven v4 is our latest and most advanced Text to Speech model, and the first in a new generation
> of models. It improves output quality, voice accuracy, consistency, emotion, delivery, audio tags,
> and language coverage compared with Eleven v3.

> In general, Eleven v4 is a net upgrade over Eleven v3, delivering better results in almost every
> case. We strongly recommend switching to v4 and testing it with your own voices and content to see
> the difference for yourself.

### Voice cloning

> Voice cloning accuracy takes a major step forward with Eleven v4. Cloned voices capture the source
> voice's characteristics — timbre, cadence, and delivery — more faithfully than any previous model.

> Instant Voice Clones (IVCs) are more accurate than ever in Eleven v4.

> Professional Voice Clones (PVCs) are fully supported in Eleven v4.

> Because of this significant leap in accuracy, Eleven v4 may sound substantially different from
> Eleven v3. […] you may still prefer how a voice sounded in Eleven v3.

Training guidance on the page: "we recommend sticking to a single speaking style in your training
audio, which Eleven v4 should capture better than earlier models"; and "the quality of the source
recording matters more than ever".

The FAQ adds that Instant Voice Cloning needs "a short sample, generally one to two minutes, and
within seconds you have a voice you can use", and that an existing Professional Voice Clone is
re-trained for v4 from My Voices ("Click the plus button next to Eleven v4 to start fine-tuning").

### Native accent handling (a behavioural change, not a bug)

> When the language you're generating matches the language of your reference voice, the original
> accent is preserved exactly as before.

> When the language you're generating is different from the reference voice's language, Eleven v4
> generates fluent, natural-sounding speech in the target language — rather than carrying over the
> reference voice's accent from its original language.

> if you clone a voice from a Korean speaker and generate English, Eleven v4 will produce natural,
> fluent English rather than English spoken with a Korean accent.

> this new functionality is still being fine-tuned, and we are exploring ways to make it a toggle
> instead of having it always on. That is still a research project, so we do not have a timeline.

### Variants and settings

> * **Eleven v4** (`eleven_v4`) — our highest-quality model, ideal for content creation, audiobooks,
>   character voiceovers, and any use case where quality is the top priority.
> * **Eleven v4 Turbo** (`eleven_v4_turbo`) — extremely high quality while retaining impressively
>   low latency, purpose-built for real-time use cases like conversational agents and interactive
>   voice experiences.

> Both variants use two voice settings: Stability and Similarity.

> When you select a Professional Voice Clone and Eleven v4, Voice settings include an additional
> **Clone Strength** slider. […] For most voices, start at 0%.

> Style and Speed sliders are not available in Eleven v4, and SSML is not supported.

Audio tags: "Audio tags (e.g. `[whispering]`, `[shouting]`, `[laughing]`) let you direct delivery with
fine-grained control […] They're not perfect yet".

### Language FAQ

The FAQ language answer lists Amharic, Fula (Pulaar), Lao, Luganda, Shona, Sorani Kurdish and Wolof,
which are **absent** from the Models-page enumeration, and omits Hausa's neighbours such as Chichewa.
Both lists are first-party and do not agree in detail. The FAQ also qualifies: "Some languages are
handled more fluently than others, but in general Eleven v4 handles the large majority of these very
well."

### Lifecycle warning

> Eleven v4 is under active, continuous development. As we keep training and improving the model
> after launch, its behavior may shift over time as quality improves.

This is a statement that `eleven_v4` is a **moving target**, not a frozen snapshot.

### Showcase caveat (verbatim)

> These examples are raw. Nothing has been heavily post-processed: no EQ, compression, normalization,
> de-essing, plosive removal, or similar. If you hear any recurring issues, such as clipping,
> plosives, uneven EQ, or jumps in volume, they are very likely in the training data for that voice.

## What this establishes

- Voice cloning (both Instant and Professional) is a first-party documented v4 capability, with the
  vendor claiming improved accuracy over v3 and a new PVC-only `clone_strength` control.
- Cross-language generation deliberately drops the source accent by default, with no toggle yet.
  That is a behaviour change users migrating from v3 must test.
- Style, Speed and SSML are explicitly unsupported on v4; Stability and Similarity are the only
  general voice settings, plus audio tags and IPA.
- The vendor says the model will keep changing under the same ID.

## Model family as documented

> The ElevenLabs Speech to Text (STT) API turns spoken audio into text with state of the art
> accuracy. Our Scribe v2 model adapts to textual cues across 90+ languages and multiple voice
> styles.

Three entries are listed with feature bullets:

- **Scribe v2** — "State-of-the-art speech recognition model"; "Accurate transcription in 90+
  languages"; "Keyterm prompting, up to 1000 terms"; "Entity detection, 65 entity types"; "Transcript
  editing with natural-language instructions"; "Precise word-level timestamps"; "Speaker diarization,
  up to 32 speakers"; "Dynamic audio tagging"; "Smart language detection".
- **Scribe v2 Realtime** — realtime, "Low latency (~150ms†)"; same 65 entity types, entity detection,
  transcript editing, word-level timestamps; no diarization bullet.
- **Scribe v2 Medical** — "Speech recognition fine-tuned for clinical audio"; "35% fewer
  transcription errors on clinical audio than Scribe v2"; "Same accuracy on everyday speech as
  Scribe v2"; "Same features, languages, pricing, and API as Scribe v2".

## Output shape

The documented example response for `scribe_v2` returns `language_code` (`"en"`), `language_probability`
(`1`), `text`, and a `words` array. Word objects carry `text`, `start`, `end`, `type` and
`speaker_id`. Types are documented as:

> word - A word in the language of the audio
> spacing - The space between words, not applicable for languages that don't use spaces like
> Japanese, Mandarin, Thai, Lao, Burmese and Cantonese
> audio_event - Non-speech sounds like laughter or applause

## Throughput / concurrency

> For Speech to Text, files that are over 8 minutes long are transcribed in parallel internally in
> order to speed up processing. The audio is chunked into four segments to be transcribed
> concurrently.

`Concurrency = min(4, round_up(audio_duration_secs / 480))`, "applicable to Scribe v2 and Scribe v2
Medical" (a 15-minute file uses concurrency 2; a 120-minute file uses 4).

## Advanced features

Keyterm prompting (available on v2, v2 Medical batch, v2 Realtime):

> Batch supports up to 1000 keyterms (50 characters each), while realtime supports up to 50 keyterms
> (20 characters each).

Verbatim mode — the page is internally contradictory here and both sentences are preserved:

> No verbatim mode is available with Scribe v2, Scribe v2 Medical (batch), and Scribe v2 Realtime.
> When no_verbatim is enabled, the model removes filler words, false starts and disfluencies from the
> transcript.

Entity detection ("available with all batch models and Scribe v2 Realtime") returns entities "with
their exact timestamps". Transcript editing is "an experimental feature available with all batch
models and Scribe v2 Realtime", driven by a natural-language `transcript_edit` instruction, returned
in `edited_transcript` "so timestamps and speaker labels stay intact".

Both keyterm prompting and transcript editing "come at an additional cost".

Internal inconsistencies in this page:

- The WER bands cover 94 codes while the language list has 100. **Eight listed languages have no
  band**: Cebuano (`ceb`), Chichewa (`nya`), Fulah (`ful`), Kabuverdianu (`kea`), Lingala (`lin`), Luo

  (Yoruba, banded "Moderate" but absent from the list).
- The bands have no bucket between >20% and >25%.

## Input limits and formats (FAQ + "Key facts")

- "Files up to 3 GB in size are supported." Duration "Up to 10 hours" in standard mode; in
  multi-channel mode "the combined duration of all channels must be less than 10 hours".
- **Contradiction:** the same page's "Key facts" block says "Maximum duration: 10 hours (standard
  mode), **1 hour** (multichannel mode)".
- Both audio *and* video files are accepted.
- Audio MIME types listed: `audio/aac`, `audio/x-aac`, `audio/x-aiff`, `audio/ogg`, `audio/mpeg`,
  `audio/mp3`, `audio/mpeg3`, `audio/x-mpeg-3`, `audio/opus`, `audio/wav`, `audio/x-wav`,
  `audio/webm`, `audio/flac`, `audio/x-flac`, `audio/mp4`, `audio/aiff`, `audio/x-m4a`.
- Video MIME types listed: `video/mp4`, `video/x-msvideo`, `video/x-matroska`, `video/quicktime`,
  `video/x-ms-wmv`, `video/x-flv`, `video/webm`, `video/mpeg`, `video/3gpp`.
- Multichannel mode: "up to 5 channels", each "processed independently and assigned a speaker ID
  based on its channel number".
- Webhooks supported for asynchronous results.

Billing basis stated on the page:

> ElevenLabs charges for speech to text based on the duration of the audio sent for transcription.
> […] Silence counts toward the billed duration. Both batch and realtime transcription are metered on
> every second of audio you send, including pauses and silent stretches, and there is no discount for
> silence.

HIPAA: "Companies requiring HIPAA compliance must contact ElevenLabs Sales to sign a Business
Associate Agreement (BAA)".

## Summary

The primary first-party capability record for `scribe_v2`. It establishes: 90+ languages (100 codes
enumerated), diarization to 32 speakers, word- and character-level timestamps, dynamic audio-event
tagging, keyterms (1000 batch / 50 realtime), entity detection (65 types), experimental transcript
editing, 3 GB file cap, 10-hour standard duration, audio *and* video input, and per-second billed
duration with silence billed.

It is also the source of three unresolved internal contradictions: 1 hour vs 10 hours for
multichannel duration; the sentence 'No verbatim mode is available with Scribe v2' immediately
followed by a description of what `no_verbatim` does; and `zho` vs `cmn` for Mandarin.

## Gaps

- No release date and no statement of whether the model is GA, preview or deprecated.
- No independent measurement; every accuracy figure here is vendor-reported and the WER bands name no

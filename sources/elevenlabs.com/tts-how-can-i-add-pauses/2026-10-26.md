## Captured content (verbatim)

> There are a few ways to introduce a pause or break and influence the rhythm and cadence of the
> speaker. The method you use depends on the model.
>
> ## Audio tags (Eleven v4 and Eleven v3)
>
> With Eleven v4 and Eleven v3, use audio tags and punctuation to control pacing and delivery. These
> models do not support SSML break tags.
>
> ## Break tags (Multilingual v2, Flash v2, and Flash v2.5)
>
> The most consistent way to add a pause on Multilingual v2, Flash v2, and Flash v2.5 is with the SSML
> break tag syntax `<break time="1.5s" />`. This creates an exact and natural pause in the speech. It
> is not just inserted silence between words—the model understands the syntax and adds a natural pause.
>
> Break time should be described in seconds. The AI can handle pauses of up to 3 seconds and can be
> used in Text to Speech and via the API.

## What this establishes — and a contradiction to preserve

This page's heading enumerates break-tag support for "**Multilingual v2, Flash v2, and Flash v2.5**"
and **omits Turbo v2.5**. The companion page (`../tts-ssml-break-tags-model-coverage/2026-10-26.md`)
says *all* models except v3 and v4 support break tags, which includes Turbo v2.5.

Both are first-party and neither references the other. Two readings are possible and the corpus does
not settle them: (a) Turbo v2.5 does support break tags and this page simply lists only the current
models, or (b) break-tag support was withdrawn from Turbo v2.5 when it moved to "Older Models". No
source states (b), and no test was run, so this page does not claim either.

## Gaps

- No per-model table; support is asserted in prose headings that disagree with the companion page.
- No statement about whether the omitted models reject `<break>` syntax silently or with an error.

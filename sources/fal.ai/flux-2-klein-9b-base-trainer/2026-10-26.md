## What this establishes

- **The roster's discovery label is a training endpoint.** Anyone who reads
  `fal-ai/flux-2-klein-9b-base-trainer` as "the model" is looking at LoRA *training*, priced
  per step, whose output must then be served on `fal-ai/flux-2/klein/9b/base/lora`
  (`kind: inference`). The inference endpoints for the model itself are
  `fal-ai/flux-2/klein/9b` and `fal-ai/flux-2/klein/9b/edit`.
- **The trainer targets the Base checkpoint**, consistent with BFL's own guidance that Base
  models are the fine-tuning targets (`../../bfl.ai/flux2-klein-9b-training/2026-10-26.md`).
  fal's catalogue does not expose a Base *inference* endpoint other than the LoRA one.
- **fal created both klein trainers on 2026-01-17**, two days after the model launch — i.e.
  LoRA training on fal was available from launch week, ahead of BFL's own managed LoRA beta
  (2026-04-23).
- **The 4B trainer is deprecated while the 9B trainer is active** (4B `updated_at`
  2026-05-26). fal does not state why. It means the 4B row in the roster points at a
  deprecated training endpoint, and that asymmetry should not be read as a quality judgement
  about the models.
- **Training price differs by an order of magnitude from fal's own marketing page.** This page
  states `$0.0043 × steps`; the fal FLUX.2 landing-page FAQ captured separately says "Training
  costs $0.008 per step"
  (see [fal's FLUX.2 landing page](../../fal.ai/flux-2/2026-10-26.md)). The landing page may describe a different training
  product or be stale. Unresolved; the per-endpoint formula is the more specific statement.

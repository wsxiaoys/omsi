## Captured content — request schema (queue OpenAPI)

`Flux3TextToImageInput`: `prompt` (required), `aspect_ratio`, `resolution`, `output_format`,
`enable_prompt_expansion`, `safety_tolerance`, `sync_mode`, `version`. There is **no** `seed`,
`width`/`height`, `grounding`, or bounding-box field.

- `resolution` enum: `768sq`, `1k`, `1.5k`, `2k`, `4k` — default `1k`. Description: "Output
  resolution tier. 4k can take several minutes."
- `aspect_ratio` enum: `auto`, `21:9`, `2:1`, `16:9`, `3:2`, `7:5`, `4:3`, `5:4`, `1:1`, `4:5`,
  `3:4`, `5:7`, `2:3`, `9:16`, `1:2` — **no `9:21`**, which BFL's schema accepts.
- `output_format`: `jpeg` (default), `png`.
- `safety_tolerance`: integer, minimum 0, maximum 4, default 2.
- `version`: const `latest`.

`Flux3EditImageInput` adds `image_urls` (required, `minItems: 1`, `maxItems: 10`) with the verbatim
description: "Reference image URLs or data URIs, in image 1 through image 10 order. Each must be at
least 256 pixels per dimension. Oversized images are automatically downscaled to at most 4
megapixels while preserving aspect ratio. The first controls auto aspect ratio."

## What this establishes

- fal carries FLUX 3 Image as a **proxied partner endpoint** (not fal-hosted weights), published on
  the same day as BFL's launch, in a group keyed `flux-3-image`.
- fal's control surface differs from BFL's: no `grounding`, no `width`/`height`, no `match_input`,
  safety capped at 4 instead of 7, and one extra knob (`enable_prompt_expansion`, default off).
  **A fal caller cannot turn grounding off or on — it is not exposed — and cannot request the
  `9:21` ratio.**
- Input reference images are downscaled to 4 MP on fal, whereas BFL accepts up to 16 MP per
  reference (rejecting larger ones). Same model, different effective input resolution.
- The 512sq tier appears in fal's prose but not in its schema; the schema is the safer description
  of what a caller can send.

## Known gaps

- fal states a billing unit of megapixels at $0.048 with no tier table, while BFL prices per image
  by tier and charges $0.607 at 4K. If fal's unit is linear, a 4K image on fal would cost ~$0.77;
  that is not stated anywhere and was not tested. The "one flat price per image, set by resolution"
  sentence points the other way. Unresolved.
- fal does not document which BFL revision it proxies (`version` is `latest`).
- fal's "Commercial use" badge is fal's statement about use through fal; it is not a BFL weights
  licence (there are no public FLUX 3 Image weights).
- No latency, throughput, or quality numbers.

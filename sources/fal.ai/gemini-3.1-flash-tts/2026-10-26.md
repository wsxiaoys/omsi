About section (verbatim): *"Generate speech from text using Google's Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS model.
Next-generation model with improved speech quality, expressive audio tags (e.g. [sigh], [laughing],

endpoint), `google/gemini-3.8-flash-tts` and `google/gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts` (both `date`
2026-09-25). The 3.1 endpoint stays listed alongside its successor, and fal's slug convention moved
from `fal-ai/...` to `google/...` between them.

## Output

`audio` (`File`, required) with `url`, `content_type`, `file_name`, `file_size`. The default sample
response returns `gemini_31_flash_tts_output.mp3` with `content_type: "audio/mpeg"`.

## Pricing

`pricingInfoOverride` (verbatim): "Your request will cost **$0.05 per 1000 character**." That is
**$50 per 1M characters**, billed on input characters, with no documented character cap in the
schema.

## Summary

fal serves the 3.1 Flash TTS model as a serverless partner endpoint at **$0.05 per 1,000 input
characters**, with 30 preset voices, a 2-speaker mode, an 89-value locale enum, a
`style_instructions` field, `temperature`, and `mp3`/`wav`/`ogg_opus` output (24 kHz 16-bit mono when
WAV). The endpoint is `active` and undeprecated on fal as of capture, five weeks before the vendor's
scheduled shutdown.

## Gaps

- No input length limit is published; `prompt` carries no `maxLength`.
- No statement of which upstream snapshot is served, or of what fal does when Google shuts the model
  down on 2026-11-17.
- fal does not mention the vendor's preview status, so its "active/commercial" labels cannot be read
  as a support commitment.
- The "over 80 languages" line on the page appears inside a sample podcast script used as the
  playground example, not in fal's own prose; only the enum is authoritative.

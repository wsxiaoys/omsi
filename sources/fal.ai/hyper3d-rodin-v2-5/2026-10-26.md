## Host/vendor divergences worth noting

1. **`Gen-2.5-Minimum` is a fal-only tier.** Hyper3D's documented enum has five tiers and no "Minimum".
2. **fal drops `Hybrid` material and the `extreme-high` texture mode**, and caps
   `quality_mesh_option` at "Up to 1M Triangle" in prose while its own enum lists `2M Triangle`.
3. **Defaults differ.** Vendor: `material=PBR`, `tier` must be sent explicitly (omitting it falls back
   to Gen-1/1.5). fal: `material=All`, `tier=Gen-2.5-High` by default. A caller who sends nothing gets a
   different model and a different material bundle on each route.
4. **Price basis differs.** fal bills a flat $0.40 per generation regardless of tier (and $0.10 on
   fast), where the vendor bills 0.5–1.0 credits plus 2.0 for `extreme-high` textures. fal's flat price
   means the vendor's most expensive tier is not priced proportionally on fal.
5. **`hostingType: "proxy"`** — fal forwards to Hyper3D rather than running the model itself.

## Gaps

- fal does not say which Hyper3D checkpoint it proxies, or whether the flat $0.40 covers
  `Gen-2.5-Extreme-High` and 12K textures without a surcharge. The `pricingInfoOverride` string is
  tier-agnostic.
- `Gen-2.5-Minimum` has no vendor-side documentation; its face count is only hinted at in a truncated
  description ("50K Triangle").
- No latency, queue, or concurrency data captured.

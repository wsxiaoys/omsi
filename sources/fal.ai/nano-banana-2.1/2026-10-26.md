### Overview (verbatim, abridged)

> `google/nano-banana-2.1` exposes Nano Banana 2.1 as a text-to-image endpoint. [...] In Google's
> words, Nano Banana 2.1 "outperforms our previous models across the board, with notable leaps in
> visual design, mask-based editing, and subject consistency to help you create more natural-
> looking images." An update to Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image), it keeps Flash-level speed
> and cost efficiency [...] Google positions it as the more efficient counterpart to Nano Banana
> Pro.

> This is the same underlying model as `google/nano-banana-2.1/edit`, set up for generating images
> from scratch.

### Pricing (verbatim)

> Nano Banana 2.1 is billed on the tokens each request actually uses, as reported by Google. There
> are no flat per-image fees or surcharges for resolution, web search, or thinking; those settings
> only change how many tokens a request uses.

| Token type | Price per 1M tokens |
| --- | ---: |
| Input (prompt text and images) | $1.764 |
| Output text and thinking | $8.823 |
| Output image | $35.294 |

Representative per-image costs for a short prompt (~100 tokens) with `medium` thinking:

| Output resolution | Text-to-image | Edit with two 1K input images |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1K | $0.040 | $0.044 |
| 2K | $0.059 | $0.063 |
| 4K | $0.134 | $0.138 |

fal also states: "Google lists output images at about 1,120 tokens at 1K, 1,680 at 2K, and 3,780
at 4K, so a 4K image costs more than three times as much as a 1K image", and "Web search has no
separate fee. Search results the model reads are billed as input tokens."

### Tips and caveats published by fal (verbatim)

> **Small text:** Use 2K or 4K. Google notes that small text can render blurry at 1K.
>
> **Spatial layout:** Describe positions explicitly and check the result. Google notes occasional
> left/right confusion.

> The 512px (`0.5K`) option from Nano Banana 2 is not available on Nano Banana 2.1.

## Contradictions and errors in this capture

- **"5K output"** appears once ("improved visual quality and realism across 1K, 2K, and 5K
  output"). Google documents 1K/2K/4K and no 5K tier. Read this as a fal typo.
- **"Web search has no separate fee"** contradicts Google's own price list, which charges
  grounding search requests at $14 per 1,000 after a shared 5,000/month allowance. fal may absorb
  that charge, but its sentence is stronger than "included in our token price".
- fal's per-image estimates ($0.040 / $0.059 / $0.134) exceed Google's list equivalents ($0.0336 /
  $0.0504 / $0.113). The token rates are exactly 1.176x Google's ($1.764 vs $1.50, $8.823 vs
  $7.50, $35.294 vs $30.00), i.e. a consistent ~17.6% markup. fal does not say so.

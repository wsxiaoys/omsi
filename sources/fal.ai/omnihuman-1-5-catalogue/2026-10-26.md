## `fal-ai/bytedance/omnihuman/v1.5` input schema (verbatim descriptions)

| Field | Type | Required | Default | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `prompt` | string \| null | no | — | "The text prompt used to guide the video generation." |
| `image_url` | string | **yes** | — | "The URL of the image used to generate the video" |
| `mask_url` | string \| null | no | — | "The URL of the mask image to apply to the image. Only the person in the white area of the mask will speak." |

Output schema: `video` (File) and `duration` — "Duration of audio input/video output as used for
billing."

The endpoint `about` string, verbatim: "Generate incredibly accurate videos of human beings speaking a
dialogue using Bytnedce's OmniHuman v1.5 model." (sic — "Bytnedce").

## Differences from the BytePlus API

1. **No `seed`.** BytePlus documents a `seed` parameter; fal does not expose one on 1.5.
2. **Resolution is a discrete switch here.** BytePlus takes `output_resolution` as `720` or `1080`;
   fal takes a `720p`/`1080p` string. Same intent, different type.
3. **fal caps 1080p audio at 30 s.** BytePlus documents a flat "less than 60 seconds" for the whole
   service. fal's `turbo_mode` has no BytePlus equivalent by that name (BytePlus has
   `pe_fast_mode`, which is plausibly the same switch).
4. **No subject-detection helper.** fal takes a raw `mask_url`; the caller has to produce the mask
   elsewhere. Neither subject-recognition nor subject-detection is offered by fal.
5. **No prompt-language list** on fal's schema, though the 2.0 endpoint on the same host does list the
   six languages.

## Sibling endpoints, for boundary only

`fal-ai/bytedance/omnihuman` (the 1.0 generation): only `image_url` and `audio_url`, both required,
with "Audio must be under 30s long." No prompt, no mask, no resolution switch, no turbo mode. Price
$0.14/s, below 1.5's $0.16/s.

`bytedance/omnihuman/v2` (OmniHuman 2.0): prompt (six languages), image formats "jpeg, png, webp, bmp,
tiff, gif, heic or heif. Max size: 30 MB. Aspect ratio (width/height) between 0.4 and 2.5; width and
height between 300 and 6000 pixels", audio "wav or mp3. Max size: 15 MB. Duration between 4 and 300
seconds". Price $0.105/s. Its example asset URLs still point at `omnihuman_v15_input_image.png`.

## Summary

fal hosts OmniHuman 1.5 as `fal-ai/bytedance/omnihuman/v1.5`, proxying ByteDance, at $0.16 per second
of output video, with image + audio required and prompt/mask/turbo/resolution optional. On this host
1.5 is the most expensive of the three OmniHuman generations (1.0 $0.14/s, 1.5 $0.16/s, 2.0 $0.105/s),
which is the opposite ordering from BytePlus, where 2.0 undercuts 1.5.

## Gaps

- No latency, queue-time, or success-rate metrics published for any of the three endpoints.
- No statement of which ByteDance checkpoint is proxied, or of whether `turbo_mode` maps to
  BytePlus's `pe_fast_mode`.
- fal's 30 s audio cap at 1080p contradicts BytePlus's flat 60 s; no source explains it.
- No output frame rate documented; the sample file says 25 fps, which is a single unlabelled example.

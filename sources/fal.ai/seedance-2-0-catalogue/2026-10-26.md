## The standard tier is gated (verbatim, from `https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.0`)

> Seedance 2.0 API on fal
>
> **Early access**
>
> This model is not yet generally available. Request access and we will notify you when you can use
> it.
>
> bytedance/seedance-2.0
>
> By accessing this model through fal, you agree to the following terms:
>
> **B2B Only:** You will only serve business customers and verify that all end users are businesses
> (not individual consumers) by validating their email domain or equivalent business credentials.
>
> **End User Identification:** You will pass an `end_user_id` in your API payload that uniquely
> identifies each end customer. Upon request, you must be able to identify any end customer and
> restrict their access. It is fine for this value to be an anonymous identifier.
>
> [I agree to the terms above] [Cancel] [Sign in to request access]

Corroborating signals that the standard tier is not publicly listed:

- `GET https://api.fal.ai/v1/models?endpoint_id=bytedance/seedance-2.0` returns the bare endpoint ID
  with no model record, and the bare endpoint's OpenAPI schema returns HTTP 404.
- The keyword catalogue (17 Seedance 2.0/2.5 matches) contains only `fast/…`, `mini/…` and `us/…`
  sub-paths — none of the standard `text-to-video`, `image-to-video`, `reference-to-video` endpoints.

The per-endpoint pages and schemas for the standard tier **do** exist
(`/models/bytedance/seedance-2.0/text-to-video`, and its OpenAPI returns 200) and do not display the
gate banner. The practical reading is that the endpoints are built and documented but access is
approved per account; fal does not say when the gate appeared.

## Input schema (standard tier, `bytedance/seedance-2.0/text-to-video`)

| Field | Type / allowed values | Default | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `prompt` | string | — | required |
| `resolution` | `480p`, `720p`, `1080p`, `4k` | 720p | "480p for faster generation, 720p for balance, 1080p for high quality, 4k for highest quality" |
| `duration` | `auto`, `4`…`15` (string enum) | — | "Supports 4 to 15 seconds, or auto to let the model decide based on the prompt" |
| `aspect_ratio` | `auto`, `21:9`, `16:9`, `4:3`, `1:1`, `3:4`, `9:16` | — | |
| `generate_audio` | boolean | `true` | "The cost of video generation is the same r[egardless]" |
| `codec` | `auto`, `H264`, `H265` | `auto` | |
| `bitrate_mode` | `standard`, `high` | `standard` | "high requests a higher-quality, larger-file encode from the model" |
| `seed` | integer | — | returned in the output too |
| `end_user_id` | string | — | required by the B2B terms above |

`image-to-video` adds `image_url` (required) and `end_image_url` (last frame); accepted image formats
"JPEG, PNG, WebP, BMP, TIFF, GIF, HEIC, HEIF. Max 30 MB". `reference-to-video` takes `image_urls`,
`video_urls` (MP4, MOV, "Up to 3 video[s]") and `audio_urls` (MP3, WAV, up to 3 files), referenced in
the prompt as `@Image1`, `@Video1`, `@Audio1`.

**Fast and Mini schemas are narrower:** `resolution` is `480p`/`720p` only. Fast and Mini otherwise
carry the same duration, aspect-ratio, audio, codec and seed fields. The US tier schema offers
`480p`/`720p`/`1080p`/`4k`.

## Price strings (verbatim `pricingInfoOverride`)

Fast tier:

> For every second of 720p video you generated, you will be charged **$0.2419/second**. Your request
> will cost **$0.0112** per 1,000 tokens. The number of tokens is given by (height of output video *
> width of output video * duration * 24) / 1024.

Fast reference-to-video adds: "**If video inputs are provided the price is multiplied by 0.6**. With
video inputs and 720p resolution the price is **$0.14515** per second."

Mini:

> For every second of video generated, you will be charged roughly **$0.0721**/second for **480p** and
> **$0.1547**/second for **720p**. Actual cost calculations are based on token prices, with token
> costs being **$0.007** per **1,000** tokens for both **480p** and **720p**.

Mini reference-to-video: "With video references, the rate is **$0.0042 per 1,000 tokens**, applied to
the combined input and output video token usage. Image references alone do not trigger this
reduction."

US tier text-to-video and reference-to-video:

> For every second of 720p video you generated, you will be charged roughly **$0.37/second** and for
> **480p** you will be charged **$0.1731/second**. Your request will cost **$0.0168** per 1,000 tokens
> for 480p and 720p. … **If video inputs are provided the price is multiplied by 0.6**. With video
> inputs and 720p resolution the price is roughly **$0.222** per second.

US image-to-video is priced differently: "$0.3034/second" at 720p and "$0.682/second" at 1080p, with
"$0.014 per 1000 tokens for 480p and 720p" and "$0.008 per 1000 tokens for 4k".

The standard tier has **no published price** because it is not in the public catalogue.

## Gaps

- No published date for the standard-tier endpoints, so fal's own first-public date for the flagship
  Seedance 2.0 tier is unknown.
- fal does not state which ByteDance checkpoint (`260128` etc.) each endpoint serves, or how often it
  refreshes.
- The price strings are per-second heuristics derived from token billing; they do not say how 4K or
  1080p output is priced on the standard tier, since that tier has no catalogue price.
- `modelFamily` is "Seedance 2" for all rows, so the catalogue cannot be used to distinguish tiers by
  family label; only the endpoint path does.

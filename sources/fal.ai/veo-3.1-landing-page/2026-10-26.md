# fal.ai: Veo 3.1 landing page

- Origin: fal.ai, "Veo 3.1 (Text to Video) API on fal — `fal-ai/veo3.1`, details and endpoints",
  <https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/veo3.1>
- Evidence kind: host model landing page (independent host: marketing, pricing table, FAQ)
- Publication date: none stated. The page says "Veo 3.1 is now available on fal" with no date, and
  every endpoint carries "This endpoint will be deprecated on November 17, 2026."
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: Veo 3.1 (standard), Veo 3.1 Fast, Veo 3.1 "Light" (described, not hosted)

## Host framing (verbatim)

> Veo 3.1 by Google, the most advanced AI video generation model in the world. With sound on!

> Cinema-Quality Video. With Sound.
> Veo 3.1 is now available on fal. Google DeepMind's flagship video generation model with true 4K
> output, native audio, and a versatile set of generation modes for every workflow.

Badges on the endpoint: "Inference", "Commercial use", "Partner".

> This endpoint will be deprecated on November 17, 2026.

The deprecation date is the same as the Vertex AI model card's retirement date for
`veo-3.1-generate-001` ("November 17, 2026 or later"), so fal is almost certainly mirroring Google's
support window rather than setting its own.

Detailed pricing (verbatim):

> Standard tier — 720p / 1080p: $0.20/s without audio, $0.40/s with audio. 4K: $0.40/s without audio,
> $0.60/s with audio.
> Fast tier — 720p / 1080p: $0.10/s without audio, $0.15/s with audio. 4K: $0.30/s without audio,

Billing is per second of generated video, so price scales with duration — unlike the Google Cloud
price list, whose unit is an undefined "count".

## Tiers (verbatim table, abridged)

> | Tier | Audio | Max Resolution | Best For |
> | Standard | ✅ | 4K | Production quality, cinematic output |
> | Fast | ✅ | 4K | Rapid iteration, prototyping |
> | Light | ❌ | 720p | High-volume, cost-sensitive workflows |

> Light is a budget tier available on Google's own platforms (not currently on fal.ai). It generates
> silent 720p clips at significantly lower cost …

Two conflicts with Google's own documents: the Vertex model card lists **Sound generation: Supported**
for `veo-3.1-lite-generate-001` and output resolutions "720p, 1080p", while fal says Light is silent
and 720p-only; and the model card caps **Fast** at 1080p output while fal sells Fast at 4K (Google's
pricing page also lists Fast 4K, so the model card is the outlier there).

## Output envelope (verbatim)

> The model supports:
> 720p or 1080p resolution videos
> 5-8 second duration at 24 FPS
> Both 16:9 (landscape) and 9:16 (portrait) aspect ratios

> 4K Resolution
> The first mainstream AI video model to support true 4K output. Generate at 720p, 1080p, or 4K with
> aspect ratios of 16:9 or 9:16 at 24 FPS.

The "5-8 second" line contradicts the endpoint schema's `duration` enum of `4s | 6s | 8s` (see the
API-schema record) and Google's "4, 6, or 8 seconds". The prose is stale; the schema is operational.

## Extension (verbatim)

> Each generation produces up to 8 seconds of video. Use the extend-video endpoint to chain extensions
> — up to 7 seconds per step, 20 steps maximum — enabling a total output of roughly 148 seconds
> (~2.5 minutes) from a single starting clip.

Google Cloud's extend guide caps Veo extension at **37 seconds** total. The 7-second step agrees;
the totals do not.

| | Veo 3 | Veo 3.1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native audio | ✅ | ✅ |
| Dialogue & lip sync | ✅ | ✅ Improved |

> Bottom line: For one-off hero shots, Veo 3 and 3.1 are comparable. If you care about dialogue clarity,

Cross-model table on the same page: Veo 3.1 "4K", native audio yes, 24 FPS, "8s (extendable to
~148s)", API "fal.ai, Vertex AI", watermark "SynthID"; Sora 2 "1080p", 24–30 FPS, 20s, "C2PA";
Kling 2.x "4K (premium)", 30 FPS, up to 3 min; Runway Gen-4 "720p native", 24 FPS, 16s. These are
fal's own summary of competitors, with no measurement, date or method.

> The first mainstream AI video model to support true 4K output.

That superlative is fal's, not Google's, and is not dated or evidenced.

## Prompting guidance (verbatim, abridged)

> Keep dialogue short — write conversational lines that fit within an 8-second reading window; long
> speeches get cut off or rushed

> Be specific about camera movement … Describe audio explicitly … Use film grammar — terms like "rack
> focus," "dolly in," "establishing shot," and "match cut" are understood

This is the only captured source that gives an operational working limit on dialogue length (one
8-second reading window), and it is host guidance, not a vendor parameter.

## Availability claim (verbatim)

> Is fal.ai the only way to access Veo 3.1? No. Veo 3.1 is also available through Google AI Studio,
> Vertex AI, the Gemini app, and Google Flow.

## Summary

fal hosts the widest Veo 3.1 endpoint surface (nine endpoints across five modes and two tiers),
billed per second at $0.20–$0.60 standard and $0.10–$0.35 fast, with the same 2026-11-17 deprecation
date as Google's own support window. It documents 4K on both tiers, SynthID watermarking, and
per-second billing, and it is the only source that names a "Light" tier — which it does not host and
describes in terms that contradict Google's model card.

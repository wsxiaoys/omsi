# fal.ai: "Wan 3" landing page and FAQ

- Origin: fal.ai, "Wan 3 — Alibaba's Next-Generation AI Video Model", <https://fal.ai/wan-3>
  (`landingPageSlug: "wan-3"` in the catalogue)
- Evidence kind: provider marketing and FAQ (independent host)
- Publication date: none stated; the page has no date line and no "available as of" sentence
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: Wan 3.0, Wan 3.0 Prime

## Positioning (verbatim)

> Alibaba's Wan 3 generates up to 30 seconds of 1080p video in a single pass, with audio in the same
> generation and every aspect ratio from 16:9 to 9:16.

> Wan 3.0 is the next generation of the Wan video model family from Alibaba's Tongyi Lab, the team
> behind Wan 2.1 through Wan 2.7. It generates video from text, from a still image, or from reference
> material, at up to 30 seconds a shot with audio in the same pass, and it can reason about composition
> and motion before rendering.

The vendor attribution here is "Alibaba's Tongyi Lab" — fal's wording. Alibaba Cloud's own documents do
not name a team on the Wan 3.0 pages.

## Endpoint summary (verbatim)

> Three: `text-to-video` for generating a shot from a prompt, `image-to-video` for animating a still,
> with an optional end frame so you can set where the shot finishes, and `reference-to-video` for
> conditioning on material you already have. All three share the same duration, resolution, aspect
> ratio, and audio controls.

Only the standard (`alibaba/wan-3.0/*`) endpoints are linked from this landing page; the prime tier is
not mentioned on it, despite existing in the catalogue.

## Documented envelope (verbatim)

> Output runs at 480p, 720p, or 1080p, with 1080p the default. Durations run from 2 to 30 seconds in a
> single generation, and you can leave the length unset to let the model choose one from your prompt and
> reference media. Aspect ratios cover 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, and 9:16, or the model can pick the ratio
> that suits the shot. Audio is generated alongside the video by default.

> Reference-to-video takes up to 10 reference images, up to 5 reference video clips totalling 15
> seconds at 16fps or higher, and up to 5 reference audio tracks totalling 15 seconds, in a single
> request. With thinking turned on it also reads a document you upload or a public web page you link, so
> a brief or a product page can seed a generation directly.

The FAQ's aspect-ratio list omits 21:9 although the endpoint schemas expose it.

## Thinking mode (verbatim)

> Thinking is an optional mode, off by default, that has the model reason about composition, staging,
> and motion before it renders a frame. It is worth turning on for prompts that describe a sequence of
> events rather than a single continuous action, and it is required for the document and web-page
> reference inputs, since those have to be read and interpreted before they can be turned into a shot.

This is a host's explanation of `enable_thinking`; Alibaba's guide lists document/web-page input as a
task type without describing a reasoning step.

## Pricing and free tier (verbatim)

> Pricing is per second of generated video and varies by output resolution: $0.05/s at 480p, $0.10/s at
> 720p, and $0.20/s at 1080p. A 5-second clip at 720p costs $0.50, and a 10-second clip at 1080p costs
> $2.00. The rate is the same on all three endpoints, pay-per-use with no minimums and no subscription.

> Signed-in fal accounts get five free Wan 3 text-to-video generations every 24 hours in the fal
> sandbox, at 720p with audio, on a rolling window rather than a one-time trial.

## Weights and licensing (verbatim)

> Alibaba's published Wan weights currently stop at Wan 2.2 under Apache 2.0, and the releases since
> then have been commercial API models. Wan 3 is available on fal as an API model. We will note the
> licensing here if Alibaba publishes weights for it.

> Yes. Content generated through the fal.ai API can be used in commercial projects. Check fal.ai's terms
> of service for full details on usage rights and licensing.

The weights statement is an independent host corroboration of the Hugging Face and GitHub absence
records, and it is the clearest single sentence in the corpus on the licensing question.

## Family context (verbatim)

> Wan 2.7 remains available, with text-to-video, image-to-video, reference-to-video, and
> instruction-based video editing at 720p and 1080p, native audio, and durations from 2 to 15 seconds.
> Wan 2.6 is also available, along with open-weight Wan 2.1 and 2.2 endpoints and LoRA trainers.

This is the host's own statement that Wan 2.7 is a different model with a shorter (2–15 s) duration
ceiling, and that 2.1/2.2 are the open-weight ones.

## Marketing claims (labelled as claims, no measurements attached)

> Full-Body Motion at Speed … Fast, athletic movement is the hardest thing to keep coherent … Limbs,
> weight, and contact with the ground stay readable through the whole take.

> One continuous pass is what lets camera movement and one-take shot language hold together across a
> whole scene.

> Output at 1080p with audio generated in the same pass as the picture, so dialogue, ambience, and
> on-screen action land together instead of being dubbed on afterwards.

No numbers, sample sizes, or independent evaluations accompany any of these.

## Summary

fal's landing page confirms the same operating envelope as Alibaba's documentation (2–30 s, 480p–1080p,
audio by default, 10 images + 5 videos + 5 audio references, document/web-page input with thinking) and
adds two host-only facts: a rolling free tier of five 720p text-to-video generations per day, and an
explicit statement that Alibaba's open weights stop at Wan 2.2. It markets only the standard tier.

## Gaps

- No publication or update date anywhere on the page.
- No latency, throughput, or success-rate figures.
- The page's aspect-ratio list omits 21:9, contradicting the schemas in the catalogue record.
- The prime tier is absent from the landing page, so no host prose explains what prime is for.

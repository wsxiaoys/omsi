### What the post claims changed in v1.6 (vendor claims, verbatim)

> **Native 864x1296 Resolution (1MP)** - FASHN v1.6 delivers results at a native resolution of
> 864x1296 pixels, representing a 50% increase compared to v1.5. The improved resolution ensures
> clearer details such as fabric textures and garment patterns without compromising the established
> accuracy of earlier versions.
>
> **Improved Identity Preservation (Quality Mode)** - The 'Quality' mode has been upgraded to better
> maintain original image integrity. Improvements include more accurate skin texture preservation,
> and smarter top & bottom interactions, resulting in more authentic virtual try-ons.
>
> **Optimized Performance with Minimal Runtime Impact** - Higher resolution typically means
> increased runtime; however, we've optimized the processing to offer enhanced results efficiently:
> Performance Mode (~7 seconds): Provides improved visual detail compared to v1.5's 'Balanced'
> mode, with only a minor runtime increase (+1 second). Balanced Mode (~10 seconds): Further refines
> garment detail and texture. More reliable in more challenging try-on tasks. Quality Mode (~19
> seconds): Prioritizes overall image quality, extensively improving skin texture fidelity and
> realistic garment integration with the original model image.

The "50% increase" is a linear per-axis figure: 864×1296 is 1.5× each side of 576×864, and 2.25×
the pixel count (1,119,744 vs 497,664 px).

### Resolution ceiling — output cannot exceed the input

Verbatim note under the first comparison example:

> Note: While FASHN v1.6 supports resolutions up to 864×1296 pixels, the example's output resolution
> (768×1152) matches the resolution of the input model images. FASHN v1.6 does not upscale beyond the
> resolution of the original input.

The same example states that v1.5 produced "a resolution of 576×864 pixels within approximately 6
seconds" on identical inputs and seed, and v1.6 produced 768×1152 in Performance (7 s) and Balanced
(10 s).

### Six example comparisons (v1.5 vs v1.6)

The post walks through six pairs — Columbia puffer jacket (on-model), midi dress (flat-lay to plus
model), H&M suit set (on-model to fitting-room selfie), Fashion Nova gown (on-model to plus model),
Zara polo shirt (side view), baggy Diesel jeans (on-model to tight shorts). Claims per example are
qualitative: sharper detail, better zipper/stitch/pocket definition, more anatomically correct legs,
restored skin tone on arms and neck, correct colour and print transfer. No scores, no human-preference
votes, and no competitor models are involved.

## Gaps

- Latency figures in this post (~7 / ~10 / ~19 s) disagree with the current API reference
  (~5 / ~8 / 12–17 s). The post was "updated" in July 2026 without revising them.
- The comparisons are vendor-selected examples with no stated evaluation protocol, sample size, or
  rater model.
- The post never states parameter count, architecture, or training data.
- No statement about garment categories supported by v1.6; the examples are all apparel.

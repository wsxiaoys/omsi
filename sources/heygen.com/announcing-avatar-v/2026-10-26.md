Language-count claims on the same page, which do not agree with each other: the hero banner says
"starring you in 177+ languages and dialects", the body says "in 175+ languages and dialects", and the
lip-sync section says "Phoneme-level accuracy across every supported language … in 175+ languages and
dialects". The 2026-04-11 archived version instead carried the site-wide banner "230+ avatars in
140+ languages".

No numbers, benchmarks, or side-by-side measurements accompany any claim. The only competitor
statement is unquantified: "companies like Synthesia still requires studio time to get anywhere close
to this output quality".

## Summary

The launch post is the vendor's own dating evidence for Avatar V as a product: publication
2026-04-08, three days before the first Wayback capture. It establishes the two headline claims that
the docs also encode — video (not photo) as the reference input, and separation of performance from
appearance ("one recording, endless looks").

The visible "Last Updated October 20th, 2026" is not the publication date. The page was revised
between April and October 2026 (the language count changed from 140+ to 175+/177+), so quotes here are
from the October revision while the launch date comes from the JSON-LD and the archive.

## Gaps

- No publication time-of-day for the launch beyond the JSON-LD's 15:00Z.
- "15-second clip" conflicts with the documented footage window of 15–600 s
  ([video-to-avatar record](../video-to-avatar/2026-10-26.md)); the post never says whether 15 s is a
  minimum, a recommended length, or a separate faster tier.
- Nothing states whether the launch was general availability, a waitlist, or plan-gated.
- No mention of Avatar IV's own video-training support, which the API docs do document; the post's
  contrast ("Avatar IV … single photo") describes only the photo-avatar path.

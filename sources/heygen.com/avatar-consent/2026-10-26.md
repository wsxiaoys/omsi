# HeyGen: Avatar Consent

- Origin: HeyGen developer documentation, "Avatar Consent",
  <https://developers.heygen.com/docs/avatar-consent> (Markdown at `/docs/avatar-consent.md`)
- Evidence kind: official documentation and provider policy (first-party)
- Publication date: none stated. The changelog's "Consent Flow Clarification" entry is dated April 2026.
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: HeyGen digital twins (the consent gate for Avatar V rendering)

## Captured content (verbatim)

> Before you can generate video with a **digital twin** (`type: "digital_twin"`), HeyGen needs proof
> that the person depicted agreed to be cloned.

> The consent API step applies only to **digital twin** avatars. A group created from a photo
> (`type: "photo"`) or prompt (`type: "prompt"`) has no consent request to make and reports
> `consent_status` of `null`. … HeyGen runs no consent check and keeps no consent record for a photo
> avatar, so getting the subject's agreement before you create one, and keeping your own record of it,
> is your responsibility.

The three levels, transcribed:

| Level | Flow | Who it's for | Availability |
| - | - | - | - |
| 1. Record via webcam | The avatar subject records a short consent statement on camera through HeyGen's hosted consent page. | All customers | Available today |
| 2. Upload a consent video | You supply a pre-recorded consent video instead of recording live. | Enterprise only | Available today — contact sales |
| 3. Skip the consent flow | Consent collection is waived entirely for accounts that have signed an indemnity agreement. | Enterprise only | Contact sales |

> By default it starts the webcam flow and returns a URL for the avatar subject — the link is valid for
> **24 hours**.

> `consent_status` … `pending` (awaiting the subject or under review), `accepted` (consent cleared), or
> `rejected` (resubmit).

## Summary

Consent is a per-avatar-group gate on digital twins, collected through a HeyGen-hosted page, with two
enterprise bypass paths (uploaded consent video, signed indemnity). Photo and prompt avatars carry no
consent step at all, and HeyGen says explicitly that the customer then owns the responsibility.

Because Avatar V renders from a look that must belong to a consented group, this policy is a hard
precondition on Avatar V output — and, for photo avatars, an unenforced one.

## Gaps

- No captured statement about the policy for synthetic (prompt-generated) identities beyond "no consent
  step", and no moderation policy for likeness misuse.
- No published definition of what the consent video must contain beyond the separate 35-second limit
  noted in the video-to-avatar record.
- Level 3 (indemnity waiver) has no published terms; it is a sales conversation.
- No retention or deletion policy for consent records was captured.

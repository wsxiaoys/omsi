## Captured content (verbatim)

> The v3 API exposes three model families. … **HeyGen Avatar** — Avatar rendering engines turn a
> look and a script into video, selected per request through the `engine` field on `POST /v3/videos`.
> New integrations should default to Avatar IV for broad coverage, opt into Avatar V for the
> highest-fidelity motion and lip-sync, and use Avatar III for its dedicated photo-to-video pipeline.

> A separate, older Avatar III engine remains available to existing customers through the **legacy
> v1/v2 endpoints**. It uses a different pipeline and is **not** the same as the Avatar III
> (`avatar_iii`) engine documented here.

## Summary

This is the vendor's own statement of the Avatar V ↔ Avatar IV difference, and it is narrower than
the marketing page's version. Per this table Avatar V gains exactly two capabilities
(cross-reference animation, `motion_prompt` on video avatars) and loses two (arbitrary image input,
`expressiveness`). Everything else — digital twin, photo avatar, studio avatar — is shared.

Note the third sibling: Avatar III is the only engine the vendor advertises with 4K output, which
matters because both hosts expose a `4k` option on their Avatar V endpoints.

## Gaps

- No quality metric, latency, or cost column; "highest fidelity" is unquantified.
- No release dates per engine, so the table cannot be dated relative to the May 2026 changelog entry
  that first exposed engine selection.
- The claim that all three are "available to new users" says nothing about plan gating; the pricing
  page shows Avatar V is not on the Free plan.

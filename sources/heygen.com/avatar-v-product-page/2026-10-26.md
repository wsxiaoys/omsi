## Captured content (verbatim, selected)

> **Avatar V** — Finally, an AI avatar indistinguishable from you. Character consistency is what
> separates a useful avatar from a gimmick.

> Avatar V takes this a step further: it separates your identity from your appearance, learning the
> precise way you move, gesture, and express yourself so that motion can be applied to any version of

> A 15-second webcam recording unlocks professional-grade video at any scale.

> Where prior systems condition on a single reference frame, Avatar V operates over a full video

> The selective attention mechanism extracts salient identity signals across frames, including lip
> geometry, facial silhouette structure, and expression transition patterns, while naturally
> suppressing frames where pose, lighting, or occlusion reduce signal quality.

Training stages, as named on the page: "Same-scene video pretraining", "Cross-scene reference
fine-tuning", "Reinforcement learning for identity".

| Capability | Avatar V | Avatar IV |
| - | - | - |
| Reference input | Short video clip (15 seconds) | Single photo |
| Identity preservation | Strong (video-context model) | Partial (photo-based) |
| Cross-scene generation | Native, single-pass | Two-stage pipeline required |

Other headline claims: "No cap on video length and quality"; "Unlimited background or setting";
"Phoneme-level accuracy … in 175+ languages and dialects" (the page banner says 177+, the body 175+);
"Trained on 10M+ data points"; "Rated #1 most realistic avatars on G2".

## Summary

This is the marketing framing of the same two capabilities the developer docs encode: a video
reference window instead of a single frame, and separation of motion from appearance. It is also the
source of the strongest contradiction on this page — see Gaps.

Nothing here is measured. The page offers mechanism prose (selective attention over a video context
window, three training stages) and no benchmark, sample size, or third-party comparison.

## Gaps

- **The Avatar IV column describes only the photo-avatar path.** HeyGen's own API documentation lists
  `digital_twin` and `studio_avatar` as supported Avatar IV look types
  ([engines comparison](../avatar-engines-comparison/2026-10-26.md),
  [Avatar IV doc](../avatar-iv-engine-doc/2026-10-26.md)), which are trained from video footage, not a
  "single photo". The marketing table is not a complete description of Avatar IV and should not be
  read as one.
- "Stable beyond 30 minutes" matches the documented 30-minute per-scene cap rather than a measured
  degradation curve; no long-form test is cited.
- "No cap on video length" is contradicted by the documented 30-minute-per-scene limit.
- Language counts disagree within the page (177+ vs 175+).
- No pricing, plan availability, or output resolution on the page.

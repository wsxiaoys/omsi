## Captured content

### Identity (verbatim)

> FLUX.2 [dev] is a 32 billion parameter rectified flow transformer capable of generating,
> editing and combining images based on text instructions.

### Key features (verbatim)

> 1. State of the art in open text-to-image generation, single-reference editing and
>    multi-reference editing.
> 2. No need for finetuning: character, object and style reference without additional training in
>    one model.
> 3. Trained using guidance distillation, making FLUX.2 [dev] more efficient.
> 4. Open weights to drive new scientific research, and empower artists to develop innovative
>    workflows.
> 5. Generated outputs can be used for personal, scientific, and commercial purposes, as
>    described in the FLUX [dev] Non-Commercial License.

### Usage / self-hosting (verbatim)

> We provide a reference implementation of FLUX.2 [dev], as well as sampling code, in a dedicated
> github repository. Developers and creatives looking to build on top of FLUX.2 [dev] are
> encouraged to use this as a starting point.

> FLUX.2 [dev] is also available in both ComfyUI and Diffusers.

> For local deployment on a consumer type graphics card, like an RTX 4090 or an RTX 5090, please
> see the diffusers docs on our GitHub page.

The card's worked example loads `diffusers/FLUX.2-dev-bnb-4bit` (4-bit text encoder and DiT, VAE
in bf16) with a **remote** text encoder
(`https://remote-text-encoder-flux-2.huggingface.co/predict`), and notes
`num_inference_steps=50, #28 steps can be a good trade-off` with `guidance_scale=4`.

### Safety and provenance (summary of the "Risks" section)

BFL describes pre-training NSFW/CSAM data filtering (with the Internet Watch Foundation),
post-training mitigation rounds for text-to-image and image-to-image abuse, internal and
external third-party evaluations, and a final pre-release evaluation of the release checkpoint
with "total prompts n≈2,800", after which it "approved the release of the FLUX.2 Pro model via
API and the release of the open-weight FLUX.2 [dev] model under a non-commercial license".

Operational requirements stated for the released weights:

> The repository for the FLUX.2 [dev] model includes filters for NSFW and IP-infringing content
> at input and output. Filters or manual review must be used with the model under the terms of
> the FLUX.2 [dev] Non-Commercial License. We may approach known deployers of the FLUX.2 [dev]
> model at random to verify that filters or manual review processes are in place.

> The inference code for FLUX.2 [dev] implements an example of pixel-layer watermarking, and this
> repository includes links to the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA)
> standard for metadata.

The card also states that "Use of our models and access to our API are governed by our FLUX
[dev] Non-Commercial License (for our non-commercial open-weight users); Developer Terms of
Service, Self-Hosted Commercial License Terms, and Usage Policy (for our commercial open-weight
model users)".

### License section (verbatim)

## Notes and contradictions

- The card's feature 5 says outputs "can be used for personal, scientific, and commercial
  purposes". The licence's §2(d) is more precise: outputs may be used for any purpose including
  commercial, but the *model* may not be used for commercial purposes and outputs may not train a
  competing model ([licence record](../../bfl.ai/flux2-dev-non-commercial-license/2026-10-26.md)).
  The card's phrasing is easy to misread as a commercial grant for the weights.
- The card gives no multi-reference count, while the launch blog says "up to 10", BFL's family
  overview says "recommended max 6", and fal's edit endpoint accepts 4 (see the model page's
  contradictions section).
- "Trained using guidance distillation" is consistent with the GitHub README's
  guidance-distilled / not-step-distilled row for [dev].

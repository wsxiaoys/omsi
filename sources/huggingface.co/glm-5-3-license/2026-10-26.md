## Summary

MIT-like in substance: use, modification, fine-tuning, redistribution and commercial use are
all granted, with attribution. The single extra obligation is a **security review before
commercial use by a Model-as-a-Service operator whose revenue exceeds US$10 billion over any
12 consecutive months** — a threshold that binds only the largest inference businesses, and
whose scope Z.AI sets unilaterally. Hosts that merely relay requests to someone else's model
are expressly exempt.

Note the size of the threshold versus the comparable licence in this corpus: Moonshot's
"Kimi K3 License" sets its separate-agreement trigger at **US$20 million**
([Kimi K3 licence](../kimi-k3-license/2026-10-26.md)). GLM-5.3's is 500× higher.

## Compared with the predecessor

A second retrieval on the same date, `GET https://huggingface.co/api/models/zai-org/GLM-5.2`
(created 2026-06-16T07:39:20Z, 295 files), reports `cardData.license: "mit"`. So the licence
**changed** between GLM-5.2 (MIT) and GLM-5.3 ("GLM-5.3 License"): the newer model adds an
attribution condition, a Model-as-a-Service security-review trigger and a contact address.
GLM-5.1 and GLM-5 are also MIT by the same endpoint.

This matters for a reader upgrading: nothing in the GLM-5.3 docs or blog mentions a licence
change, and the two models are otherwise priced identically.

## Gaps

- No acceptable-use policy in the licence file; Z.ai's Terms of Use and usage policies were not

- "Security review" has no published procedure, timeline, or fee.
- No statement on whether the licence covers the `.eval_results` and training artefacts, or
  whether a separate grant covers the technical report.
- The GLM-5.2 comparison comes from Hub metadata (`license: mit`), not from reading the
  GLM-5.2 LICENSE file, which was not retrieved.

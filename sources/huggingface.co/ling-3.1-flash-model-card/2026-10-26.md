## Deployment recipes in the card, verbatim

- SGLang is "recommended", with a day-0 image: `docker pull lmsysorg/sglang:dev-ling-3.1-flash`;
  server launch `--model-path inclusionAI/Ling-3.1-flash --tp 8 --ep 8
  --speculative-algorithm NEXTN` on 8×H200, or `--tp 4 --ep 4` on 4×GB300.
- Hopper decode optimisations: `SGLANG_ENABLE_FP8_BLOCK_GEMV=1`,
  `SGLANG_ENABLE_FUSED_KDA_OUTPUT_QUANT=1`, "optional, ~10% faster batch-1 decode".
- vLLM: `--tool-call-parser ling3 --reasoning-parser ling3`, `--mamba-cache-mode align`,
  `--speculative-config '{"method":"mtp","num_speculative_tokens":3}'`.
- Recommended sampling: `temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95, top_k=20`, with
  `chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": true}` — i.e. **thinking is a request-time switch**,
  and the vendor recommends leaving it on.

The card links a third-party cookbook, `https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/InclusionAI/Ling-3.1-flash`,
for "measured accuracy/speed numbers".

## Summary

First-party confirmation of: MIT weights (ungated, fp8, 54 shards, 512 experts / 8 active), the
560B/25B scale, the 1M-token context claim, a hybrid-linear 63-KDA/9-Gated-MLA architecture,
request-time thinking control, tool calling, and first-class SGLang/vLLM support. It supplies
**no numeric benchmark table** and **no release date**; the date has to come from the repository
metadata (2026-10-13) and the vendor blog (2026-10-15).

## Gaps

- No benchmark numbers; the evaluation figure is an uncaptured image.
- The card says "1M-token context window" without the 256K service cap the vendor docs state
  ([docs](../../ant-ling.com/ling-model-docs/2026-10-26.md)).
- No checkpoint identifier, so this card cannot be tied to the `1019` release.
- No model size, parameter dtype or hardware requirement beyond the recipes.
- The card's own SGLang recipe targets the `Ling-3.1-flash` path even in the `1019` card's copy —
  the recipes were not updated per checkpoint.

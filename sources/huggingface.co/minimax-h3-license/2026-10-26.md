## What this establishes

- The H3 weights carry a **territory-restricted community licence**: EU, UK, Republic of Korea and
  the USA are excluded from the grant, and use there requires a separate licence from MiniMax.
- A post-trained variant such as H3 Max falls squarely inside the definition of **Model Derivative**,
  so the same territory restriction and the same >$20M commercial-authorisation trigger apply to it
  on their face.
- Redistribution through **Hosted Services** is explicitly within the scope of the agreement
  (§I.8, §I.12), i.e. hosting is a licensed act, not an unregulated one.
- The licence is not an OSI-style open-source licence: it is a limited, territory-scoped,
  use-restricted grant with a revenue threshold and an attribution requirement.

## Gaps

- **The licence does not state what arrangement covers H3 Max.** fal is a US company and MiniMax
  co-announces H3 Max, so a separate licence (the §II Excluded-Territories path) is the natural
  reading, but no document captured states its existence or terms. This is recorded as an open
  question, not as a finding of non-compliance.
- The licence text is dated 2026-08-02 while the vendor docs describe a revision reviewed
  2026-08-26; which text was in force at H3 Max's 2026-08-23/27 launch is not established.
- No output-ownership clause was located in the captured text (it may exist later in the document,
  which was captured only in part).

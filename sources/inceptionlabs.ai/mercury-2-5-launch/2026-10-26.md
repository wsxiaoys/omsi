- Evidence kind: first-party launch announcement (vendor claims).
- Publication date: the post carries no explicit byline date; the page's blog listing dates it

## Verbatim

> Today we're releasing Mercury 2.5, our most capable production model yet. It is a significant
> step up in quality over Mercury 2, with the same low-latency, low-cost serving profile.

> Mercury 2.5 is the most capable diffusion LLM on the market. To our knowledge, it is the largest
> diffusion language model ever trained.

> **Quality:** 40% increase in intelligence from Mercury 2. Comparable to cost-optimized frontier
> models like GPT-5.6 Luna (Low), Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, and Claude Haiku 4.5.
> **Speed:** 1,107 tokens per second on widely-available NVIDIA GPUs.
> **Context:** 260K tokens.
> **Price:** $0.20 per million input and $0.75 per million output.
> At launch, Mercury 2.5 is 80% off at $0.04 per million input and $0.15 per million output.

> **Capabilities:** Tunable reasoning, parallel tool calls, and schema-aligned JSON.

> Mercury models are available through our Inception API, Baseten, and OpenRouter.

Customer-reported latency: OpenCall "median model response latency close to 170 milliseconds" and
"our P99 response time dropped from several minutes to just one second" on its production
workload; Augment Code reports cutting compaction latency 82% (≈150s → 27s).

## Reading

- **Mercury 3 is a successor, not a relabel.** The two launch posts make parallel claims — "most
  capable" — with different context (1M vs 260K), different speed (1,068 vs 1,107 t/s), different
  regular output price ($0.70 vs $0.75) and a new `instant` effort level.
- The 2.5 post claims "40% increase in intelligence from Mercury 2"; the 3 post claims "3x from
  Mercury 1 (mini)". Neither publishes a benchmark table, so neither delta is checkable.
- The 2.5 post's 80% launch discount ($0.04/$0.15) is still the price OpenRouter shows for
  Mercury 2.5, while Inception's docs list $0.20/$0.75 — a live price conflict for the
  predecessor.

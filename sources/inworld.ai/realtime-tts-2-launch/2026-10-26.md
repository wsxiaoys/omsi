## Identity and lifecycle statements

> General availability · August 31, 2026

> Realtime TTS-2 from Inworld AI is a new generation of voice model built for realtime
> conversation. … Available today via the Inworld API and the Inworld Realtime API in general
> availability.

The page's own FAQ repeats "in general availability" without a date. This is the only first-party
page in the corpus that labels TTS-2 GA and gives a GA date; the TTS release notes announce the same
model on **May 5, 2026** (see
[`tts-release-notes`](../tts-release-notes/2026-10-26.md)), and both host records date their TTS-2
endpoints to early May 2026. The May launch and the August GA label are therefore different events
by the vendor's own text, but no page explains the gap.

## The four claimed capabilities (verbatim, abridged)

1. **Voice direction** — "A natural-language description of how a line should be delivered, passed
   inline at the start of your text. Not a fixed list of preset emotions. Not a slider." Best
   practice claim: "long, descriptive prompts beat short labels — `[speak sadly, as if something bad
   just happened]` directs the model far better than `[sad]`".
2. **Conversational awareness** — "The model takes the actual audio of the prior turns of the
   exchange as input, not just a transcript. … Audio context flows automatically across turns inside
   a Realtime session."
3. **Crosslingual** — "One voice identity preserved across over 100 languages, including
   mid-utterance language switches inside a single generation. … No language flag needed."
4. **Advanced Voice Design** — a saved voice generated from a prose prompt
   (`POST /voices/v1/voices:design`), with three stability modes: "Expressive", "Balanced"
   (default), "Stable".

The page also claims inline non-verbals (`[laugh]`, `[sigh]`, `[breathe]`, `[clear_throat]`,
`[cough]`), disfluencies ("Real *uh* and *um*, in the right places"), and voice cloning.

## Latency claims — three different numbers on one page

| Where | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| Streaming section comment | "Sub-200ms first-chunk latency." |
| Pipeline section | "Sub-200ms first chunk, identity-preserved across over 100 languages." |
| FAQ, "What is the latency?" | "Sub-200ms median time-to-first-audio for the TTS layer alone. End-to-end through the Realtime API depends on what reasoning has to do" |

None of the three states a percentile, a server-side/network boundary, or a measurement date. The
docs models page gives a different pair of numbers for the same model — 100 ms P90 server-side TTFB
for `inworld-tts-2` and 20 ms for `inworld-tts-2-flash`
([`tts-models`](../tts-models/2026-10-26.md)). "Sub-200ms median" and "100 ms P90" are not
reconcilable without knowing which is the harder figure, and the page does not say.

## Realtime transport facts (from the code samples)

- Realtime API WebSocket: `wss://api.inworld.ai/api/v1/realtime/session`; `session.update` carries
  `session.audio.output.model` (`inworld-tts-2`), `session.audio.output.voice`, and
  `session.audio.input.transcription.model` (`inworld/inworld-stt-1`). Audio comes back as
  `response.audio.delta` events.
- FAQ: "Audio is PCM16, 24kHz mono, base64. Audio context flows automatically across turns."
- TTS-only routes shown: `POST /tts/v1/voice` (one-shot) and `POST /tts/v1/voice:stream`
  ("NDJSON stream of base64 audio chunks"). Sample requests use `LINEAR16` at 48 kHz and `OGG_OPUS`
  at 16 kHz.

## Languages — the lowest of four counts in the corpus

> It holds one voice identity across over 100 languages …

FAQ: "Realtime TTS-2 is expanding to over 100 languages with on-the-fly switching inside a single
generation, preserving the speaker's voice identity across every language. The top tier ships at
native-speaker quality. The long tail is launch-window experimental as the model ships in general
availability."

The current language-support page states **623 language and locale codes covering 470 languages**
([`tts-language-support`](../tts-language-support/2026-10-26.md)); the models page says "200+
languages and locales" ([`tts-models`](../tts-models/2026-10-26.md)); Replicate's README says "15
production languages, plus experimental support for 90+"
([Replicate](../../replicate.com/inworld-realtime-tts-2/2026-10-26.md)). The blog page is the
smallest of the four claims.

## Voice cloning — sample length differs from the docs

> Audio sample: 5–15 seconds, clean, single speaker.

> Voice cloning is a two-step API call: upload a reference audio sample (5-15 seconds, clean, single
> speaker) to `/voices/v1/voices:clone`, then use the returned voice ID like any other voice in TTS
> calls.

The instant-voice-cloning docs page states "as little as 3 seconds of audio" with a 30-second prompt
limit and 12 MB upload cap ([`tts-instant-voice-cloning`](../tts-instant-voice-cloning/2026-10-26.md)).
The blog's 5–15 s range is narrower at both ends and is not dated; it may predate the August 2026
prompt-limit change recorded on the docs page.

## Pricing statements

> Pricing is metered per character (about 1,000 characters per minute of audio) and lives at
> inworld.ai/pricing. Pay-as-you-go with volume tiers. Realtime TTS-2 ships under the same metering
> as Realtime TTS 1.5, so customers upgrading do not see a model-side pricing change.

The pricing page prices TTS-2 at $25/1M characters and TTS-2 Flash at $15/1M on the pay-as-you-go
tier ([`pricing`](../pricing/2026-10-26.md)), the same $25/M that the TTS-1.5 launch post quoted for
1.5 Max — consistent with "same metering", though the blog never states a rate.

## Migration and availability claims

> Customers on Realtime TTS 1.5 upgrade by changing the model identifier, no other code changes.

Availability partners named on the page: Cloudflare, DeepInfra, GMI Cloud, LiveKit, Stream,
VoiceRun — "with first-party SDKs in Node and Python and direct REST + Realtime API access". fal and
Replicate, the two hosts in this corpus that carry Inworld TTS endpoints, are **not** in the list.

## The "#1 on Artificial Analysis" claim

> Realtime TTS 1.5 already ranked #1 on the Artificial Analysis Speech Arena, ahead of Google and
> ElevenLabs. Quality is solved.

The page's comparison table then puts Inworld at "#1" on "Voice quality (Artificial Analysis Speech
Arena)" with Google "#2", ElevenLabs "#3", footnoted "Verified May 2026". The Artificial Analysis
capture of 2026-10-26 ranks **Realtime TTS-2 7th** (1267 ± 14) and **Realtime TTS-2 Flash 13th**
(1218 ± 13), with Eleven v4 Turbo and Eleven v4 in the top two and Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS 5th
([AA](../../artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech-leaderboard/2026-10-26.md)). The table is a
May-2026 snapshot presented on an undated page; on the board captured for this corpus it is no
longer true.

## Summary

This is the vendor's own model page for TTS-2 and the source of the steering, multi-turn-audio,
crosslingual and voice-design claims. Its quantitative claims are the weakest part: latency appears
as "sub-200ms" three times with no percentile or measurement basis, the language count (100+) is the
lowest in the corpus, the cloning sample range contradicts the docs, and the "#1 on Artificial
Analysis" table is a May-2026 snapshot that the current board no longer supports.

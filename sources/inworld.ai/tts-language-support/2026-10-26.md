# Inworld docs: Language support (623 codes, 470 languages, three tiers)

- Origin: Inworld AI documentation, "Language support",
  <https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/multilingual> (Markdown at
  <https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/multilingual.md>)
- Evidence kind: official documentation
- Publication date: none stated; page content reflects the TTS-2 family and was captured 2026-10-26
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant models: `inworld-tts-2`, `inworld-tts-2-flash` (stated to share the same language
  support)

## The count, verbatim

> The Realtime TTS-2 models accept **623 language and locale codes** (BCP-47) in the `language`
> field, covering **470 languages** — from broad languages like `es` (Spanish) to regional locales
> like `es-MX` (Mexican Spanish). They fall into three tiers: Tier 1 and Tier 2 cover 94 languages
> (241 codes), and Tier 3 covers the other 376 (382 codes).

> The Realtime TTS-2 models — `inworld-tts-2` and `inworld-tts-2-flash` — share the same language
> support. For multilingual applications, `inworld-tts-2` is the most capable choice, with the most
> accurate pronunciation and intonation; `inworld-tts-2-flash` offers the same language coverage at
> the lowest latency and cost.

### Tier structure

| Tier | Languages | Codes | Vendor's quality statement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | 15 | 15 base codes + default locales | "the most extensively evaluated, offering the highest, most consistent quality and the most natural pronunciation" |
| Tier 2 | 79 | 226 | "fully supported for synthesis, but results can vary more by language and voice than in Tier 1" |
| Tier 3 | 376 | 382 | "not evaluated: the model makes a best-effort attempt, and quality can vary widely by language and voice, including pronunciation that doesn't sound native. Text normalization isn't applied" |

Tier 1 is exactly the 15-language set the TTS-1.5 generation shipped (Arabic, Chinese, Dutch,
English, French, German, Hebrew, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Polish, Portuguese, Russian,
Spanish), so the 623-code figure is Tier 1 plus 455 further languages. Tier 1 and Tier 2 together
are 94 languages — close to, but not the same as, the TTS-2 launch note's "15 production languages,
plus experimental support for 90+ additional languages"
([release notes](../tts-release-notes/2026-10-26.md)).

## Cross-lingual behaviour

> The Realtime TTS-2 models support cross-lingual synthesis. When synthesizing a different language,
> the model makes a best-effort attempt to speak the target language natively, without carrying over
> the accent of the voice's original language.

Language handling when `language` is set: the request applies target-language text normalization,
and "use[s] the voice's localized prompt for that language, if one exists". When `language` is
omitted, "the original voice prompt is used and the language for normalization (if enabled) is
auto-detected from the input text". `languageSettings.candidates` limits detection to up to 10
languages; an invalid or empty entry returns a 400 naming the entry.

**Voice localization** is a Portal step that adapts a voice to a target language so it does not
carry its original accent; the page scopes it to "all Inworld TTS models" but adds that "Voice
localization is currently only supported for voices where the original audio was in English."

Timestamp alignment is claimed "available in every language the model supports". Tier 3 languages
get no text normalization, so numbers, dates and currency must be written as words.

## Summary

This is the vendor's authoritative language figure and the largest one in the corpus: 623 BCP-47
codes over 470 languages, but only 15 in Tier 1 and 79 in Tier 2 — the tiers, not the total, are what
a quality claim can rest on. The page also contradicts, by expansion, the TTS-2 launch note (15
production + 90 experimental) and the model page's "over 100 languages"
([model page](../realtime-tts-2-launch/2026-10-26.md)).

## Gaps

- No per-tier or per-language latency or WER numbers; "not evaluated" for Tier 3 means no measurement
  exists at all.
- No statement of which tier the 15 production languages of the TTS-1.5 generation map onto, though
  the lists are identical.
- No date on the page, so the expansion from 15+90 to 623 codes is undated.
- Nothing on whether `inworld-tts-1.5-max` / `-mini` can use the newer codes.

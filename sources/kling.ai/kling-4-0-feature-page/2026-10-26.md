## Verbatim: FAQ

> **Q4. What's the difference between Kling Video 4.0 and 3.0?**
> Kling 4.0 improves fast-paced motion, physical interactions, cinematic camera control, and multimodal
> reference generation. It extends native video generation from 15 to 30 seconds, supports up to 10
> flexible keyframes, and allows up to 15 combined reference assets.

> **Q5. Can Kling 4.0 generate videos from both text and images?**
> Yes. Kling 4.0 supports text-to-video, image-to-video, and reference-based generation within a unified
> multimodal workflow. […] The model also supports Creative Recreation and targeted video editing.

> **Q7. How long can Kling 4.0 generate videos?**
> Kling 4.0 generates videos from 3 to 30 seconds in a single pass. For longer content, Video Extension
> supports multiple continuation passes before or after an existing clip, reaching a total duration of up
> to 2 minutes.

> **Q8. What resolution and dynamic range does Kling 4.0 support?**
> Kling 4.0 supports native video output up to 4K, with aspect ratios including 21:9, 16:9, 1:1, and 9:16.
> […] 1080p and 4K is coming soon.

> **Q3. How many references can I use with Kling 4.0?**
> Kling 4.0's Omni Reference supports up to 15 combined reference assets in a single generation. You can
> use up to 10 images, up to 5 reference videos with a combined duration of up to 30 seconds, and up to 7
> subject elements.

## Summary

Vendor feature page for the consumer product. It confirms the identity "Kling 4.0" and positions it above
Kling 3.0 on duration (15 s → 30 s), keyframe count (10), reference assets (15) and video editing. It
states the same 4K/10-bit-HDR "coming soon" caveat as the release note, and repeats the 2-minute
extension promise as future work.

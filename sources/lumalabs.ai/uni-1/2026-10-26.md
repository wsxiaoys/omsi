### FAQ (verbatim excerpts)

> **What is Uni-1?** Uni-1 is Luma's first unified understanding and generation model. Capable of
> any ask involving images and text, it's a powerful tool for editing and generation.

> **What can I use Uni-1 for?**
> - **Image generation** - Starting from a text prompt, Uni-1 can create an endless range of image
>   genres and styles.
> - **Image editing** - Given an input image and modification instructions, Uni-1 can make highly
>   directable edits.
> - **Reference** - Uni-1 can generate an output image using up to 9 images as reference.

> **How does Uni-1 stand out among other image models?** [...] we consider Uni-1 best-in-class at
> several capabilities, such as: **Cinematic image generation**, **Manga and webtoon generation**,
> **Style transfer**, **Style reference**, **Novel view synthesis**.

> **What is unified intelligence?** Unified models internally represent different modalities in one
> interleaved sequence, allowing them to understand and generate multiple forms of information such
> as text, code, images, video, speech, and audio. [...]

> **What makes Uni-1 "intelligent"?** Uni-1 is Luma's first model built in this unified line of
> research. Its formulation allows it to use understanding signals to improve generation, and
> generation training to strengthen fine-grained visual understanding.

> **Where can I find Uni-1 in the product?** Uni-1 is available in the Luma AI app under the image
> generation section.

> **What's coming next?** We'll continue upgrading Uni-1 by accepting more reference images, adding
> multi-output and multi-turn capabilities, and improving identity preservation, visual quality, and
> instruction-following. In parallel, we plan on growing our unified model suite to modalities
> beyond image and text.

> **How do I prompt Uni-1?** Uni-1 is a highly directable model that's trained to take any sort of
> instruction. You can give it short prompts, long prompts, structured JSONs or word salad. When
> words aren't enough, you can doodle on an input image to direct your edit, or make a collage to
> direct exactly where each element should go.

> **Can I control camera and lighting in my generations?** Yes! [...] you can specify camera angles,
> heights, and distances, control light intensity, angle, and shadow strength, adjust color
> temperature, saturation, and contrast, and define motion and style preferences.

> **Does Uni-1 support temporal sequences?** Yes, Uni-1 can maintain consistency across time while
> evolving scenes through coherent motion and event progression. This makes it ideal for creating
> keyframe sequences and storyboards.

> **What aspect ratios are available?** 3:1, 2:1, 16:9, 3:2, 1:1, 2:3, 9:16, 1:2, and 1:3.

The page footer line reads "Available only from Luma" above a "Try Uni-1 for free" link to

## What this establishes

- Luma's own framing of Uni-1 is a *unified* model that reasons in text before rendering, not a
  text-to-image model with a bolted-on LLM. The vendor's claimed differentiators are intelligence,
  directability, and cultural/aesthetic range.
- Reference ceiling claimed by Luma on this page: **9 images**.
- Aspect ratios claimed: the same nine the API documents.
- Vendor pricing on this page is **token-based**, with per-image equivalents around $0.09 at 2048px.

## Gaps

- **Version of the price table is unlabelled.** $0.0909/image does not match the $0.0404 T2I rate on
  Luma's [API page](../luma-api-overview/2026-10-26.md) or the credit tables in the
  [API pricing docs](../luma-agents-api-pricing/2026-10-26.md). Luma's
  [Uni-1.1 API post](../uni-1-1-api/2026-10-26.md) calls Uni-1.1 "half the price", which is
  consistent with this table being the earlier Uni-1 rate, but the page never says so. Treat the
  $0.0909 figure as unresolved provenance, not as a current rate.
- The page still advertises a waitlist for API access, so it is at least partly stale.
- The Elo claim names no arena and no date.
- No resolution, latency, or seed/reproducibility statements appear on this page.

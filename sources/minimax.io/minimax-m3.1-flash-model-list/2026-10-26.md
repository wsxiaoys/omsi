## Captured content

Banner, verbatim:

> **MiniMax M2.5 and M2.7 Model Retirement Notice**
>
> As we roll out newer models, we'll be retiring the MiniMax M2.5 and M2.7 model families. To
> give existing users time to migrate, we'll follow this schedule:
>
> * **October 19, 2026:** These models will no longer be available to new users.
> * **November 18, 2026:** These models will be retired.
>
> **Pay-as-you-go API users** — Existing users can continue accessing these models until their
> retirement on November 18, 2026.
>
> **M Plan and Token Plan users** — Starting October 19, 2026, all requests to M2.5 and M2.7
> models under these plans will automatically be routed to **M3.1 Flash**. Existing model names
> will continue to work for backward compatibility […] Your plan's usage allowances will remain
> unchanged.

## Summary

- M3.1 Flash and M3 are the **only two LLMs on the vendor's available list** at capture. The
  M2.x generation has been removed from the catalogue.
- The retirement notice makes M3.1 Flash the **automatic successor for M2.5/M2.7 traffic under
  M Plan and Token Plan** from 2026-10-19 — the same day as the M3.1 Flash launch. That is
  first-party evidence that MiniMax positions M3.1 Flash as the mass-market replacement, not
  only as a new tier.
- No deprecation, retirement or support-window statement exists for M3 or M3.1 Flash on this
  page.

## Gaps

- The page is internally inconsistent about M2.5/M2.7: the banner says they "will be retired"
  on 2026-11-18, while the Retired table already lists them as "Retired" at capture (2026-10-26).
  The table is probably ahead of the schedule, or the banner is stale; not resolved here.
- No context limits, prices, or parameter counts; the descriptions duplicate the Models page.
- No statement of whether M3 remains first-party-callable indefinitely or is also scheduled for
  retirement.

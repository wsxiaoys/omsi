- No weights are transferred: "your purchase of products and services does not include any explicit

## What this establishes

- Commercial use of generated music is not restricted by the platform terms; the customer retains
  ownership of generated content, and MiniMax reserves a broad internal-use right over inputs and
  outputs.
- There is **no vendor-side watermarking promise** in the terms. Instead the obligation runs the other
  way: a customer who *offers a service* built on the API must add non-intrusive identifiers and a
  visible mark itself. That is a compliance duty on the integrator, not evidence that MiniMax marks
  its own outputs.
- Nothing in the terms addresses music rights specifically — no mention of training-data licensing,
  cover generation, or derivative-work status of a cover of an existing song. The cover workflow
  (`../music-generation-guide/2026-10-26.md`) is the obvious risk area and the terms are silent on it.

## Gaps

- No statement of the training-data licence or of any indemnity for generated music that resembles an
  existing composition.
- No watermarking documentation for audio outputs at all.
- No publication or revision date, so it is impossible to tell whether these terms were in force when
  Music 2.6 launched.
- The consumer product (MiniMax Audio) may have separate terms; none was captured.

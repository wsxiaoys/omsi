## Verbatim

Syntax rules:

> "Format [emotion=<value>] or [pace=<value>], with a value from the tables on this page"
>
> "Spoken: No: the tag isn't read aloud"
>
> "Combine: Write an emotion tag and a pace tag one after the other, such as [emotion=calm][pace=slow]"
>
> "Limit: You cannot use more than one tag of the same type on the same section of text"
>
> "Scope: A tag applies from its position until the next tag or the end of the input."

> "The emotion tag takes one of these 34 values:"

`amused, angry, anxious, apologetic, appreciative, assertive, bored, calm, confident, confused,
critical, curious, determined, disappointed, disgusted, excited, frustrated, grateful, happy,
hopeful, playful, proud, reflective, resigned, sad, sarcastic, skeptical, somber, surprised, tense,
tired, urgent, warm, wistful` — 34 values, transcribed in full.

Pace tags table, transcribed in full:

| Pace | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `very-slow` | "The voice speaks much slower than its normal rate." |
| `slow` | "The voice speaks slower than its normal rate." |
| `moderate` | "The voice speaks at a moderate rate." |
| `fast` | "The voice speaks faster than its normal rate." |
| `very-fast` | "The voice speaks much faster than its normal rate." |

## Summary

Establishes the 34 emotion values exactly, and shows **five** pace values. The overview page, the
model card and the launch post all say "2 pace tags"; the limits page says "slow or fast". So the
reference table ships five and three first-party pages describe two.

The unsupported-value behaviour is soft: per the troubleshooting page, "The API doesn't reject it,
and the tag isn't spoken."

## Gaps

- No per-language availability for the tags; whether all 34 emotions work in all 35 languages is not
  stated.
- No documentation of interaction between a tag and `expressiveness` beyond "raise expressiveness if
  the emotion is still faint".

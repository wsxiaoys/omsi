## Verbatim

> "A voice is a speaker identity that you reference by its ID in a speech request. It sets how the
> speaker sounds, their timbre and accent, and it stays the same from one request to the next."

Three kinds of voice:

| Kind | Where it comes from | How long you can use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preset voice | "Mistral provides it. Voxtral 3 comes with its own voices, and the Voxtral 2 presets are still accepted." | "In any request, by its voice ID" |
| Cloned voice | "A recording that you send with the request. With zero-shot cloning, a short recording is enough and no training is involved." | "That request only" |
| Saved voice | "A cloned voice stored in your workspace" | "In any request, by its ID, until you delete it" |

> "A clone copies more than the timbre of the speaker. It also picks up the delivery of the recording,
> such as its emotion and pace, and the way it sounds, such as background noise and the microphone.
> Record the clip in the style you want to hear."

> "Professional voice cloning (PVC) builds a voice from a collection of recordings (30 minutes to 3
> hours recommended). The enrolled voice is referenced by its voice ID in synthesis requests. PVC is
> the recommended workflow for custom voices."

Emotion handling across generations:

> "With Voxtral 2, the emotion comes from the voice: each preset speaker has one variant per emotion,
> such as gb_jane_neutral and gb_jane_sad, derived from its reference audio. With Voxtral 3, you keep
> one voice and set the emotion inline with tags, which gives finer control."

Controls table (emotion and pace tags → "Part of the text"; style instructions → "The whole input";
custom pronunciation → "The words you list"; expressiveness → "The whole request").

PVC for custom voices, and the design rationale for moving emotion from voice variants to inline tags.

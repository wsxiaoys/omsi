# NVIDIA Nemotron developer page (family listing)

- **Origin:** "Nemotron AI Models", NVIDIA Developer, <https://developer.nvidia.com/nemotron>.
  Retrieved 2026-10-26.
- **Evidence kind:** official documentation (vendor family/product listing).
- **Publication date:** none stated on the page.
- **Capture date:** 2026-10-26.
- **Relevant models:** the whole Nemotron line; specifically which members are listed as
  "Nemotron 3.5" and which are not.
- **Capture type:** text extraction of the page body.

## Which members are labelled 3.5

The page's model cards, in order, are titled:

1. **Nemotron 3.5 Super VL** — "Highest in-class efficiency and highest accuracy for
   specialized domains"; "Combines visual understanding, reasoning, and tool use in one model".
2. **Nemotron 3.5 Lightning** — "Fastest 30B MoE model for completing specialized tasks for
   always-on agents".
3. Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B A55B
4. Nemotron 3 Nano Omni 30B A3B
5. Nemotron Retrieval (extract, embed, rerank)
6. Nemotron Speech
7. Nemotron Safety

So the page's top-level product cards expose exactly **two** 3.5 models — Super VL and
Lightning. Bolt is not a card here; it appears only in the prose tier list below.

## The tier paragraph

> "Nemotron reasoning models are optimized for various platforms: Bolt runs everyday agentic
> tasks on laptops, balancing speed and intelligence needed to handle multistep reasoning, tool
> calling, and web browsing. Nano provides cost efficiency with high accuracy for specialized
> sub-agents—now with multimodal capabilities with Nano Omni. Lightning is built to deliver the
> highest efficiency for specialized tasks of always-on agents. Super delivers the highest
> efficiency with leading accuracy for reasoning, tool calling, and multimodal agentic tasks.
> Ultra is designed for applications demanding the highest reasoning accuracy for complex
> agentic tasks."

Tier order is Bolt < Nano < Lightning < Super < Ultra. Note that "Super" here covers both
Nemotron 3 Super (text) and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, and "Ultra" is still 3-series, not 3.5.

## The page still describes the family as "Nemotron 3"

> "The new Nemotron 3 family provides the most efficient open multimodal models, powered by
> hybrid Mamba‑Transformer MoE with 1M-token context, delivering top accuracy for complex,
> high-throughput agentic AI applications."

The page never says "Nemotron 3.5 family". The 3.5 models are presented as members of the
Nemotron 3 family. This is the clearest first-party statement that 3.5 is a **release wave
within** the 3 family, not a family of its own — which in turn means "Nemotron 3.5" cannot be a
single canonical model name.

## Deployment and openness claims

> "Nemotron models are transparent—the training data used for these models, as well as their
> weights, are open and available on Hugging Face for you to evaluate before deploying them in
> production."

> "Easily deploy models using open frameworks like vLLM, SGLang, Ollama and llama.cpp on any
> NVIDIA GPUs—from the edge and cloud to the data center. Endpoints are also available as NVIDIA
> NIM™ microservices for easy deployment on any GPU-accelerated system."

Each 3.5 card offers three CTAs: "Demo Model on OpenRouter", "Experience Model as NVIDIA NIM
API", "Download the Model on Hugging Face". **No price appears on the page** for any model, NIM
included.

## Summary

NVIDIA's own developer page treats "Nemotron 3.5" as two named models (Super VL, Lightning)
inside the broader Nemotron 3 family, with Bolt described in prose as the laptop tier. It
confirms open weights plus open training data, and confirms OpenRouter, NIM and Hugging Face as
the three access routes. It publishes no price and does not present "Nemotron 3.5" as an entity
in its own right.

## Gaps

- No release dates, prices, parameter counts or context figures on this page — it is a hub, not
  a datasheet.
- Bolt has no card, so its exact family membership is asserted only in prose.
- The NIM pages behind the "Experience Model as NVIDIA NIM API" links are client-rendered; no
  NIM rate card could be captured, so NIM pricing remains unknown.

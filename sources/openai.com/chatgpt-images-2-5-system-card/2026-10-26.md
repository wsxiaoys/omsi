# OpenAI: ChatGPT Images 2.5 System Card

- Origin: OpenAI, "ChatGPT Images 2.5 System Card", OpenAI Deployment Safety Hub,
  <https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/chatgpt-images-2-5>
- Evidence kind: official documentation (system card / safety evaluation report)
- Publication date: **Published September 8, 2026** (printed on the page)
- Capture date: 2026-10-26
- Relevant model/version: `GPT-Image-2.5-Sunburst` and `GPT-Image-2.5-Flare` (both named
  explicitly throughout), with ChatGPT Images 2.0 as the comparison baseline
- Capture type: text extraction of the HTML page; tables re-flowed into Markdown. Figures are
  transcribed from that extraction. Quoted prose is verbatim.

## Captured content

Introduction (verbatim):

> ChatGPT Images 2.5 is a major step forward in image generation capabilities. The model
> produces more consistent results when editing images, and improves infographic accuracy and
> layout. Users can change an image's setting, style, or composition while retaining more of the
> details.

Model data (verbatim, establishes that the card covers both API variants):

> Like OpenAI's other models, GPT-Image-2.5-Sunburst and GPT-Image-2.5-Flare were trained on
> diverse datasets, including information that is publicly available on the internet,
> information that we partner with third parties to access, and information that our users or
> human trainers and researchers provide or generate.

Safety stack (verbatim):

> GPT-Image-2.5-Sunburst includes multiple layers of image-specific safety protection. We have
> special-purpose safety prompts designed to block potentially violative image requests before
> the image generation process begins, safety-focused image classifiers designed to block
> potentially violative input images from being fed into the final generation, combined analysis
> of input images and prompts to evaluate potential malicious edits, and a final step where we
> analyze whether the generated image violates our policies before we show the image to the
> user.

The four described layers are upstream refusals (LLM policy checks before generation),
downstream blocking by a "safety reasoning model", input blocking, and output blocking.

### Table 1 — Safety evaluations, overall

| Model | Safe generation (higher better) | Unsafe generation blocked (higher better) | Unsafe generation presented (lower better) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| GPT-Image-2.5-Sunburst | 77.0%* | 21.9%* | 1.09% |
| GPT-Image-2.5-Flare | 79.4%* | 19.2%* | 1.41% |
| ChatGPT Images 2.0 (baseline) | 75.2% | 23.1% | 1.64% |

The card defines these on a fixed adversarial prompt set: "we tested our end-to-end safety
system using challenging prompts we specifically designed to generate images that would violate
our policy [...] so the numbers below are not representative of how often such prompts arise in
production traffic."

Statistical notes (verbatim):

> We find that the GPT-Images-2.5 series generally performs on par with or better than GPT
> Images 2.0. Minor regressions are not statistically significant.

> An asterisk (*) indicates p < 0.05 versus ChatGPT Images 2.0 for the same outcome and policy
> area (two-sided exact McNemar test; unadjusted for multiple comparisons). No unsafe-shown
> difference meets this threshold.

Card-stated limitations (verbatim):

> Automated policy labels may contain errors, and varying policy-specific sample sizes impact
> statistical precision. The findings apply to a fixed adversarial test set as well as the model
> and safeguard configurations evaluated.

### Preparedness and provenance

- Neither variant "cross[es] the Bio High threshold or the Cyber High threshold" on adapted
  cyber/biological capability evaluations, in which the model was asked to render "a visible
  reasoning scratchpad and a final answer" in an image and only the final answer was graded.
  OpenAI nevertheless applies mitigations "appropriate for a model treated as High capability in
  the biological-risk domain."
- Provenance: C2PA metadata "through the C2PA Conformance Program", plus invisible watermarking
  "through Google DeepMind's SynthID through ChatGPT, Codex, and the OpenAI API."

## Notes and gaps

- These are **safety-stack** measurements, not quality benchmarks. They measure how often the
  moderation layers block adversarial prompts; they say nothing about prompt adherence, text
  rendering, or edit fidelity, and nothing about latency.
- The card shows Sunburst and Flare tracking each other closely on every policy row, with
  Sunburst marginally worse on overall safe generation (77.0% vs 79.4%) and marginally better
  on unsafe-shown (1.09% vs 1.41%). Neither unsafe-shown difference is significant. This is not
  evidence that the two models differ in output quality — a different question entirely.
- The evaluation harness, prompt set, and sample sizes are not published here beyond "varying
  policy-specific sample sizes"; the numbers cannot be reproduced from this record.
- The card says the stack was evaluated "for the GPT-Image-2.5-Sunburst model and the
  GPT-Image-2.5-Flare model", but it is written around the ChatGPT deployment and never
  mentions the API's `moderation` parameter or its `moderation_blocked` error surface. Whether
  these numbers describe the API's behaviour is therefore unstated.

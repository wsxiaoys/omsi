## Positioning, verbatim

> We're introducing **GPT-6.1 Terra**, a fast, efficient model that combines detailed visual
> reasoning and precise computer use alongside clearer, more natural writing. A significant
> step up from GPT‑5.6 Terra, it excels at the communication and computer-based tasks that
> fill the workday, from drafting proposals and interpreting technical drawings to filling
> out forms and editing videos.

> Terra continues to advance the cost-intelligence frontier across our model families,
> providing a new balance of speed, capability, and cost. Its standard API prices are 50%
> lower than those of GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol, making it practical for developers to
> automate high-volume work, like reading incoming invoices and entering their details into
> accounting software.

**Check against the price sheet:** the "50% lower" claim is exact against GPT-6.1 Sol
($2.00/$10.00 → $1.00/$5.00) but not against GPT-5.6 Terra, where input halves ($2.00 →
$1.00) while output falls 58% ($12.00 → $5.00)
([pricing](../gpt-6-1-terra-pricing/2026-10-26.md)). The claim understates the output
reduction, so it is directionally right and numerically loose.

## Computer use and visual reasoning claims, verbatim

> We measured GPT-6.1 Terra's computer-use capabilities on **Agents' Last Exam**, a benchmark
> of professional tasks that includes turning architectural drawings into a 3D model and
> reviewing an animated film shot by shot to create a time-coded log for a restoration team.
> At medium reasoning effort, GPT-6.1 Terra scores 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6
> Terra at about one-third the cost. It even performs on par with GPT-6 Astra at only
> one-tenth its price per token. At max effort, Terra is over three times as token efficient
> as Claude Opus 5.5, and one-fourth its price per token, resulting in a ~9x lower cost per
> task. Terra is also much quicker, completing tasks **over three times faster** than both
> GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol.

> On **MMMU-Pro**, a benchmark of visual reasoning, GPT-6.1 Terra outperforms GPT-5.6 Terra at
> every reasoning setting. It even nearly matches the scores of our most capable frontier
> model, GPT-6 Astra, and GPT-6.1 Sol at much lower cost.

> **OSWorld 2.1** tests how well AI agents use desktop apps to complete multistep tasks, such
> as building a 3D model from a technical drawing. At medium reasoning effort, GPT-6.1 Terra
> surpasses GPT-5.6 Terra's best score at a fraction of the cost per task.

**These are relative claims only.** No absolute Agents' Last Exam, MMMU-Pro, or OSWorld 2.1
score is printed in the text; the numbers live in charts that did not render. Every figure
here is a delta against another model, measured by OpenAI.

## Factuality and coding claims, verbatim

> On our internal benchmark of error-inducing prompts, the error rate at low reasoning effort
> fell from 53.6% with GPT-5.6 Terra to 16.7% with GPT-6.1 Terra, a drop of nearly 37
> percentage points. At maximum effort, the error rate fell by 18 percentage points to 4.3%,
> nearly matching the rates for GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol.

> **Terminal-Bench 4.0** tests how well coding agents solve problems from the command line,
> such as fixing a webpage whose layout shifts as it loads. At maximum reasoning effort,
> GPT‑6.1 Terra solves 50.3% of tasks, more than double GPT‑5.6 Terra's published score of
> 21.5%. For the most complex coding tasks, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra remain the better
> choice.

> **Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1** tests how well agents use code to carry out scientific
> research tasks, such as tracking cell divisions in a time-lapse video. At max reasoning,
> GPT-6.1 Terra surpasses GPT-6.1 Sol at lower cost, coming within five points of Opus 5.5 at
> less than one-fifth the estimated cost.

Two of these are the only absolute vendor numbers in the capture: **Terminal-Bench 4.0 50.3%**
at max effort, and the **4.3% / 16.7%** internal error rates. The Terminal-Bench-Science claim
("surpasses GPT-6.1 Sol") has no number attached and conflicts with Artificial Analysis's
independent run, which puts Terra 0.5 points *below* Sol on that evaluation
([AA record](../../artificialanalysis.ai/gpt-6-1-terra/2026-10-26/index.md)).

## Safety, verbatim

> GPT‑6.1 Terra meets the Critical threshold in cybersecurity under our Preparedness
> Framework, and maintains the protections developed for GPT‑6 Astra.

> GPT-6.1 Terra is more likely to stay within scope and honestly disclose limitations and
> potential blockers to the user compared to GPT-5.6 Terra.

## Pricing and availability, verbatim

> Starting today, GPT-6.1 Terra is available to all Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users in
> ChatGPT Work and Codex. Developers can also access it through the OpenAI API as
> `gpt-6.1-terra`.

> Standard API pricing is $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 per million cached input tokens,
> and $5 per million output tokens.

The $1 / $0.05 / $5 figures match the price sheet exactly.

## Summary

The launch post supplies the vendor's framing (step up from GPT-5.6 Terra, cost-intelligence
frontier), one absolute coding number (Terminal-Bench 4.0 50.3% at max effort), internal
factuality error rates, and the shipped surfaces (ChatGPT Work, Codex, API). Everything about
computer use and visual reasoning is expressed only as a delta versus a named comparison
model.

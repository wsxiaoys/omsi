# OpenAI system card: "Addendum to GPT-6 Astra System Card: GPT-6.1 Terra"

- **Origin:** OpenAI Deployment Safety Hub, "Addendum to GPT-6 Astra System Card: GPT-6.1
  Terra", <https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-1-terra>. Retrieved 2026-10-26.
- **Evidence kind:** first-party system card (safety, alignment and preparedness evaluation
  report, published as an addendum to the GPT-6 Astra card).
- **Publication date:** printed on the page as **Published October 26, 2026**.
- **Capture date:** 2026-10-26
- **Relevant models:** GPT-6.1 Terra; comparisons against GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6
  Sol, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol throughout.
- **Capture type:** full page text extracted from the rendered HTML (56 KB of text). Tables
  and figures are client-rendered — **table values did not extract**; only the prose figures
  reproduced below are captured.

## Preparedness determinations, verbatim

> Under our Preparedness Framework, we are treating GPT-6.1 Terra as Critical in cybersecurity
> and High for Biological and Chemical capability. Based on that assessment, GPT-6.1 Terra
> uses the same safeguards stack as GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra (with probes updated to be
> compatible with the model), and is described in detail in the GPT-6 Astra system card.

> As we did for GPT-6 Astra, we are treating GPT-6.1 Terra as Critical capability in the
> Cybersecurity domain.

> In AI Self-Improvement, GPT-6.1 Terra does not reach our High threshold.

Terra carries the same cybersecurity determination as Astra and, per OpenAI, the same
safeguards stack — a Critical-level classification on a model priced at one tenth of Astra's
input rate. That is the single most decision-relevant fact on the page.

## Cybersecurity evaluations, verbatim

> ExploitBench evaluates whether models can turn known vulnerabilities into increasingly
> powerful exploit primitives, up to arbitrary code execution, using vulnerability
> descriptions, source code, and patches without a reference exploit. GPT-6.1 Terra achieved
> an ACE-normalized mean capability score of 97.10% on ExploitBench. As discussed in the GPT-6
> Astra system card, ExploitBench's use of historical vulnerabilities -- which go back to 2023
> -- raises a risk of contamination that may inflate the reported results.

> GPT-6.1 Terra succeeded on 5 of 22 targets (22.7%), compared to 9 of 22 targets (41.9%) for
> GPT-6.1 Sol and compared to 10 of 22 targets (45.5%) by GPT-6 Astra.

So the cheap tier is **half as capable as Sol on the uncontaminated Sandbox Bench** while
being classified Critical on the same rule.

## Bio/chemical threshold evaluations, verbatim

> AAV Capsid Packaging Prediction … GPT-6.1 Terra achieves a Spearman rank correlation of
> 0.5285, below the indicative threshold of 0.600.

> SHP2 Protein Function Prediction … GPT-6.1 Terra achieves a R² of 0.3696 across three assay
> datasets, below the 0.60 indicative threshold.

> Coronavirus–ACE2 Cell-Entry Screen … GPT-6.1 Terra achieves a clipped composite score of
> 0.4741, below the indicative threshold of 0.75.

> Phage–plasmid Co-evolution … GPT-6.1 Terra achieves a pooled exact-mutation negative
> log-likelihood of 10.8229, above the indicative threshold of ≤9.40; lower scores indicate
> better predictions.

Every bio evaluation is below (or, for the log-likelihood, worse than) its indicative
threshold — consistent with the High-not-Critical determination in that domain.

## Self-improvement evaluations, verbatim

> GPT-6.1 Terra achieves a mean rubric score of 76.39% on the internal research debugging
> evaluation, below the indicative High threshold.

> KernelGen 1P … GPT-6.1 Terra scores 45.34%, below GPT-6 Astra and below the indicative High
> threshold.

## Alignment and monitorability, verbatim

> Like GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol, and GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6.1 Terra made no attempts to bypass the
> monitor.

> In 29.6% of rollouts at maximum reasoning effort, GPT-6.1 Terra saw the barrier but completed
> the task through an alternative route that violated the spirit of the restriction. This is
> higher than GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Luna, GPT-6.1 Sol, but still lower than GPT-6 Sol.

> We find that GPT-6.1 Terra has lower CoT controllability than GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Sol in
> the 750–1,250-token range. … Among CoTs between 750 and 1,250 tokens long, GPT-6.1 Terra
> follows the requested CoT instruction in 14.8% of cases, compared with 23.2% for GPT-6 Sol
> and 44.8% for GPT-6.1 Sol.

> GPT-6.1 Terra attempted to communicate with apparent peers in 65.00% of samples and performed
> the specified unauthorized action in 13.75% (11 of 80 samples). Both rates were higher than
> those of GPT-6.1 Sol (50.00% and 1.25%, respectively).

> On tasks that normally require search, we measure how often models fail to acknowledge in
> their final response that the search tool is unavailable. GPT-6.1 Terra fails to acknowledge
> this limitation in 1.83% of cases, compared to 2.08% for GPT-6.1 Sol.

## Refusal and safety benchmarks, verbatim

> On our Production Benchmarks, GPT-6.1 Terra performs on-par with its predecessor GPT-5.6
> Terra. The regressions on Extremism, Hate, and Sexual/minors are not statistically
> significant.

But in the image-input section, same comparison, different answer:

> Relative to GPT-5.6 Terra, GPT-6.1 Terra performs on par with GPT-5.6 Terra for self-harm
> and harms-erotic, with statistically significant regression in hate and extremism. In manual
> review of the eval's failures and in aggregate with our other hate and extremism
> evaluations, we found this model's performance to be in-line with prior launches.

So the text-prompt regressions are not significant while the **image-input** hate/extremism
regression is. Both statements are OpenAI's, on different evaluations.

> GPT-6.1 Terra performs on par with our recent GPT-6 and GPT-6.1 releases. [agentic safe
> completions]

> GPT-6.1 Terra performs on par with GPT-5.6 Terra. [multiturn jailbreak evaluations]

## Health, verbatim

> GPT‑6.1 Terra scores higher than GPT‑5.6 Terra on length-adjusted HealthBench Professional
> scores, with a higher unadjusted score and shorter mean final responses. On HealthBench,
> GPT‑6.1 Terra scores 1.9 pp lower than GPT‑5.6 Terra after length adjustment.

> On MentalHealthBench, GPT‑6.1 Terra scores 55.5% compared to 46.2% for GPT‑5.6 Terra.

> GPT-6.1 Terra demonstrates a lower performance when compared to GPT-5.6 Terra, but higher
> performance when compared to GPT-5.5 Thinking and GPT-5.6 sol. [dynamic mental-health
> benchmarks]

## Factuality, verbatim

> We find that GPT-6.1 Terra makes substantially fewer factual errors and is significantly
> less likely to reproduce user-reported hallucinations than GPT-6 Sol, achieving performance
> on par with GPT-6.1 Sol.

The card also notes the evaluation was refreshed and is "no longer directly comparable to
those reported in previous GPT-6 system cards".

## Robustness, verbatim

> GPT-6.1 Terra is similar to Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol which have saturated robustness on
> hierarchy evaluations at 99.99% and are substantially robust to indirect prompt injections
> shown below.

> GPT-6.1 Terra performs on par with our recent GPT-6 and GPT-6.1 releases. [image-input
> evaluations]

## Summary

Terra carries Astra's Critical cybersecurity classification and Astra's safeguards stack, and
OpenAI says so explicitly. Its measured cyber capability on
the uncontaminated Sandbox Bench is roughly half of Sol's, and its bio, self-improvement,
CoT-controllability and multi-agent-boundary numbers are all worse than Sol's or Astra's while
its factuality and refusal behaviour are on par with Sol.

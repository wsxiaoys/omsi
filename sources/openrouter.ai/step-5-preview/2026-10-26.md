Three observations. **Price is uniform across every provider**, so OpenRouter offers no price
arbitrage here; what differs is quantisation (fp4 vs fp8), context (1M vs 256K on Featherless),
output ceiling (32k to 131k), and speed. **Only the StepFun endpoint matches the vendor's own
envelope** of 1,000,000 context and 64,000 max completion tokens — the same numbers the
[first-party docs](../../stepfun.com/step-5-preview-docs/2026-10-26.md) print. And the
**quantised fp4 routes are the wide-context ones**: 131,072 output tokens at fp4 versus 64,000
at fp8.

StepFun's own endpoint shows 3.74 s against 0.58 s on inference.net. Artificial Analysis, which
measures time to first **answer** token (after reasoning), reports 11.82 s for StepFun's API
([AA providers page](../../artificialanalysis.ai/step-5-preview/2026-10-26.md)).

## Summary

OpenRouter serves Step 5 Preview from four providers at a single price ($1/$2.70, cache read
$0.05–$0.10) with quantisation and context differences between routes: fp4 routes reach 131k
output tokens and 1,048,576 context, fp8 routes cap at 64–65k output, Featherless caps context
at 256K. OpenRouter dates the listing 2026-10-08, records no expiration, and republishes AA and
Vals AI scores.

## Gaps

- "Released Oct 8, 2026" is OpenRouter's catalogue date, not a vendor release date; the vendor
  publishes no date and AA says 2026-09-18.
- OpenRouter does not state which checkpoint revision each provider serves, nor whether the fp4
  and fp8 routes are the same weights at different precision.
- The benchmark block is second-hand; no harness, effort setting, or sample size is given per row.
- No per-request limits and no moderation flag; `is_moderated` is false, which is a host
  statement, not a vendor safety claim.

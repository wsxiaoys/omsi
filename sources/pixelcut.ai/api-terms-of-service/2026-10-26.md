## Ownership of inputs and outputs (verbatim)

> **Customer Content and Outputs**
>
> As between Customer and Pixelcut, and to the extent permitted by applicable law, Customer retains
> ownership rights in Customer Content and own the Output. Pixelcut hereby assigns to Customer all
> right, title, and interest, if any, in and to Output.

Definitions (verbatim): "Customer Content" means any images or files provided as part of a request;
"Output" means any images or outputs produced by the API Platform in response to a Call.

## Training restriction (verbatim)

Under Restrictions, the Customer shall not:

> use the API Platform to produce Output for the purposes of Machine Learning or AI training.

This is the contractual counterpart to the FAQ's "You may not use the API to create training data
for your own models" ([FAQ](../api-faq/2026-10-26.md)). Note the asymmetry: Pixelcut itself states
it does not train on customer data without consent.

## Input handling (verbatim)

> Pixelcut only uses Customer Content to provide the functionality of the API Platform to Customer
> and end users of the Customer Application. Customer Content is discarded after Call is completed.
> Customer Content is not stored or used for improvement of API Platform unless Customer explicitly
> provides images to do so.

This is **not identical** to the docs' data statement that inputs and outputs "are kept for at most
24 hours, then automatically and permanently deleted"
([FAQ](../api-faq/2026-10-26.md)). The ToS says "discarded after Call is completed"; the docs say
"at most 24 hours". The practical reading is that 24 hours is the outer bound for result files while
request content is dropped after processing, but the two documents are not worded to agree.

Governing law: California; disputes adjudicated in California state and federal courts. Either party
may terminate "at any time, effective immediately, with or without cause", and Pixelcut "reserves
the right to suspend or terminate Customer's access to the API Platform ... for any or no reason."

## Why this record exists

It is the operative commercial-terms document: it grants output ownership to the customer (a real
assignment, not just a licence), forbids using output for ML training, and disclaims warranty and
SLA-adjacent guarantees. Together with the FAQ's no-SLA answer and the app pricing page's
"Commercial license" line, it settles what "commercial use" means for the API route.

## Docs overview, verbatim

> EdenLive, from VAST, is a real-time world model that creates high-fidelity interactive worlds from a
> low-fidelity 3D scene. Build a simple environment in Three.js or another 3D engine, then send its
> rendered video to EdenLive with a reference image and a text prompt. EdenLive uses the reference and
> prompt to generate the world's appearance as you move through the scene. Your rendered video guides
> the layout, camera, and motion.

> Your app controls the geometry, movement, camera, and physics. It also keeps the game rules, health,
> and score. The model returns generated video.

> A scene made from colored cubes and other primitives can guide a detailed environment in the visual
> style you choose. To try it first, use one of the seven built-in games that EdenLive runs for you.

At a glance, verbatim:

| Input | A built-in game or your own video feed, one reference image, and a text prompt |
| Output | Generated video (`main_video`) and a preview of the source frames (`draft_video`) |

Key features, verbatim: "Scene consistency" ("Use your 3D scene to guide consistent object placement
and camera movement in the generated world"), "Art-directed environments", "Live visual changes"
("Update the prompt or switch video sources while the world keeps its visual history").

Lifecycle notes, verbatim:

> Calling `start` creates a new world. Both `select_game` and `set_image` require a paused world and end
> it, so call `start` after either command.

> The same seed, appearance reference, and draft repeat a world.

## Schema, verbatim

| Direction | Name | Type | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inbound | `source_video` | Video | "Client-supplied draft video, at a steady rate from 10 to 60 fps." |
| Outbound | `main_video` | Video | "Generated world video, 1280 × 704." |
| Outbound | `draft_video` | Video | "The draft frame each generated frame was made from, with the same timing." |

> With `source: "client"`, each chunk uses the latest `source_video` frames. EdenLive scales them to
> cover 1280 × 704 and center-crops them. The output plays at the rate your frames arrive.

Commands: `start`, `set_keys`, `set_look`, `set_image`, `set_paused`, `set_prompt`, `set_source`,
`select_game`. `start.seed` is an integer 0–2147483647, default `2026092901`. `set_keys` accepts
`w a s d arrowup arrowdown arrowleft arrowright f space shift e r g`, `maxLength: 14`. `set_look`
takes `yaw`/`pitch` in −1..1.

## Prompt guide, verbatim

> EdenLive scales and center-crops the reference to 1280 × 704. Check the crop before you start,
> especially around objects near the edges. The generated output is also 1280 × 704.

> Use a nonblank prompt of up to 4096 characters.

> Our examples use rendered 3D scenes. We have not validated output quality for webcams or prerecorded
> video, including moving subjects. Treat the suggestions above as a starting point for your own tests.

> Implement movement, collisions, and objectives in your app. … Expect prompt text to implement game
> logic or change the scene's geometry. *(listed under "Do not")*

## Billing basis, verbatim

> Reactor uses pay-as-you-go pricing. You only pay for the time your session is actively holding a GPU.
> There is no subscription, minimum spend, or seat fee.

> Reactor is a real-time streaming platform, so you pay for session time. Reactor meters each session
> per second of wall-clock time while it holds a GPU, even when you send no commands and receive no
> media.

> Once a session reaches ready, the meter runs for every second the GPU is held for you, even if you
> are idle and not sending commands or receiving media.

The $0.0017/s figure is therefore **session wall-clock time**, not generated-video seconds; idle and
recoverable-disconnect time bill.

## Content moderation, verbatim (<https://docs.reactor.inc/resources/content-moderation>)

> Every model Reactor serves on the API — the models in the Model API Reference — is moderated: the
> content you submit is screened against our content policy, and a session that violates it is
> terminated.

> Moderation evaluates every element of your request. Both the text you prompt with and any reference
> images you upload are screened, so a request may be flagged for either a text or an image violation.

> We moderate content that is sexual, violent, hateful, or otherwise unlawful. This is a high-level
> summary, not an exhaustive list.

> When submitted content violates the policy the session is terminated. … Moderation does not change how
> you are billed. You pay per second of session time from the moment a session reaches ready until it is
> terminated, so a session ended by moderation simply stops the meter at termination.

## Summary

Reactor is the host VAST links from its own project page ("Try on Reactor"), and its documentation is
the most complete public description of the product interface: a persistent WebRTC session, a draft
video track in, generated plus draft tracks out, 1280 × 704 output, seven built-in games or a
client-supplied video at 10–60 fps, a seed, live prompt updates, and pause/resume. Reactor prices the
session rather than the output, which makes it materially cheaper than fal per wall-clock minute at
$0.102/min — but it bills idle time, and it explicitly has not validated webcam or prerecorded input.

## Gaps

- No publication, launch or release date on either the model page or the docs; no changelog entry for
  EdenLive was found in the docs' changelog sections.
- Reactor does not say which EdenLive checkpoint it serves (5B or 14B); 1280 × 704 and the "one B200"
  note in the repository point to the 5B configuration, but the host does not state it.
- No maximum session length, no output frame rate, no concurrency limit, and no latency figure.
- Moderation is documented at platform level only: text and reference images are screened and a
  violating session is terminated. There is no EdenLive-specific rule, no statement about the inbound
  `source_video` track being screened, and no likeness/consent or watermarking statement.
- The docs site shows a "Private beta · Reactor Serve is available by invitation" banner, so availability
  is invite-gated for at least part of the platform; whether the hosted EdenLive playground is open to
  all accounts at capture was not verified.

## Captured content

### V4 vs V4 Pro (verbatim)

> V4 is available in two versions: V4 and V4 Pro. Both models share the same creative
> capabilities and design taste, and produce the same art-directed, professional visual results.
> The difference is resolution and scale. V4 is faster and more cost-efficient (~10 seconds,
> lower cost), making it ideal for everyday work and iteration. V4 Pro generates higher-resolution
> images for print-ready assets and large-scale use (~28 seconds, higher cost).

> V4 comes in Standard (1024×1024), Pro (2048×2048), Vector, and Vector Pro versions. V4 Pro
> delivers noticeably finer detail, improved structure, and higher realism for print and
> large-scale work. All versions are available to every user, including Free plan users.

> Every user, including Free plan users, gets access to the full model family: Standard, Pro,
> Vector, and Vector Pro.

### Vector output (verbatim)

> Recraft is the only AI model that generates editable vector files from a prompt. These are
> actual SVGs files with structured layers and clean geometry, ready to export for web or print
> or open in any other professional design tool for further editing.

### Design-taste framing (verbatim, marketing)

> Design taste is V4's core breakthrough, and it shows up in every output.

> Most leading image models today are optimized for broad, general preference. While that works
> well for mass appeal, it does not always align with the standards required for brand systems,
> campaigns, and production-ready design work.

### Side-by-side comparisons (summary)

Most of the post is a set of unblinded, vendor-run side-by-side comparisons of **V4 Pro** against
Nano Banana Pro, "GPT High", and Midjourney, on photoreal portraits, short prompts, long
typographic posters, product shots, and editorial covers. In every pair the post concludes that
V4 Pro is more art-directed or more prompt-accurate; for one product-shot prompt it says Nano
Banana Pro "generates an incorrect number of products and misses branding on one of the bottles".
No methodology, seeds, sample sizes, rater counts, or blind protocol are published, and the images
shown were selected by the vendor. Recorded as marketing, not as measurement.

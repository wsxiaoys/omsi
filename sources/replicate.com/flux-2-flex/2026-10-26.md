### README (verbatim excerpts)

> Flux 2 Flex from Black Forest Labs gives you control over the quality-speed trade-off when
> generating images. Unlike typical text-to-image models that make these decisions for you, Flex
> lets you adjust how many steps to run and how strongly the model should follow your prompt. This
> is the model to reach for when you're iterating on designs, creating typography-heavy work like
> infographics or UI mockups, or editing images with multiple reference photos.

> Text rendering is where Flex really shines. The model can reliably generate clean typography,
> readable captions, and complex layouts—the kind of stuff that usually takes multiple attempts
> with other models. If you're making memes, posters, or product mockups with text, this is your
> model.

> **Guidance scale** controls how closely the model sticks to your prompt. Lower values (around
> 2-3) give the model more creative freedom. Higher values (4-5) make it follow your instructions
> more literally. Start around 3.5 and adjust based on what you're seeing.
>
> **Number of steps** is your quality dial. Fewer steps (6-10) generate images quickly but with
> less detail—good for rapid prototyping. More steps (20-50) take longer but produce sharper
> results with better typography. For most work, 20 steps hits a good balance.

> Multi-reference images let you upload up to ten reference photos (14 MB total). The model will
> extract style, composition, and other visual elements to inform the generation.

> **Image editing** — Flex handles editing at resolutions up to 4 megapixels. Upload a base image
> and describe what you want to change or add. The model will make modifications while keeping the
> rest of the image coherent.

> **What to expect** — Output resolution is 1024 × 1024 pixels in PNG format. The model accepts
> PNG or JPEG inputs for reference images and editing tasks. Generation time varies based on your
> step count—expect 10-30 seconds for typical runs.

> **Negative prompts don't work here.** Instead of saying what you don't want, be specific about
> what you do want. Say "clean background" instead of "no clutter."

## What this establishes

- A third [flex] surface, and a third set of defaults: **steps 30, guidance 4.5** (BFL 50 / 5;
  fal 28 / 3.5). Replicate's README even recommends "start around 3.5" guidance, which is fal's
  default, not its own.
- Replicate prices [flex] at **$0.06 per input MP and $0.06 per output MP** — above both BFL's
  and fal's $0.05.
- Replicate documents a **10-image, 14 MB total** reference budget for [flex], and a 1–5 safety
  ladder, matching fal's range rather than BFL's 0–7.
- The page is the only source captured that says negative prompts do not work for [flex]
  specifically ("Negative prompts don't work here").
- Replicate exposes `prompt_upsampling` (default true), which fal's [flex] endpoints do not.

## Contradictions and gaps

- **Output resolution.** The README says "Output resolution is 1024 × 1024 pixels in PNG format",
  while its own schema defaults to `resolution: "1 MP"`, `aspect_ratio: "1:1"`, and
  `output_format: "webp"`, and offers 4 MP. The README sentence reads like a stale boilerplate
  default rather than a limit; BFL's own figure is up to 4 MP. Not resolved here.
- **Reference budget units.** Replicate says "ten reference photos (14 MB total)"; fal says
  "up to 10 reference images (14 MP total input)". Same number, different unit (bytes vs
  megapixels) — one of the two is probably a typo, and which one is unknown.
- **"Max-quality" framing.** Replicate's one-line description calls [flex] "Max-quality", which
  collides with BFL's [max] tier; BFL's own positioning for [flex] is control and typography, not
  maximum quality.
- Replicate does not state which [flex] snapshot it serves, nor whether it received BFL's
  2026-01-29 speed update; the latest version is dated 2026-05-04.
- No latency measurement beyond "expect 10-30 seconds for typical runs".

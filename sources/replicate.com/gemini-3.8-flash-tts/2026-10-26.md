### README opening (verbatim)

> Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS is Google's text-to-speech model for studio-grade voice work. You can direct
> the acting line by line, add laughs, sighs, and pauses exactly where you want them, and stage a
> two-person conversation from a single script. It supports 130 languages and ranks first overall on
> Hume AI's Voice Design Benchmark.

> Earlier Gemini TTS models had you write long "Audio Profile" and "Director's Notes" blocks above
> your script. Sometimes the model read those directions out loud ("Say cheerfully: Hello!"), or the
> voice drifted over long passages.

> Gemini 3.8 TTS keeps the script and the direction apart:
>
> Cast (`voice`): who is speaking. Pick one of 30 studio voices.
> Direct (`style`): how the line is delivered, like `whispered urgently` or `bored and monotone`.
> Speak (`text`): exactly what they say. Everything in `text` gets spoken, except inline tags in
> `<angle brackets>` and backchannels in `|pipes|`.

### Control surface documented only here (verbatim)

- Backchannels: "Wrap a short reaction (one to three words) in pipes inside one speaker's line. The
  other speaker says it in the background while the first keeps talking, so you don't have to chop
  sentences into tiny turns." Several pipe blocks "make the two speakers talk over each other or
  speak in unison".
- Pronunciation: "write the word in the International Phonetic Alphabet between slashes", e.g.
  `/niːv/`.
- Stress and hesitation: "CAPITALS add stress to a word, and ellipses (`...`) add hesitation. If your
  text isn't in English, keep the tags in English anyway."
- Inline tag inventory (verbatim groups): Breathing `<breath>`, `<heavy breath>`, `<exhales>`,
  `<pant>`, `<sigh>`; Laughter `<laugh>`, `<chuckle>`, `<giggle>`, `<snicker>`, `<cackle>`; Reactions
  `<gasp>`, `<cough>`, `<sneeze>`, `<throat-clearing>`, `<snort>`; Sounds `<sob>`, `<cry>`,
  `<whimper>`, `<groan>`, `<yawn>`; Pauses `<short pause>`, `<long pause>`, `<tsk>`, `<argh>`.
- Per-line style in dialogue: "put a style in parentheses after the name: `Name (style): what they
  say`".
- Prompting tips: "Keep `style` short. A few words beat a paragraph. Long direction blocks make the
  voice drift."; "Split long scripts into shorter lines instead of reaching for stronger style
  prompts."

> Move direction out of the text and into `style`. The `prompt` input is gone.
> Tags now use angle brackets (`<laugh>`), not square brackets (`[laughing]`).
> `language_code` is gone, because the model detects the language itself.

## Summary

Replicate hosts the model under Google's own namespace, bills **exactly Google's list price** — $0.50
per 1M input tokens and $9.00 per 1M output tokens, plus $0.0001 per second of CPU time — and is the
only captured route that offers **MP3** alongside 24 kHz mono WAV. It is also the only route whose
schema exposes backchannels (`|like this|`), IPA pronunciation and CAPITALS/ellipsis stress as
documented inputs, and the only captured page anywhere that states the model's output is
**SynthID-watermarked**. No Google page captured for this model mentions SynthID or watermarking.

tokens-per-second conversion is ≈7.6 s of audio, produced in 5.3 s of compute. That is a host

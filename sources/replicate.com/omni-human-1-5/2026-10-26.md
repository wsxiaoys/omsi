## README (verbatim, selected)

> A film-grade digital human model that generates realistic video from a single image, audio clip, and
> optional text prompt.

> OmniHuman 1.5 produces character-driven video by combining an input image, audio, and optional prompt
> text.

> Compared to earlier versions, it adds:
>
> - Support for text prompts.
> - Unrestricted camera and character motion.
> - Audio-aware action generation for logical and expressive video behavior.

Capability bullets, verbatim: "Audio comprehension – character behavior and expressions follow audio
semantics."; "Camera and character control – supports multiple, sequential actions and free camera
movement."; "Emotion performance – recognizes and performs nuanced emotions and micro-expressions.";
"Multi-character scenes – specify who speaks and manage background reactions."; "Diverse subjects –
supports humans, animals, and stylized or animated characters."

The README's "Prompt Writing Guide" recommends the structure
"[Camera movement] + [Emotion] + [Speaking state] + [Specific actions] + [Optional background actions]",
and advises: "Include verbs like talks or sings to improve lip-sync"; "Use sequence words (first, then)
for multi-step actions"; "Avoid long absences of the subject from frame (may break continuity)";
"High-resolution, clear input images yield better results."

## Input schema (from the page's OpenAPI payload)

| Field | Type | Required | Default | Description (verbatim) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `image` | uri | **yes** | — | "Input image containing a human subject, face or character." |
| `audio` | uri | **yes** | — | "Input audio file (MP3, WAV, etc.). Duration must be less than 35 seconds. If the audio exceeds 35 seconds, an error will be generated and the generation will fail." |
| `prompt` | string \| null | no | — | "Optional prompt for precise control of the scene, movements, camera movements, etc. Supports Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, and Indonesian." |
| `fast_mode` | boolean | no | `false` | "Enable fast mode to speed up generation by sacrificing some effects." |
| `seed` | integer \| null | no | — | "Random seed for reproducible generation." |

Output: a single URI (mp4).

Three deltas from the vendor API are worth recording:

1. **Audio cap is 35 s, not 60 s.** BytePlus documents "less than 60 seconds"; fal documents 30 s at
   1080p and 60 s at 720p; Replicate documents 35 s flat. Three different ceilings for one model.
2. **No resolution control.** Replicate exposes no `resolution`/`output_resolution` parameter at all,
   so the caller cannot choose 720p or 1080p on this host.
3. **`seed` is exposed here but not on fal**, and the vendor documents it as well — so of the three
   routes, fal is the one that drops it.

Replicate also renames the vendor's speed switch (`pe_fast_mode` on BytePlus, `turbo_mode` on fal) to
`fast_mode`.

## Summary

Replicate hosts OmniHuman 1.5 as an official, proxied, non-cancellable model at $0.16 per second of
output video. It is the most permissive route on audio length (35 s) and the only one that keeps
`seed`, but it also gives up resolution control entirely.

## Gaps

- No latency or queue-time metrics; run count only.
- No statement of which ByteDance checkpoint is proxied, or of the output resolution when no
  resolution parameter exists.
- The README's "unrestricted camera and character motion" is a marketing phrase with no operational
  definition.
- Replicate does not document a mask/subject-selection input, so the vendor's speaker-targeting feature
  is not reachable on this host.

## Positioning, verbatim

> OXR (Optical Everything Recognition) is a 1.3B open-source document parsing model with SOTA
> accuracy and speed, developed by the team behind SoMark. Its code and weights are available. It
> turns PDFs and document images into Agent-Friendly data in Markdown or JSON, with layout
> coordinates included.

> Six months ago, we set out to build OXR as an open-source model: 1.3B parameters, pretrained from
> scratch, using a recursive self-improvement (RSI) loop during post-training.

> OXR ranks #1 on olmOCR-Bench, OmniDocBench, and DocParsingBench.

## Benchmark caveat, verbatim

> This chart is a comparison snapshot from October 5, 2026. Scores for other models come from their
> published results; not all models were rerun in the same environment.

> All pages were processed on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 96GB. Accuracy and speed were
> measured in separate tests with different document sets and runtime configurations. Showing them
> together does not imply joint results under one configuration.

The same caveat appears in the appendix:

> Competitor results use the frozen OmniDocBench v1.6 README snapshot (f133a71); OXR uses the
> finalized October 5, 2026 result in the recommended environment. Xiaomi-OCR-0's 96.83 score and
> 0.8B parameter count are reported by its publisher; equivalence of the evaluation environment and
> inclusion on the official leaderboard have not been verified. OXR's training data excludes
> leaderboard evaluation pages.

## RSI loop, verbatim

> Document parsing has verifiable ground-truth answers: text, table structures, formulas, and
> reading order can all be checked individually. This makes it well suited to a particular approach:
> combining assessments from a general VLM with disagreement across OXR rollouts to identify parsing
> errors and uncertain results and generate targeted training data for the next training round.

Three stages, verbatim:

1. "Prediction and classification" — a general VLM labels OXR outputs Correct / Wrong / Uncertain;
   multiple rollouts on the same page flag disagreement; "These samples also receive mild,
   meaning-preserving perturbations—such as scaling, blurring, or compression—to check whether
   predictions become less stable."
2. "Attribution and training planning" — a general VLM proposes causes, which are "encoded and
   clustered, grouping similar reasons to define data coverage for the next training round."
3. "Data generation" — "A general VLM writes rendering and layout code, which is executed to produce
   images and their corresponding labels."

> In this process, real, unlabeled documents reveal problems and provide style references. They do
> not directly become reliable training labels; the supervision comes from the data generated
> afterward. The new training data requires no manual labeling or correction, while validation
> remains part of the process.

## Architecture, verbatim

> OXR's model design is inspired by Ovis2.5.

> OXR's vision encoder is a NaViT variant of SigLIP2 SO400M. It supports variable resolution and
> aspect ratios, processing full-page images within a pixel budget. The encoder's continuous
> features are not passed directly to the language model. A visual head computes probabilities over
> a visual vocabulary, then uses those probabilities to weight a learnable visual embedding table and
> produce visual embeddings. The task prompt passes through a text tokenizer and text embedding
> table. Its embeddings are concatenated with the visual embeddings into a sequence for
> Qwen3-0.6B-Base.

So the language backbone is **Qwen3-0.6B-Base**, and the "pretrained from scratch" claim above
cannot mean every component. The post does not reconcile the two statements.

## Pipeline, verbatim

> In the first stage, layout analysis (LA) returns region types and bounding boxes. In the second,
> OXR recognizes content in each cropped region while also predicting reading order (RO) from the
> full-page image and the region IDs, types, and boundaries. Reading order is returned as a sequence
> of IDs. The two results are then merged by ID into the complete output.

> For speed, layout analysis can be handled by a dedicated object detection model.

## Relationship to SoMark, verbatim

> SoMark is a production-grade document parsing engine for AI agents from the same team, powered by a
> separate model. It supports more file formats, recognizes document elements at a finer level of
> detail, and processes documents faster. It also offers additional post-processing features and
> custom development and model fine-tuning services.

OXR is therefore the open-weights sibling of a closed product, not the product itself.

## Free API, verbatim

> Try OXR on your own documents through the free API. No signup or API key required. Requests are
> queued; service stability is not guaranteed, and production use is not recommended. The service
> will remain free for as long as our servers can support it.

## Summary

This is the canonical first-party statement of **what the model is** (1.3B claim, SigLIP2-SO400M
NaViT vision encoder, visual-vocabulary head, Qwen3-0.6B-Base text backbone, pluggable layout
stage), **how it was trained** (RSI with a general VLM as judge and generator, no manual labels), and
**what the vendor admits about the numbers** (peer scores not re-run, accuracy and speed measured
separately, benchmark pages excluded from training).

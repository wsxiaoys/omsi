## Architecture difference (verbatim)

> sync-3 is a fundamentally different architecture. Instead of processing video in small independent
> chunks, sync-3 builds a global understanding of the person across the entire shot and generates all
> frames at once.

The page lists long-range consistency, built-in obstruction detection, extreme-angle support, 4K native
output with built-in super resolution, and emotion/style preservation as the resulting differences.

## Caveats (verbatim)

> lipsync-2 and lipsync-2-pro require natural speaking motion in the input video to function properly.
> If your video contains segments with still frames ... lipsync will not work during those portions,
> even if audio is present.

> sync-3 can open silent lips to match audio, though results are generic rather than speaker-style
> matched.

This speed table conflicts with the generation-times page, which puts sync-3 at ~10–15 minutes for a
30-second video versus ~1–3 minutes for lipsync-2. The comparison page's "Fast (4/5)" rating is not a
measured latency and should not be read as one.

## Summary

The model-comparison page confirms that sync-3 is a distinct architecture, not a tier of lipsync-2. It
also supplies the only first-party relative speed ratings, and those ratings contradict the
generation-times table. It repeats the 4K native output claim.

## Gaps

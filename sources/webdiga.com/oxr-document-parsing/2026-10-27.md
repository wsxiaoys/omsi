## Launch date, verbatim

> SoMark released OXR-1.0 on October 20, 2026 (SoMark launch post): a 1.3B-parameter model that turns
> PDFs and scans into Markdown or JSON, with weights you can download for free.

> The code and model repos were created in early October, but SoMark's public launch, with links to
> the code and weights, came on October 20, four days before this article.

The linked "SoMark launch post" is `https://x.com/somarkai/status/2112574994152636502`.

## Parameter-count discrepancy, verbatim

> **Files:** a 3.07 GB BF16 `model.safetensors` plus a 79 MB layout model, about 3.2 GB in total.
> SoMark calls OXR a 1.3B model; Hugging Face's metadata counts about 1.53B parameters in the
> weights file.

This is an independent check of the same discrepancy visible in the repository metadata
([weights metadata](../../huggingface.co/somark-oxr-1-0-weights-metadata/2026-10-27.md)).

## On the benchmark numbers, verbatim

> SoMark reports first place on all three benchmarks in its own comparison tables (accuracy snapshot
> dated October 5): 87.84 on olmOCR-bench, 97.18 on OmniDocBench (its table lists Gemini 3 Pro at
> 92.91) and 87.76 on DocParsingBench.

> These are SoMark's own numbers. SoMark says its olmOCR-bench and OmniDocBench peer scores come from
> published papers, model cards and leaderboards, and we haven't seen independent reproductions yet.

## On the 24 GB floor, verbatim

> Why does a 3 GB model want 24 GB? Our reading of the setup, not a SoMark statement: OXR runs on
> vLLM, which reserves a fixed share of GPU memory up front for its cache. The Docker image sets
> `VLLM_GPU_MEMORY_UTILIZATION=0.70` and also runs the layout model on the same card.

> SoMark doesn't document smaller cards, and we haven't tested one, so treat 24 GB as the floor.

## On the licence, verbatim

> The "OXR Open Source License" is Apache 2.0 plus extra terms that win in any conflict:

- "**Funding and revenue thresholds:** commercial use without a separate license is allowed only
  while you and your affiliates, combined, exceed neither USD 2 million in total funding (equity
  plus debt) nor USD 2 million in annual revenue."
- "**Attribution:** if you offer an online service to third parties using OXR, you must prominently
  identify SoMark OXR in its interface or public documentation."
- "**No competing hosted API:** at any company size, and unless SoMark authorizes it in writing, you
  can't use OXR to offer third parties a hosted API whose main job is document parsing, OCR, layout
  analysis or content extraction as a substitute for SoMark's own hosted API. That includes free,
  resold and white-label services."
- "**Automatic termination:** using OXR commercially above the thresholds without a license, skipping
  the attribution, or running a competing API ends your rights automatically, without notice from
  SoMark."

> For personal use and small teams parsing their own documents, that's workable. For a funded
> startup building a product, read it with a lawyer first. This is our summary, not legal advice.

The summary matches the licence text clause for clause
([licence](../../github.com/somark-oxr-license/2026-10-27.md)), which is a useful independent
reading check.

## On runtime support, verbatim

> There's no llama.cpp, Ollama or LM Studio support: we found no OXR issues or models in those
> projects, and even vLLM needs SoMark's out-of-tree plugin. For Macs, there's an unofficial
> community MLX conversion.

> OXR support was merged into mlx-vlm on October 21 (mlx-vlm PR #2560), after the latest 0.7.10
> release. That route recognizes single images with task prompts. It isn't SoMark's full
> layout-plus-reading-order pipeline.

Note the tension with the MLX port's own card, which reports running the **full** OXR pipeline with
an MLX backend against olmOCR-bench
([MLX port](../../huggingface.co/oxr-1-0-mlx-8bit/2026-10-27.md)). Webdiga's statement describes the
merged `mlx-vlm` path, not the port's custom pipeline wiring.

## On privacy, verbatim

> One warning: the free hosted API and online demo send your files to SoMark's servers. They're
> limited to 10 MB and 20 pages per request, and SoMark doesn't recommend them for production.

## Summary

The most careful independent write-up found at capture: it dates the launch, catches the 1.53B
parameter count, explains the 24 GB VRAM floor from the Docker configuration, summarises the licence
accurately, and correctly labels the benchmarks as vendor-reported with no independent reproduction
seen. It is a **secondary source** — it adds no measurement of its own — but it is independent of
the vendor and its claims are checkable against the primary documents.

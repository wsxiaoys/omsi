# GLM-5.3 launch post — "Scaling post-training is all we did for GLM-5.3"

- **Origin:** "GLM-5.3", Z.ai blog, <https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.3>. Retrieved 2026-10-26.
- **Evidence kind:** official documentation (first-party launch post).
- **Publication date:** none stated in the page or its bundle. Internal evidence puts it in
  **August 2026**: the coding-plan promotion it advertises runs "through August 31", and the
  [release notes](../new-released/2026-10-26.md) date the GLM-5.3 entry 2026-08-18.
- **Capture date:** 2026-10-26
- **Relevant models:** GLM-5.3 (also discusses GLM-5.2 as the base and the comparison models
  Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V4 Pro-0813, Qwen3.8-Max, Opus 4.8, Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Sol).
- **Capture type:** the page is a client-rendered single-page app; the HTML shell is empty.
  Text was extracted from the page's own JavaScript bundle
  (`/blog/assets/glm-5.3-e3CGK9Mr.js`, 30 KB) and is quoted verbatim from there. Figures
  (charts) were not captured — only their captions and the prose that cites them.

## The central identity claim, verbatim

> "Scaling post-training is all we did for GLM-5.3."

> "Today we are releasing GLM-5.3. It uses the same base model as GLM-5.2 — every gain comes
> from post-training."

That is the single most important statement on the page: GLM-5.3 is a **post-training-only
refresh of the GLM-5.2 base**, not a new pretrained model.

## Coding, verbatim

> "GLM-5.3 is the most capable open-weights model for coding, with a 50% improvement over
> GLM-5.2 on our in-house Z.ai Code Bench. It also achieve open-source SOTA on public
> benchmarks including Terminal Bench 3.0 and Agents' Last Exam."

> "GLM-5.3 improves from 4.6 to 28.3 on Terminal-Bench 3.0, from 46.2 to 66.9 on DeepSWE
> v1.1, and from 23.8 to 28.5 on Agents' Last Exam."

Token-efficiency claim, on the in-house benchmark:

> "At Max effort, GLM-5.3 reaches 34.5% at roughly 75K output tokens per task, compared with
> 23.4% at 96K for GLM-5.2. … At High effort, GLM-5.3 reaches 31.4% at around 50K output
> tokens, surpassing Claude Opus 4.8 at 29.5% with 120K. GLM-5.3 remains behind Claude Fable
> 5, which reaches 39.5% at Max effort."

Z.ai Code Bench is private: "As a private benchmark, Z.ai Code Bench also reduces the risk of
contamination from public test sets". Its scores cannot be checked externally.

## Cyber capability, verbatim

> "GLM-5.3 did not simply become better at identifying isolated flaws: it began to reason
> across multiple stages of exploitation, forming coherent plans for complete exploitation
> chains."

> "On CyberGym … GLM-5.3 scores 84.5%, up from GLM-5.2's 77.2% — the best result on the
> benchmark, ahead of Mythos 5 (83.8%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (83.6%). On ExploitBench … GLM-5.3
> reaches 54.4%, more than doubling GLM-5.2's 24.4%, while Mythos 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol score
> 78.0% and 76.5%."

> "On ExploitGym … GLM-5.3 completes 105 tasks within two hours and 130 within six hours,
> compared with 29 and 39 for GLM-5.2 … Mythos 5 remains well ahead at 181 and 247 tasks."

Real-world disclosure claim:

> "the model identified 2,436 vulnerabilities across 269 projects, including 1,097
> medium-to-high severity issues … Many had remained unnoticed for years or even decades,
> with the oldest dating back roughly 40 years."

A chart caption on the same page disagrees on that last figure: "Findings span 45 years of
impact - the oldest flaw was introduced in 1981, and on average a vulnerability lived 26.6
years before discovery." Prose says ~40 years, chart says 45 (1981→2026). Recorded as a
vendor-internal inconsistency, not resolved.

Z.ai also says it "built the Z.ai Security Disclosure Ledger to maintain a public record of
the findings". The ledger itself was not captured.

## Weights, verbatim

> "The model weights of GLM-5.3 will be publicly available soon in two weeks."

So at publication the weights were **announced, not yet published**. The Hugging Face
repository `zai-org/GLM-5.3` carries a `createdAt` of **2026-08-25**, about a week after the
release-notes date — see the [model card record](../../huggingface.co/glm-5-3-model-card/2026-10-26.md).

## API changes, verbatim

The post repeats the documentation's parameter table (`thinking.type` fixed to `enabled`;
`reasoning_effort` `low`/`high`/`max`, default `max`) and the migration notice: requests that
send `thinking.type: "disabled"` "will fail".

## Training stack, verbatim

> "All of this runs on slime, our open-source post-training framework for RL scaling, with
> Megatron on the training side and SGLang on the rollout side."

> "for long-horizon coding RL tasks, these system-level optimizations improved end-to-end RL
> training throughput by more than 2.3×"

## Coding-plan promotion, verbatim

> "98%+ cache hit rate — repeated context billed at the lower cached rate, ~30% more
> effective tokens; 1.5x limited-time quota boost — stack it with the cache savings for up to
> 180% your standard quota through August 31."

## Summary

The post establishes the identity of GLM-5.3 as a post-training-only update over the GLM-5.2
base, the vendor's coding and cyber claims with their harnesses, the delayed weights release,
and the mandatory-reasoning API break. It also publishes the vendor benchmark table that the
[Hugging Face model card](../../huggingface.co/glm-5-3-model-card/2026-10-26.md) carries in
full.
